# 04 Clustering and Stress Testing

Scope: How Phase 2 clusters the ideas that resonated into 2 to 3 distinct directions and stress-tests each on user value, feasibility, and differentiation before anything is committed.

Grounding spine: yubi-OS/yubiOS skills/idea-refine/SKILL.md (source doc).

## Cluster first, into meaningfully different directions

Phase 2 begins only after the user reacts to Phase 1 output. The skill then clusters "the ideas that resonated into 2 to 3 distinct directions," with the explicit quality bar that "each direction should feel meaningfully different, not just variations on a theme." Three flavors of the same idea is not three directions; it is one direction with bad labels.

The count is a forcing function. With 2 to 3 clusters, the stress test can be run honestly on each. With 10 clusters, evaluation degenerates into scoring theater.

## The three stress-test criteria

Each direction is tested against exactly three criteria:

- **User value:** Who benefits and how much? Is this a painkiller or a vitamin?
- **Feasibility:** What's the technical and resource cost? What's the hardest part?
- **Differentiation:** What makes this genuinely different? Would someone switch from their current solution?

The painkiller-versus-vitamin question is the user-value test in its sharpest form: it asks whether the idea removes pain people already feel or merely adds niceness. The differentiation question is a switching test: it presumes the user already has some solution and asks what would make them move.

The skill points to a companion rubric for this phase: "Read refinement-criteria.md in this skill directory for the full evaluation rubric."

## How the criteria map to wider evaluation practice

Structured idea evaluation in industry uses the same axes. Common scoring models quantify reach, impact, confidence, and effort (the RICE model gives "a balanced view of potential value," especially "for product or digital innovation because it quantifies how many people will benefit, how much they'll benefit, how confident you are, and how hard it will be to execute") (https://ideawake.com/idea-evaluation-process-and-criteria/, jev weight 0.30, weak backing). Workshop methods rate business value and feasibility on ordinal scales and warn about a failure mode the skill also guards against: "social conformity pressure prevents genuine evaluation and reinforces groupthink rather than revealing independent judgment" (https://www.ideaclouds.net/resources/workshop-methods/idea-evaluation-methods/, jev weight 0.29, weak backing). The skill's one-on-one dialogue format sidesteps groupthink by design, but its three criteria are the same value-feasibility-differentiation triangle in conversational form.

On differentiation specifically, innovation-management practice treats it as the criterion that protects the idea: "Clear differentiation builds a lasting competitive advantage and protects innovation efforts from imitation" (https://www.itonics-innovation.com/blog/idea-development, jev weight 0.28, weak backing). An idea that scores well on value and feasibility but fails the switching test usually loses to the incumbent's inertia.

## Stress testing connects to concept testing

The stress test in the skill is a structured argument, not a user study. But it is positioned as the precursor to validation: the one-pager's MVP scope exists precisely "to test the core assumption." Concept-testing practice makes the same sequencing point: "Every test starts with a specific question. Pinpoint the single most critical assumption that needs to be true for your idea to succeed" (https://www.figma.com/resource-library/concept-testing-examples/, jev weight 0.43, weak backing). The skill's contribution is making sure that by the time a concept is tested in the world, the three-criteria stress test has already killed the directions that could not survive their own arithmetic.
