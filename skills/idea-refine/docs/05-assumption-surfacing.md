# 05 Surfacing Hidden Assumptions

Scope: Phase 2's assumption-surfacing step, the three things to name for every direction, and the premortem and assumption-mapping practices the step operationalizes.

Grounding spine: yubi-OS/yubiOS skills/idea-refine/SKILL.md (source doc).

## The step most ideation skips

For each clustered direction, the skill demands three explicit lists:

- **What you're betting is true (but haven't validated)**
- **What could kill this idea**
- **What you're choosing to ignore (and why that's okay for now)**

The source doc's emphasis is unusual for an ideation method: "This is where most ideation fails. Don't skip it." The same sentence reappears in the skill's guidelines, and the anti-pattern list reinforces it: "Don't produce a plan without surfacing assumptions. Untested assumptions are the #1 killer of good ideas." The third list (deliberate omissions) is the least standard: it forces an explicit record of what the direction is ignoring, so the omission is a decision rather than an oversight.

## The premortem: a formal version of "what could kill this"

The second list is a premortem in miniature. The technique is documented in Harvard Business Review: "In a premortem, team members assume that the project they are planning has just failed, as so many do, and then generate plausible reasons for its demise. Those with reservations may speak freely at the outset, so that the project can be improved rather than autopsied" (https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem, jev weight 0.67). The method comes out of Gary Klein's research program on naturalistic decision making, which studies how experts actually decide under uncertainty rather than how laboratory models say they should (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_A._Klein, jev weight 0.60).

The skill's version is lighter than a full premortem exercise but keeps the two operative moves: assume the direction has already died, and let reservations surface before commitment rather than after.

## Assumption mapping: identify, then test

The first list (unvalidated bets) maps onto assumption mapping, the practice of making a product's implicit assumptions visible so they can be tested cheaply. Practitioner guides describe the payoff directly: "By assessing assumptions early on, you can avoid costly mistakes that derail your development process, or even lead to product failure" (https://maze.co/blog/assumption-mapping/, jev weight 0.49, weak backing) and "Assumption mapping enables you to test and validate your assumptions, which helps you de-risk your products more effectively" (https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/assumption-mapping-guide-examples/, jev weight 0.41, weak backing).

The skill's one-pager turns this into a standing artifact: the "Key Assumptions to Validate" section requires each assumption to be written with a test strategy attached ("[Assumption 1, how to test it]"). An assumption without a test path is not recorded as knowledge, it is recorded as a to-do.

## Continuous validation, not one-shot

Assumption testing is not a single gate. Teresa Torres's continuous discovery practice describes "a weekly practice of talking to customers, mapping opportunities, and testing assumptions that keeps product teams grounded in real user needs rather than internal opinions" (https://unseenfounder.com/book/continuous-discovery-habits/, jev weight 0.42, weak backing). The skill's verification checklist captures the same spirit at session scope: "Hidden assumptions are explicitly listed with validation strategies," and the user must confirm the final direction "before any implementation work."

## Why this step earns its emphasis

The three lists do different jobs. The bets list protects against confident building on unverified ground. The kill list protects against survivorship thinking (a direction that cannot name its own killers has not been examined). The omissions list protects against scope creep later, because the boundary was chosen on purpose and written down. Together they convert Phase 2 from "pick the direction we liked" into a recorded, falsifiable commitment, which is what makes the Phase 3 one-pager actionable rather than inspirational.
