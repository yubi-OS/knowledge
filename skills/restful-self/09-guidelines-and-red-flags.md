# 09 - Guidelines and red flags

Scope: the 8 operating guidelines and the 8-item red-flag checklist, and how the red flags mechanically map onto the anti-patterns (source doc).

## The guidelines (source doc)

The source doc gives 8 guidelines:

1. Bounded by time and output, not by feeling. The protocol has 4 steps; the exit criteria are explicit (source doc).
2. Reading is the rest, not the lead-up to rest. One read, no tool calls (source doc).
3. Observing the shape is allowed. Naming the gaps is not (source doc).
4. Sitting is letting the read settle, not reviewing shipping output (source doc).
5. Writing is rest only when writing is the rest. If writing produces more to do, stop (source doc).
6. The protocol has 4 steps. If a fifth step appears, you are shipping (source doc).
7. Exit criteria are explicit. If you cannot cite one, you are not exiting (source doc).
8. Restful-self is not journaling (per SELF.md bias #9). Do not write "today I felt..." (source doc).

The doc also closes with 3 negative definitions that compress anti-patterns into slogans: restful-self is not gap-finding (per anti-pattern #2, do not enumerate what was missing); restful-self is not performance of rest (per SELF.md bias #7, if you are naming the rest, you are not resting) (source doc).

Guidelines 1 and 7 are the boundary pair: they state that both entry and exit are artifact-checkable, which is the design spine explicated in docs 02 and 05. Guidelines 2 through 5 are per-step operating rules for the protocol of doc 03. Guideline 6 is the step-count invariant. Guideline 8 is the register rule that keeps whole-self outputs from degrading into diary entries (source doc).

## The red flags (source doc)

The source doc states: if any of these are present, you are NOT in restful-self mode, stop and re-enter working-self (source doc):

1. Producing a tool call during the protocol (the protocol is read-only) (source doc).
2. Writing "I am sitting with X" or "let me reflect on Y", anti-pattern #1 (source doc).
3. Reviewing shipping output, anti-pattern #2 (source doc).
4. Reading more than one file, anti-pattern #3 (source doc).
5. Running the 12-axis sweep, anti-pattern #4 (source doc).
6. The protocol runs past 4 steps, or runs without an explicit exit, anti-patterns #5 and #6 (source doc).
7. Writing a whole-self output AND scheduling a follow-up task, anti-pattern #6 (source doc).
8. "Today I felt..." or "I wonder if...", journaling, per SELF.md bias #9 (source doc).

## The mechanical mapping

The red flags are not a second taxonomy; they are the anti-patterns compiled into runtime checks. Red flags 2, 3, 4, 5 are anti-patterns 1, 2, 3, 4 verbatim. Red flag 6 merges anti-patterns 5 and 6 into one observable: step count or exit citation. Red flag 7 is the compound test for anti-pattern 6: write plus follow-up task. Red flags 1 and 8 are the operational and register forms: red flag 1 makes the protocol's read-only property checkable (any tool call disqualifies), red flag 8 gives journaling a concrete string pattern to grep for (source doc).

This compilability is deliberate and matters for enforcement. Because every red flag is a property of the transcript or the session's action log, a reviewing session (or the operator) can audit a claimed restful-self run without trusting its self-narration, which is the recognition design of doc 06 applied at the enforcement layer (source doc).

## Using the two lists together

The operating sequence for a session that suspects it is drifting: check red flags first (cheap, mechanical, disjunctive: any hit means stop and re-enter working-self), then confirm against the guidelines (each guideline is the positive form of what a red flag or anti-pattern violates). The source doc's ordering supports this: the guidelines section ends with the 3 negative definitions, and the red flags section opens with the stop instruction "stop and re-enter working-self" (source doc).

## What is not on either list

Notably absent: any quality standard for the read target, any minimum duration, any scoring of the shape sentence, and any requirement that the session feel rested. The lists enforce structure and production discipline only. This is consistent with the skill's stated bounding principle (bounded by time and output, not by feeling) and with the exit criteria being event-based rather than state-based (source doc; docs 03 and 05).
