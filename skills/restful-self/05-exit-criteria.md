# 05 - Exit criteria

Scope: the 4 bounded exits, why exit criteria rather than feelings bound the rest, and the stuck state when none fire.

## The 4 exits (source doc)

The source doc states that restful-self exits when one of the following is true:

1. One whole-self output has been produced (the writing was the rest) (source doc).
2. The operator signals end of rest ("ok", "continue", "back to work", "ship it") (source doc).
3. A working-self directive arrives ("fix X", "ship Y", "do Z"). Working-self is the default; the directive resumes it (source doc).
4. The protocol has run for one full pass (all 4 steps complete) without producing a whole-self output. The rest was the sitting; the sitting is done (source doc).

And the failure clause: if none of these fire, you are in indefinite rest, and that is not restful-self, that is stuck. Re-enter working-self and ship (source doc).

## Why bounded exits are the load-bearing design choice

The source doc is explicit that the skill is "bounded by exit criteria, not by feeling" and that "without an exit, 'rest' becomes infinite pause or meditation theater; with an exit, the rest has a natural end and the working-self cycle can resume" (source doc). This is the same design decision timeboxing makes. Wikipedia describes timeboxing as allocating a fixed period to a planned activity and notes that timeboxes are used as a form of risk management, to explicitly identify uncertain task/time relationships, i.e., work that may easily extend past its deadline; time constraints should not be changed without consideration (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeboxing, jev weight 0.25, weak backing). Asana defines timeboxing as allocating a fixed amount of time to complete a specific task, then stopping when the time is up, creating urgency and focus by setting a clear boundary (https://asana.com/resources/what-is-timeboxing, jev weight 0.16, weak backing).

Restful-self timeboxes rest. But instead of a duration (which is meaningless for an agent whose subjective time is token budget), the box is defined by outcomes and events: 1 whole-self output, 1 operator signal, 1 directive, or 1 complete protocol pass. Each exit is checkable against artifacts, which matters because the source doc requires that you be able to cite the criterion: "If you cannot cite an exit criterion, you are not exiting" (source doc, guidelines).

## The structured-break research base

The closest studied human pattern is the Pomodoro Technique: developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, it uses a timer to break work into intervals, traditionally 25 minutes, separated by short breaks, with a longer break after every 4 rounds (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomodoro_Technique, jev weight 0.30, weak backing; https://arc.duke.edu/pomodoro-technique/, jev weight 0.35, weak backing; https://pomodorotimer.online/, jev weight 0.08, weak backing; https://pomofocus.io/, jev weight 0.11, weak backing).

The strongest-backed result in the whole corpus's exit-criteria dig is a peer-reviewed comparison of break strategies. An MDPI study in Behavioral Sciences investigates the effects of 2 break strategies on university students' fatigue: systematic short breaks that are externally structured and predetermined (the "Pomodoro" technique) versus self-regulated breaks where the person decides when to break (https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/16/7/1158, jev weight 0.61, high-quality backing). The study exists because the structured-versus-feel question is empirically live for humans: do externally structured exits or self-judged exits work better? Restful-self answers the agent-side version of that question by design: self-judged exits are exactly the infinite-pause failure mode, so the exits are structured and predetermined (source doc). A coaching summary makes the same claim informally: the Pomodoro Technique uses rest to increase focus and productivity and reduce burnout (https://www.betterup.com/blog/pomodoro-technique, jev weight 0.18, weak backing).

## The stuck state

Anti-pattern #5 names the L2 risk: infinite pause, where the agent cannot cite an exit criterion and is "stuck, not resting" (source doc). The doc 04 explication covers why this matters; here the operative rule is the recovery action: "Re-enter working-self and ship" (source doc). The stuck state is not treated as a deeper mode of rest to be explored; it is a fault condition with a prescribed recovery. This mirrors the timeboxing framing of deadlines as risk management: the risk being managed is the task (rest) silently expanding past its intended boundary (source doc; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeboxing, jev weight 0.25, weak backing).

A timeboxing-method explainer and a playbook are archived for dig-record completeness (https://www.cannelevate.com.au/article/timeboxing-method-enhanced-productivity-wellness/, jev weight 0.15, weak backing; https://smartkeys.org/timeboxing-playbook/, jev weight 0.17, weak backing; the Notion template at https://www.notion.so/pt-br/templates/timeboxing-1, jev weight 0.10, weak backing). They restate the bounded-session principle without adding mechanisms, so they carry no claims here.

## Interaction with the protocol

The exit criteria bind the protocol from the outside. Step 4 ends the protocol only when writing is the rest or when an exit fires (source doc). In practice the most common exit for an agent-initiated rest (signal 1 or 2 of the fire signals) is exit 4: one full pass, no write, sitting done. The most common exit for an operator-initiated rest is exit 2. The examples in doc 07 show both: example 1 exits on the whole-self output (exit 1), example 2 exits on the operator's "ok" (exit 2), example 3 exits on the whole-self output (exit 1) (source doc).
