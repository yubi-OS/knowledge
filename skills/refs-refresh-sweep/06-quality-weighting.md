# 06 - Phase 4: jev Collection-Quality Weighting

Scope: the noul weighting pass that scores every collected dig result for source quality as it lands, the batching shape, and the measured separation that makes the metric trustworthy.

Grounding spine: `yubi-OS/yubiOS skills/refs-refresh-sweep/SKILL.md` (source doc), plus source-evaluation and LLM relevance-judgment references via dig.

## The question shape

Every result is weighted with a noul question: "Is result '<title>' (<url>) a high-quality authoritative source worth citing?", with true = primary source (upstream project, official docs, release notes, kernel/git, standards body) and false = aggregator, forum, marketing, dead link, or off-topic (source doc). Weight = the noul probability. Results at 0.5 or above count as authoritative backing; below 0.5 is weak backing and must be labeled as such wherever the weight appears in prose.

## Batch as results land

Weighting happens in batches of 5 results per request, and it happens as results land rather than at the end, matching the incremental-persistence discipline that runs through the whole skill (source doc). The source doc calls Phase 4 the place "where jev visibly earns its seat": in the validating run, weights separated upstream releases (0.90 to 0.96) from aggregators (0.01 to 0.16) cleanly. Phase 4 weighted 144 results in 31 requests at roughly $0.006 (source doc, Examples).

## Dig grounding: source-quality evaluation is an old discipline

The noul criteria are a mechanical version of classical source evaluation. Harvard's source-evaluation guide instructs a writer to determine the qualifications of the author, the purpose and context in which the source was created, and the scope of the source before relying on it (https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/evaluating-sources-0, weight 0.62; companion page at https://usingsources.fas.harvard.edu/resource/evaluating-sources, weight 0.59). The noul question compresses exactly those axes into a binary with a probability: authority (primary vs aggregator), purpose (documentation vs marketing), and scope (on-topic vs off).

## Dig grounding: LLM relevance judgment at scale

The idea of using an LLM to score result quality is now a production feature: OpenSearch 3.5 introduced an LLM as a Judge feature for search relevance evaluation that uses large language models to automatically assess search result quality at scale, blog post dated June 11, 2026 (https://opensearch.org/blog/introducing-llm-as-a-judge-scaling-search-relevance-evaluation-with-ai/, weight 0.62). Academic work provides the methodology layer: a 2025 ACM paper on benchmarking LLM-based relevance judgment methods offers a practical methodology for evaluating diverse LLM-based relevance assessment methods and released relevance judgments generated by both an open-source (Llama3.2b) and a commercial (gpt-4o) model (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3726302.3730305, weight 0.53, at the weak threshold). A Nature pipeline paper on automated citation-quality scoring surfaced in the dig but carries weak weight (0.35).

## Limits the metric does not fix

Two honest caveats, one from the dig and one from this mint's own data:

1. Authority is not relevance. During this corpus's mint, a high-authority but off-topic hit (a financial regulator's homepage) received a 0.88 noul weight. The question asks about source quality, so query design still carries the topical-relevance burden; the weighting only filters junk, aggregators, and marketing.
2. Reliability needs the rubric. The survey literature treats LLM-judge reliability as an open challenge requiring careful design (doc 04); the skill's mitigation is the explicit true/false criteria on every question plus the human-readable archive of every raw answer (doc 07).

## The archive contract

Phase 5's `archive.json` stores every result with its weight and the FULL raw decision record: type, instructions, the raw answer object including probabilities and legend, model, usage tokens, and requested_at (source doc; schema in doc 07). `redo_of` carries the index of a superseded unweighted entry when a result was rescored, so rescoring is auditable rather than destructive.

## Numbers from this corpus's own mint

This corpus's 84 dig results were weighted in 7 batched requests (12 results per batch): 30 results at 0.5 or above, 54 below, range 0.01 to 0.97. Official documentation (SearXNG docs 0.89 to 0.91, GitHub docs 0.92 to 0.97, Cloudflare docs 0.89) separated cleanly from community forum posts and marketing pages (0.07 to 0.19), reproducing the validating run's separation pattern.
