# 03 The Five Lenses

Grounding spine: yubi-OS/yubiOS `skills/ideate-solo/SKILL.md` (source doc).

## Scope

The five autonomous lenses: inversion, constraint removal, audience shift, combination, and simplification. Each is applied independently; lenses are never blended inside one variation.

## The lenses as the source doc defines them

- **Inversion.** What is the opposite of this idea? What would the world look like if we did the inverse? The lens must surface the structural assumption the inversion breaks.
- **Constraint removal.** If budget, time, technology, or organizational buy-in were not factors, what would this become? The source doc notes the constraint-removal variation is often the "true" idea underneath the scope-limited one.
- **Audience shift.** What if this were for a different user? Pick the most opposite plausible audience (power user vs novice, individual vs enterprise, expert vs consumer). The variation reveals which user assumptions are load-bearing.
- **Combination.** What adjacent idea could this merge with? List 3 to 5 adjacent ideas, pick the one creating the strongest combined value. The lens surfaces the idea's category boundary.
- **Simplification.** What is the version 10x simpler? Strip features until only the core remains. The simplification variation is often the MVP.

Each lens output is a named variation with a one-sentence description and its lens recorded, per the source doc.

## External grounding for the lens mechanisms

**Inversion** maps to the reverse/other-way-around move in systematic invention catalogs. TRIZ, the Theory of Inventive Problem Solving, was derived by Altshuller from analysis of thousands of patents and encodes recurring inventive moves as 40 principles, usable independent of industry (TRIZ official site, https://triz.org/principles/, jev weight 0.57, strong backing; Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRIZ, weight 0.34, weak backing; triz40 principle list, https://www.triz40.com/aff_Principles_TRIZ.php, weight 0.23, weak backing). Inversion as a creative prompt ("what if the opposite were true?") is the same family of move.

**Constraint removal** maps to assumption-busting and What-If facilitation techniques. Assumption busting works by deliberately seeking out and addressing previously-unquestioned assumptions that blind us to possibilities (CreatingMinds, http://creatingminds.org/tools/assumption_busting.htm, weight 0.30, weak backing). The IxDF describes "challenge assumptions" as an ideation method that helps teams break free from conventional thinking and push past false beliefs by identifying and testing them (Interaction Design Foundation, https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/challenge-assumptions, weight 0.50, weak-to-moderate backing). The "What If?" technique steps away from current limitations and imagined alternative realities to unlock bolder options (Julia Vastrik, https://juliavastrik.com/facilitation/what-if-brainstorming-technique/, weight 0.25, weak backing). Ideate-solo's constraint-removal lens is this move run by a single agent with no facilitator.

**Combination** maps to the Combine operator of SCAMPER, whose seven operators are Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, and Reverse (Designorate, https://www.designorate.com/a-guide-to-the-scamper-technique-for-creative-thinking/, weight 0.23, weak backing). The lens operationalizes Combine as a two-stage move: enumerate 3 to 5 adjacent ideas, then select the strongest merged value, which makes the category boundary explicit rather than incidental.

**Simplification** maps to the Eliminate operator of the same SCAMPER set, pushed to an extreme: strip until only the core remains, which is the MVP-shaped output the scoring stage later favors for testability.

**Audience shift** has no single named external technique in the dig set; it is a persona-inversion move. Its purpose in the source doc is diagnostic, not generative: it exists to reveal which user assumptions are load-bearing, which feeds directly into the one-pager's "Key Assumptions to Validate" section.

## The no-blending rule

Applying each lens independently is a structural guarantee of variation diversity. SCAMPER guidance similarly warns teams to avoid prematurely converging on solutions and to explore multiple possibilities before evaluation begins (IMD, https://www.imd.org/blog/innovation/scamper-method-design-thinking/, weight 0.34, weak backing). In ideate-solo the enforcement is mechanical: one variation carries exactly one lens label, and the generation log records the mapping.
