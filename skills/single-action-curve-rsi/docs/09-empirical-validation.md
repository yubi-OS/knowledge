## Cycle 1: the divergence experiment

The first cycle ran the atom end-to-end on one 34K, 6-section deep-research file with chordal proxy distance and identity Mobius. Measured numbers (source doc): 7 of 9 primitives covered; the missing ones were has_purpose and has_test. PC1+PC2 was 0.8081, passing the 0.40 gate. The point norm was 1.000000. d_pre was 0.797896. The candidate deltas diverged: flipping has_purpose would have produced delta -0.065767 (increasing the distance, so the geodesic criterion rejected it), while flipping has_test produced delta +0.086242, the geodesic winner. The single-action target was has_test, with a proposed concrete edit adding a Verification plan section, cost medium at roughly 20 lines. The cheapest edit (has_purpose, 5 lines) was not the geodesic winner. The atom validated the claim its existence rests on: the geodesic-only criterion and the cheapest-edit criterion genuinely diverge, and only one of them is guaranteed non-regressing (source doc).

## Cycles 2 to 12: the corpus sweep

A cycle runner then processed 11 further deep-research files, one cycle per file, for 12 cycles total. The measured series (source doc): the peak was cycle 2 at delta +0.3092 on the advisor-report file, target has_source, cost low. Cumulative delta across 12 cycles was +1.4108, mean +0.1176, standard deviation +0.1036. Cycles 7 and 12 returned delta exactly 0: local-minimum files where neither primitive flip reduced the geodesic distance, and the atom returned 0 rather than a negative value. Every cycle with at least 1 missing primitive produced positive delta. The atom never produced a negative delta across 12 cycles (source doc).

## Cycles 13 to 15: shifting peak and diminishing marginal value

Cycle 13/14 applied the cycle-2 winner's edit (adding a Cached evidence section, roughly 40 lines) and added a new file, then re-ran the sweep. The result validated the shifting-peak phenomenon: cycle 2's delta dropped from +0.3092 to +0.2705, and the peak moved not to the new file but to the file that was previously second-highest, cycle 8 at +0.2810 (source doc). The interpretation in the source doc: every cycle reduces the marginal value of the next single-action edit; the edit was real and good, but the system correctly measures that the next pass is smaller (source doc). Cumulative delta across 14 cycles rose to +1.8230, mean +0.1302.

Cycle 15 applied the cycle-8 winner's edit (a Verification plan section, roughly 70 lines) and found the third peak back at the advisor-report, +0.2705, tracing the oscillation C2 (+0.3092) to C8 (+0.2810) to C2 (+0.2705). Each peak run's delta decreased by 0.013 to 0.028. One notable measurement: the edited pkcs11-ecdsa file's d_pre rose from 0.5689 to 0.7393 because the new section's coverage profile was structurally different from the preceding sections, shifting the PCA basis (source doc). Mean delta fell from 0.1302 to 0.1153, and the source doc predicted a fixpoint in roughly 3 to 5 more cycles.

## Cycles 16 to 20: fixpoint

The final 5 peak-run edits brought the peak delta to +0.1872 (a 39.5% reduction across 3 peak runs), the mean delta to +0.0844 (a 28.2% reduction), and the local-minimum file count to 4 of 20 files. Per-file delta reductions ranged from -43.4% to -100% (the comparative-report file converged to delta 0). The source doc declares the RSI fixpoint reached on 4 conditions (source doc): peak delta reduction above 35% across 3 consecutive peak runs, local-minimum file fraction above 40%, cumulative delta plateaued, and the cycle-cap recommendation to declare convergence after the third peak run when all 4 conditions hold, without cycling further unless the user explicitly overrides.

Diminishing returns, the economic principle that successive increments of the same input yield smaller outputs, is the pattern the trajectory exhibits (Diminishing returns, weight 0.59, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns). Empirical studies observe the same shape in repeated measurement effort: additional investment produces progressively smaller gains (weak backing: ScienceDirect diminishing returns study, weight 0.49, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844021010926; weak backing: arXiv on measuring long-run returns to iterative improvement, weight 0.43, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09677). The fixpoint rule itself is a fixed-point iteration stopping criterion: iterate until the change per step falls below a threshold, then stop (Fixed-point iteration, weight 0.65, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-point_iteration). An ACM-indexed treatment of iterative-method convergence supplies the general framing for bounded iterative improvement (dl.acm.org iterative methods in optimization, weight 0.60, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3121360.3121374).

Three properties of the atom, all stated in the source doc's summary: 1, internally consistent, no cycle ever produced negative delta; 2, diminishing returns are predictable, per-file delta reduction is monotonic after each edit; 3, monotonically useful at corpus level, cumulative delta remains positive even with 4 local-minimum files.
