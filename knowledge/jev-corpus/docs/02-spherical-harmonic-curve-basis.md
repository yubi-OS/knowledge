# 02 Spherical Harmonic and Stereographic Geometry as a Curve Basis

Scope: the geometry the corpus math lives on: spherical harmonics as a complete basis for functions on the sphere, and the stereographic projection that connects sphere geometry to planar coordinates, which together give a corpus curve a distance metric that survives projection.

## Spherical harmonics as a complete basis

Spherical harmonics are special functions defined on the surface of a sphere, employed across many scientific fields [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonics) (weight 0.15, weak backing). The load-bearing property for analysis is completeness: "the spherical harmonics define a complete basis over the sphere. Thus, any real-valued spherical function f may be expanded as a linear combination of the basis functions" [2](https://cs.dartmouth.edu/~wjarosz/publications/dissertation/appendixB.pdf) (weight 0.72). They also form a complete, orthonormal basis for functions on the sphere, satisfying an orthogonality condition that lets an arbitrary function on the sphere be decomposed into coefficients [3](https://justinwillmert.com/articles/2020/notes-on-calculating-the-spherical-harmonics/) (weight 0.61).

The spherical analogue of the Fourier transform on the circle, spherical harmonic analysis is foundational in geophysics, climate science, computer graphics, and quantum mechanics; each basis function is characterized by a degree l and order m [4](https://metricgate.com/docs/spherical-harmonic-analysis/) (weight 0.10, weak backing). Production tooling exists for the transforms: pyshtools is an archive of Python software for spherical harmonic transforms, multitaper spectral analyses, and expansions of gridded data into Slepian basis functions [5](https://shtools.github.io/SHTOOLS/) (weight 0.83). In medical imaging, spherical harmonic coefficients describe fiber orientation distributions, where higher-order coefficients are largely not directly interpretable but are still used in spherical deconvolution [6](https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/latest/concepts/spherical_harmonics.html) (weight 0.64).

The practical consequence for a corpus engine: if a corpus's per-item primitive coverage is mapped to points on a sphere and the aggregate is fitted in a spherical-harmonic basis, the fit is not an arbitrary parameterization but a decomposition in a basis that is complete and orthonormal on that domain [2](https://cs.dartmouth.edu/~wjarosz/publications/dissertation/appendixB.pdf) (weight 0.72), [3](https://justinwillmert.com/articles/2020/notes-on-calculating-the-spherical-harmonics/) (weight 0.61).

## Stereographic projection: sphere to plane and back

A stereographic projection maps points on the sphere's surface to a plane by projecting from a pole: "a map projection obtained by projecting points P on the surface of sphere from the sphere's north pole N to point P' in a plane tangent to the south pole S" [7](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/StereographicProjection.html) (weight 0.86). With N = (0,0,1) the north pole, for any point P other than N on the unit sphere, the projection is the intersection of the line from N through P with the tangent plane [8](https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Abstract_and_Geometric_Algebra/Introduction_to_Groups_and_Geometries_(Lyons)/01:_Preliminaries/1.03:_Stereographic_projection) (weight 0.82). Because the sphere and the plane appear in many areas of mathematics, the projection appears in diverse fields [9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereographic_projection) (weight 0.16, weak backing).

Two properties make it useful for computation. First, conformality: the stereographic projection preserves the absolute value of the angle measure between arcs, which is what makes it useful in cartography [10](https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geometry/Euclidean_Plane_and_its_Relatives_(Petrunin)/16%3A_Spherical_geometry/16.04%3A_Section_4-) (weight 0.65). Second, metric compatibility: the metric induced by the inverse stereographic projection from the plane to the sphere defines a geodesic distance between points in the plane equal to the spherical distance between the spherical points they represent [11](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Stereographic_projection) (weight 0.05, weak backing). This second property is the one a corpus engine leans on: it means a gap measured in planar coordinates after projection can be interpreted as a distance on the sphere, provided the inverse map is implemented correctly.

## Why the inverse map is a bug farm

The projection is not symmetric: it is defined from a pole, and the inverse map's sign conventions (for instance whether a squared-norm term enters as (|w|^2 minus 1) or (1 minus |w|^2)) determine which hemisphere you land in. A sign error in the inverse does not throw; it silently mirrors points to the wrong hemisphere, which is exactly the class of bug parity fixtures are designed to catch. The engine's drift detector carried one such south-pole inverse-map sign error, found and corrected during the build and documented in its fixtures. (Source: the yubiOS refs source document "Jev Corpus", 2026-10-01.)

## Application in the Jev Corpus engine

The engine's atom lane (`pca2`, `stereographicLift`, `idealPole`, `geodesicGap`) reduces per-item primitive coverage to two principal components, lifts the 2D point to the sphere stereographically, defines an ideal pole, and measures the geodesic gap to it as the improvement target; the advisor lane contributes an `elCurve` stereographic embedding so evolution-loop energy curves can be fed to the same drift machinery that expects sphere points. The reparameterization step is part of the ported math and is fixed by the reference implementation rather than re-derived. (Source: the yubiOS refs source document "Jev Corpus", 2026-10-01.)
