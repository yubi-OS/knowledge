# 04 Atom Plans, Descent, and Boltzmann Collapse

Scope: how a corpus improvement target becomes a sequence of atomic steps: descent methods that move along a local model of a surface, the Boltzmann acceptance rule from simulated annealing that keeps the search from freezing, and the discipline of dry-run proposals before any change executes.

## Descent: iterative steps on a local model

The fundamental idea of gradient descent is to approximate a complicated function with tractable simpler models and take steps assuming the model is locally accurate at the current point [1](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2024/67220s24_L07_gradient_descent/L07.pdf) (weight 0.87). Descent lemmas formalize the guarantee that each step of the method decreases the objective under suitable conditions [2](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2025/67220s25_L12_gradient_descent/L12.pdf) (weight 0.87). Gradient descent itself is a method for unconstrained mathematical optimization, a first-order iterative algorithm for minimizing a differentiable multivariate function [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient_descent) (weight 0.16, weak backing). Lecture material frames it as not a single method but a general framework with many possible realizations [4](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~andoni/s17_advanced/algorithms/mainSpace/files/scribe21.pdf) (weight 0.73).

The discrete-step variant matters for a corpus engine, because a corpus's atomic edits are discrete, not differentiable. A method built on approximate discrete gradients ensures convergence for all amplitudes of the step size, contrary to classical implementations [5](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9931872) (weight 0.92). This is the theoretical license for "pick the atom that reduces the gap most, repeat": a discrete greedy rule can converge when its step rule is designed with care [5](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9931872) (weight 0.92).

## The Boltzmann rule and temperature

Simulated annealing addresses the greedy rule's classic failure: getting stuck in a local minimum. The GNU Scientific Library's implementation takes random walks through the problem space looking for low-energy points, where the probability of taking a step is determined by the Boltzmann distribution [6](https://www.gnu.org/software/gsl/doc/html/siman.html) (weight 0.62). The classic acceptance criterion comes from statistical mechanics: a system in thermal equilibrium at temperature T is found in a state with energy E with probability proportional to the Boltzmann factor [7](https://www.baeldung.com/cs/simulated-annealing) (weight 0.42, weak backing). The mechanism's components are proposal, statistical mechanics analogy, temperature, Boltzmann distribution, energy, and global minimum; the temperature parameter controls the balance between global search and local search [8](https://xuwd11.github.io/am207/wiki/simanneal.html) (weight 0.23, weak backing), [9](https://bghojogh.github.io/assets/files/ece-457a-f23/ECE457A_simulated_annealing.pdf) (weight 0.55). As the temperature is reduced, exploration decreases and exploitation increases, gradually narrowing the search [9](https://bghojogh.github.io/assets/files/ece-457a-f23/ECE457A_simulated_annealing.pdf) (weight 0.55). Simulated annealing sits inside the broader stochastic-optimization family, alongside methods like particle swarm optimization [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulated_annealing) (weight 0.15, weak backing). Modern treatments implement the Metropolis criterion and Boltzmann transition probability with exponential or logarithmic cooling schedules [11](https://yuhi-sa.github.io/en/posts/20260226_simulated_annealing/1/) (weight 0.14, weak backing).

For an engine that proposes one edit per cycle, the practical reading is: early cycles may accept larger, less certain steps; as measurements accumulate, the acceptance rule sharpens toward the locally best atom. Whether that thermostat is literal (a temperature parameter over candidate atoms) or structural (a hysteresis rule over measured deltas) is a design decision; the invariant is that the proposal step is stochastic or greedy, and execution is a separate, controlled act [6](https://www.gnu.org/software/gsl/doc/html/siman.html) (weight 0.62), [9](https://bghojogh.github.io/assets/files/ece-457a-f23/ECE457A_simulated_annealing.pdf) (weight 0.55).

## Atom plans as dry-run proposals

The separation between proposing and executing is the safety property that makes the rest of the machinery usable. In the Jev Corpus engine, `atomStep` computes the single atom (a candidate corpus change, represented as a primitive-coverage vector on the sphere) whose flip reduces the geodesic distance to the ideal pole the most; `atomDescent` iterates that rule; `boltzmannCollapse` applies the Boltzmann-style acceptance from the project's `collapse.py` reference. Every produced plan is a dry-run proposal: it becomes real only through a separately gated directive through the platform's existing fail-closed approval gate, never as a side effect of the math. The port was parity-tested with atom deltas asserted to be non-negative, throwing on violation per the reference's atom_delta_nonneg property, and fixtures were generated from the Python implementation. (Source: the yubiOS refs source document "Jev Corpus", 2026-10-01.)
