## The seeds behind the lenses

Two of the operator's seeds are visible in this dig: the 6-fold (lane 1) and the lens with aberration correction (lane 3).

### The 6-fold

Lane 1's verdict was 0.9358. The underlying object is the symmetry group of the regular hexagon. Wolfram MathWorld describes the dihedral group D_6 as the symmetries of a regular hexagon, generated by a rotation and a reflection: [Dihedral Group D_6 - from Wolfram MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/DihedralGroupD6.html) (clef weight: 0.5825). A University of Lethbridge page gives the Cayley table for the hexagon's symmetry group: [Symmetry Group of Regular Hexagon - ULethbridge](https://www.cs.uleth.ca/~holzmann/notes/dihedral/dihedral.html) (clef weight: 0.2907) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). LibreTexts introduces dihedral groups as symmetry groups arising naturally in geometry: [3.3: Dihedral Groups (Group of Symmetries) - Mathematics LibreTexts](https://math.libretexts.org/Courses/Mount_Royal_University/Abstract_Algebra_I/Chapter_3%3A_Permutation_Groups/3.3%3A_Dihedral_Groups_(Group_of_Symmetries)) (clef weight: 0.4375) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). The Wikipedia article on the dihedral group of order 6 covers the notational ambiguity between D3 and D6: [Dihedral group of order 6 - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_group_of_order_6) (clef weight: 0.1488) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5), and the hexagonal-prism article lists the prism's D6h symmetry of order 24: [Hexagonal prism - Wikipedia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/hexagonal_prism) (clef weight: 0.1739) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). The companion one-pager records the measured answer: the 6-fold ring is spectrally compressible, not hierarchically ordered.

### The lens and aberrations

Lane 3's verdict was 0.8452, the lowest of the three lanes but still high. V3 (Inversion lens) turns the instrument into one that corrects itself. Aberration theory is the vocabulary for what a correcting loop would fix. RP Photonics explains chromatic, spherical, astigmatism, coma, and field aberrations and distinguishes aberrations from diffraction limits: [Optical Aberrations - chromatic, spherical, astigmatism, coma, field ...](https://www.rp-photonics.com/optical_aberrations.html) (clef weight: 0.7229). Telescope-optics.net presents the Seidel primary aberrations as an aggregate wavefront aberration function: [3.5.1. Seidel aberrations, wavefront aberration function](https://telescope-optics.net/Seidel_aberrations.htm) (clef weight: 0.3642) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). A metrology tutorial covers basic wavefront aberration theory for optical testing: [PDF Basic Wavefront Aberration Theory for Optical Metrology](http://rohr.aiax.de/BasicAberrationsandOpticalTesting.pdf) (clef weight: 0.3941) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). An arXiv paper derives misalignment aberration patterns and contrasts the Seidel and Zernike definitions of astigmatism: [Misalignment Aberration Patterns in](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.0708) (clef weight: 0.4907) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). A page on the ophthalmic Seidel test shares only the name and is unrelated: [Seidel Test - EyeWiki](https://eyewiki.org/Seidel_Test) (clef weight: 0.0817) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5).

## Why V3 scored well but lost the MVP

V3's advance (0.7083) and testable (0.8484) are respectable, yet its MVP probability was 0.0072. The note explains it: strongest as follow-on actuation. A correcting lens needs a trusted measurement to correct against; building it first inverts the dependency.

## How the winner was assembled

The winning option combines V1 (the instrument and gate) with V6 (the calibration principle). Their individual scores were the top two on advance and both above 0.94 on testability. The MVP question confirmed the combination at 0.6859, more than three times V1 alone.
