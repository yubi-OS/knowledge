# 03. The 1-page investor brief

Scope: The 1-page investor brief: what it is for, which sections survive the compression from concept brief to one page, and the discipline of a single risk and a single ask.

## Purpose: hook, not summary

An investor one-pager is a single-page summary of a startup designed to hook attention and earn a meeting. It condenses the value proposition, market opportunity, traction, team, and funding ask into a format investors can digest in 30 to 90 seconds (https://www.fundreef.com/one-pager-for-investors-template-and-examples/, jev weight 0.36, weak backing). The design constraint that follows: every section must survive being read in under 2 minutes, which means the document is a filter, not a report. If a section does not help a stranger decide "take the meeting or not", it does not belong on the page.

This is the third compression in the artifact chain covered by this corpus: the SCAMPER mind map is the wide ideation pass, the product concept brief (doc 02) is the structured second pass, and the one-pager is the compressed external-facing pass. Each compression should lose detail, not change claims. If a number or claim changes between the concept brief and the one-pager, that is a defect, not a refinement.

## The section set

Practitioner guides converge on 7 essential sections: company headline, problem statement, solution overview, traction metrics, market opportunity, team credentials, and the ask (https://rivereditor.com/blogs/write-investor-one-pager-fundraising, jev weight 0.28, weak backing). A single-page document structure helps founders summarize pitch, traction, team, and funding ask in a format investors review quickly (https://ticnote.com/en/blog/startup-one-pager-template, jev weight 0.14, weak backing). Template vendors (Visme, Storydoc, AiPPT, SlideTeam, Pitch) package the same skeleton with different visuals, which is itself a signal that the section set is convention rather than validated craft (https://visme.co/blog/startup-one-pager/, jev weight 0.33, weak backing; https://www.storydoc.com/blog/how-to-create-a-startup-one-pager, jev weight 0.23, weak backing).

Mapped onto the concept-brief sections of doc 02, the one-pager keeps: the problem (1 or 2 sentences), the solution (the 5 or fewer headline features), the target market (one line per segment), the business model (one line), the differentiator (one sentence), the MVP (2 features maximum), success metrics (one line), one key risk, and the ask. It drops: product principles, the full feature list, the next-step prototype workflow, and any risk beyond the single most important one.

Two sections deserve special discipline:

- One key risk, not a risk register. Naming the single risk that would kill the deal (for a governance-first product, that adoption friction from added complexity) signals honesty and self-knowledge; a list of 5 hedged risks signals neither.
- One ask. The ask should be concrete (seed capital or named design partners) and tied to what it validates, not to what it builds.

## What compression tests

The one-pager is also a coherence test for the upstream artifacts. Compressing the concept brief forces choices that the longer document could blur: if the 5 features do not reduce to a single differentiator sentence, the positioning is not yet sharp; if the MVP cannot be stated as 2 features, it is not yet minimal; if the success metrics cannot be stated in one line, they are not yet observable.

For products aimed at trust-sensitive buyers, the one-pager carries an extra burden: enterprise buyers approach new vendor relationships with caution because large deployments touch sensitive data, critical operations, and long-term strategic goals, and a single weakness in governance or communication can stall negotiations (https://www.worldauthors.org/trust-accelerator-how-strategic-transparency-wins-enterprise-contracts/, jev weight 0.19, weak backing). A governance-first product's one-pager should therefore show its own governance discipline: where the numbers come from, which claims are directional, and which are proven.

## Caveats for corpus readers

Every source surfaced for this subtopic is a template vendor or content-marketing blog, and none carried an authoritative jev weight above 0.5. The 7-section skeleton is widely repeated convention, not an evidence-based finding. The one empirical anchor in the set is the 30 to 90 second digestion claim, which is itself a vendor assertion. Treat the structure as a useful convention with weak backing, and treat deviations from it as cheap experiments rather than violations.
