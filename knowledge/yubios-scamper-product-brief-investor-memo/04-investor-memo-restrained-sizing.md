# 04. Investor memo and restrained market sizing

Scope: Restrained market sizing in investor memos: the TAM proxy trap, bottom-up versus top-down sizing, SAM and SOM discipline, and why early-stage numbers should be small.

## The two sizing methods

There are two principal methods for estimating market size. The top-down approach starts with a broad market figure and narrows it down with filters; the bottom-up approach builds from known data points, such as the number of target customers multiplied by achievable revenue per customer, to estimate the overall market size (https://fieldsignalhq.com/resources/blog/market-sizing-a-practical-guide-for-investors-and-operators, jev weight 0.37, weak backing). Bottom-up sizing estimates market potential by analyzing individual customer segments, geographic regions, or product categories and aggregating the data into a comprehensive estimate (https://fourweekmba.com/bottom-up-market-sizing/, jev weight 0.36, weak backing).

The practitioner consensus favors bottom-up for early-stage claims. Bottom-up sizing provides more accurate and investor-ready insights than top-down estimates, especially for startups in niche or emerging industries (https://visible.vc/blog/bottom-up-market-sizing/, jev weight 0.23, weak backing). The bottom-up approach starts with a detailed analysis of the company's target market segments and builds up to TAM, SAM, and SOM using primary data such as customer surveys, interviews, and sales data (https://foundationinc.co/lab/tam-sam-som, jev weight 0.16, weak backing).

## The TAM proxy and its failure mode

A TAM proxy is a number borrowed from an adjacent market and presented as the addressable market for the actual product. Its failure mode is arithmetic honesty: the proxy is real (for example, a named analyst's endpoint-security forecast) but the leap from "the adjacent market is $20B" to "our SAM is $2B" is unstated multiplication by vibes. Practitioner guides catalog this as the classic mistake, and one guide proposes explicit ratio checks designed to expose a made-up SOM (https://preuve.ai/blog/market-size-calculator, jev weight 0.08, weak backing). Spectup's guide adds a verification checklist and a list of common mistakes for defending market size (https://www.spectup.com/resource-hub/tam-som-sam, jev weight 0.22, weak backing), and stage-expectation guides describe what VCs expect at each stage (https://www.icanpitch.com/blog/tam-sam-som-market-sizing-guide, jev weight 0.13, weak backing).

The restrained pattern, in contrast, keeps every layer labeled:

- TAM stated as a proxy for an adjacent market, with the adjacent market named and the number attributed to a specific analyst report with a retrieval date.
- SAM derived by an explicit filter ("organizations that explicitly care about auditability, data minimization, and revocable admin power"), stated qualitatively as a small minority of the total rather than converted to a fake-precise number.
- SOM stated as an early target (single-digit million ARR), not a capture forecast.

The restraint is the point: a memo that claims the full adjacent market as its TAM reads as naive; a memo that says "the adjacent market is $18B to $25B, our serviceable slice is a small minority of it, and our first phase targets single-digit million ARR" reads as a team that understands the difference between market existence and market capture.

## Why small early numbers are more credible

Guides on the top-down versus bottom-up decision consistently note that top-down numbers invite skepticism because every filter is an assumption (https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/market-sizing-step-by-step, jev weight 0.18, weak backing; https://www.hackingthecaseinterview.com/pages/market-sizing, jev weight 0.19, weak backing). Bottom-up chains of small, checkable numbers (how many such organizations exist, what they spend per seat, what share a first product could plausibly win) survive diligence better. Worked examples of bottom-up sizing exist to calibrate against (https://investsomemoney.com/bottom-up-market-sizing-example/, jev weight 0.17, weak backing; https://upmetrics.co/blog/tam-sam-som-market-size-metrics, jev weight 0.14, weak backing; https://hginsights.com/blog/market-sizing-the-complete-guide-to-calculating-tam-sam-som-and-building-a-data-driven-growth-strategy/, jev weight 0.17, weak backing).
