# 03 - The Hyperspherical Lift: PCA, Stereographic Projection, Mobius Reparameterization

Scope: the fit stage that turns per-file binary coverage vectors into points on a sphere: PCA to two dimensions, stereographic lift to S2, and Mobius reparameterization with identity initialization.

## Stage one: reduce to two dimensions

The reduction step is ordinary PCA. The scikit-learn reference defines principal component analysis as linear dimensionality reduction using singular value decomposition of the data to project it to a lower dimensional space, and its own examples include 2D projections (https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.decomposition.PCA.html, jev weight 0.94, high). For visualization of high-dimensional data in Python, Plotly's PCA support renders the projected components as an interactive visualization (https://plotly.com/python/pca-visualization/, jev weight 0.61, high). Weaker educational sources agree on the mechanics: PCA is a statistical technique for reducing dimensionality while preserving the maximum amount of information and enabling visualization of multidimensional data (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_component_analysis, jev weight 0.33, weak), and projecting onto the first component effectively transforms multidimensional data into fewer dimensions while retaining the important structure (https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/data-analysis/principal-component-analysis-pca/, jev weight 0.14, weak). The quality gate to carry forward from these sources: keep the top 2 components and check their explained variance before trusting the embedding.

## Stage two: the stereographic lift

Stereographic projection is the bridge from the 2D plane to the sphere. The Riemann sphere can be visualized as the complex number plane wrapped around a sphere by stereographic projection (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_sphere, jev weight 0.51, high). The correspondence is exact: stereographic projection sets up a one-to-one relationship between points of the plane and points of the sphere, with the single exception of the north pole, and it is convenient to take the sphere as the model for the complex projective line (https://www.cut-the-knot.org/arithmetic/algebra/RiemannSphere.shtml, jev weight 0.77, high). Every PCA-projected corpus point therefore lands on a well-defined point of S2, with the pole reserved for the point at infinity.

## Stage three: Mobius reparameterization

A Mobius transformation has a concrete geometric construction on this same sphere: it can be obtained by stereographic projection of the complex plane onto a sphere, followed by a rigid motion of the sphere in R3, followed by stereographic projection back to the plane (https://teaching.smp.uq.edu.au/scims/Complex_analysis/SProjection.html, jev weight 0.75, high). The class these transformations belong to is equally well defined: Mobius transformations are exactly the bijective conformal maps from the Riemann sphere to itself, the automorphisms of the sphere as a complex manifold (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6bius_transformation, jev weight 0.45, weak). A talk-page formulation adds the decomposition explicitly: a Mobius transformation of the complex plane is an inverse stereographic projection, followed by the action of an SO(3) element on the sphere, followed by stereographic projection back (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Stereographic_projection, jev weight 0.05, weak).

For the corpus fit the operational consequences are three. First, identity initialization is legitimate: the identity map is itself a Mobius transformation, so starting with no reparameterization is starting inside the family. Second, refinement is a search over sphere rigid motions (the SO(3) action) plus the complex parameters, not an arbitrary nonlinear fit, which keeps the model small. Third, conformality is the invariant to preserve: any refinement that breaks the conformal structure is no longer a Mobius transformation, so held-out cross-ratio checks are the natural validation metric during L-BFGS-B refinement.

## Why lift a corpus at all

The point of the full chain, binary vectors through PCA through the lift, is that the output geometry supports the analysis the corpus needs. Points on S2 admit a geodesic metric, equal-area partitioning, and nearest-neighbor isolation radii measured as chordal distances, none of which are natural on the raw 9-dimensional hypercube. The lift is what converts a per-file feature table into a spatial map where "two files cover similar primitive combinations" becomes "two files are close on the sphere," and where an isolated point is visually and computationally detectable as a sparse cell.
