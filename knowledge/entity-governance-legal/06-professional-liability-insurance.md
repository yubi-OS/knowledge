# 06. Professional liability and insurance considerations

Scope: errors-and-omissions (professional liability) and cyber liability insurance as the coverage categories relevant to a solo security consultancy or open-source support business, and what drives the decision to carry them.

## Professional liability (E&O) for consultants

Professional liability insurance, also called errors and omissions (E&O) insurance, protects consultants from lawsuits over work mistakes (https://www.techinsurance.com/professional-liability-insurance/professional-liability-insurance-for-consultants, jev weight 0.29, weak backing). The same category is also called professional indemnity insurance or malpractice insurance, and it protects a business if a client claims the work caused them financial harm due to a mistake or oversight (https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/errors-omissions, jev weight 0.24, weak backing).

For independent consultants specifically, E&O is treated as the most important policy to carry, and the exposure scales with the business: the premium and the recommended limits track the consultant's fee level and the size of the engagements involved (https://dareable.com/commercial-insurance/best-professional-liability-insurance-for-consultants, jev weight 0.14, weak backing). Broker coverage for consulting businesses is standard enough that online quote workflows exist for professional liability for consultants (https://www.insureon.com/consulting-business-insurance/professional-liability, jev weight 0.25, weak backing).

## Cyber liability as a separate category

Cyber liability insurance is a distinct policy from E&O, covering the costs of a data breach or malicious software attack rather than claims of professional negligence (https://cybersecop.com/cyber-liability-insurance, jev weight 0.17, weak backing). Insurance marketplaces offer cyber insurance as its own product line for consulting businesses (https://www.insureon.com/consulting-business-insurance/cyber-liability, jev weight 0.14, weak backing), including a dedicated product line for cybersecurity companies and consultants that covers allegations of negligence alongside breach response costs (https://www.insureon.com/technology-business-insurance/cybersecurity, jev weight 0.14, weak backing).

Practitioner argument from the security industry supports carrying it even for security-literate firms: an incident-response practitioner's write-up argues cyber liability insurance is needed because incident investigation work routinely uncovers compromised accounts and related financial exposure (https://www.pentestpartners.com/security-blog/cyber-liability-insurance-you-need-it-and-heres-why, jev weight 0.32, weak backing).

## What this frames for a project deciding

For a project with support/SLA and consulting offers in flight, the dig supports a specific framing, not a coverage recommendation:

1. E&O is the category that maps directly to the risk of delivering faulty security advice or faulty support under contract (https://www.techinsurance.com/professional-liability-insurance/professional-liability-insurance-for-consultants, jev weight 0.29, weak backing).
2. The coverage decision scales with fee volume and engagement size (https://dareable.com/commercial-insurance/best-professional-liability-insurance-for-consultants, jev weight 0.14, weak backing), both of which are undetermined for a pre-launch project.
3. Cyber liability is a second, separate line, most relevant once the project operates hosted services holding customer data (https://www.insureon.com/consulting-business-insurance/cyber-liability, jev weight 0.14, weak backing).

Both decisions depend on the entity type (doc 01) and on offer volume, which the source document for this corpus records as unresolved.
