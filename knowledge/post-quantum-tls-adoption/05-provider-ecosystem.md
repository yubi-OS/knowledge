# 05. The provider ecosystem beyond Cloudflare: Google, measurement surveys, concentration

Scope: non-Cloudflare provider deployments, the Google Cloud post-quantum roadmap and load-balancer capabilities, independent measurement studies, and the concentration-versus-adoption framing.

## Google Cloud

Google Cloud published an updated roadmap in August 2026 to migrate to post-quantum cryptography by 2029, stating that PQC rollout across internal and customer-facing services is underway (source: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/pqc-in-plaintext-google-clouds-post-quantum-cryptography-roadmap, weight 0.86). The same roadmap includes quantum-confidential TLS 1.3 handshakes for Google Cloud services and configured load balancers (per the source corpus, same URL). Google's security resources page carries the same 2029 commitment and frames it around NIST's PQC standards (source: https://cloud.google.com/security/resources/post-quantum-cryptography, weight 0.63, marginal backing).

Documentation for Google Cloud load balancers now covers post-quantum TLS capabilities for securing workloads against quantum computing threats, which makes load balancers the concrete TLS-terminating surface customers can configure (source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/load-balancing/docs/post-quantum-tls, weight 0.92). Third-party coverage of the roadmap notes that full readiness is targeted for 2029 with some work expected to continue into the next decade (source: https://www.securityweek.com/google-cloud-sets-out-post-quantum-roadmap-with-2029-readiness-goal/, weight 0.72). The roadmap splits responsibility: customers own client-side software updates, asymmetric key lifecycle management, and quantum-safe configuration of Google Cloud services, while Google retains its network, global front ends, encryption in transit, and the ALTS protocol (source: https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/google-cloud-pqc-roadmap/, weight 0.56, marginal backing).

## Independent measurement studies

Academic measurement work now exists at scale. One large-scale empirical evaluation covers 32,011 domains across diverse sectors, analyzing negotiated TLS parameters including protocol versions, cipher suites, key exchange mechanisms, and certificates (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16473, weight 0.74). Its headline split: 50.7% of analyzed domains remain fully vulnerable to quantum attacks through classical-only mechanisms, while 49.3% demonstrate partial post-quantum readiness through hybrid key exchange (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16473, weight 0.75). A separate longitudinal study established more than 2 billion TLS handshakes across 1 million domains from 11 globally distributed vantage points and observed configuration convergence: PQ-TLS deployment overwhelmingly centers on a small set of constructions (source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29005, weight 0.75; https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29005v1, weight 0.77).

## Concentration, not adoption

Press coverage of the top-million-domain numbers carries an explicit caveat. Security researcher Jan Schaumann, who scans the public internet for TLS configurations, saw little beyond large providers deploying post-quantum key exchange; in a March 2025 scan Cloudflare accounted for 96% of supporting sites in the top 100,000 domains, and by February 2026 a top-million scan still showed the same concentration pattern (source: https://cacm.acm.org/news/post-quantum-tls-finished-the-easy-half/, weight 0.89). The source corpus reports a 49.22% share of the top million domains framed as concentration rather than organic adoption, with the underlying primary dataset not directly retrievable in that pass.

The combined reading: headline percentages overcount independent operators because a handful of CDN and cloud terminators negotiate the hybrid on behalf of millions of dependent domains. For migration planning, what matters is not the aggregate share but whether your own TLS-terminating tier, which for most sites is exactly one of those providers or your own reverse proxy, negotiates the hybrid on both legs.
## What the provider split means for architecture choices

The provider picture splits TLS-terminating infrastructure into two archetypes. The first is a provider-terminated site, where the CDN or cloud load balancer negotiates the hybrid on the client leg and the operator's only real decision is the origin leg; Google's load-balancer documentation and Cloudflare's origin documentation are the two reference surfaces for that decision (sources: https://docs.cloud.google.com/load-balancing/docs/post-quantum-tls, weight 0.92; https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/post-quantum-cryptography/pqc-to-origin/, weight 0.95). The second is self-terminated infrastructure, where the operator owns the library choice outright, and the 2029 roadmaps of both major providers set the same external deadline.

The shared-responsibility split Google published is a useful checklist for the second archetype: customers own client-side software updates, asymmetric key lifecycle management, and quantum-safe configuration of services, while the provider retains its network, front ends, encryption in transit, and internal protocols (source: https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/google-cloud-pqc-roadmap/, weight 0.56, marginal backing). In practice that means a provider roadmap promise does not cover your origin servers, your certificate inventory, or your client fleet.

## Convergence as a fingerprint

The measurement studies agree that deployment converges on a single construction, which has a diagnostic use: because PQ-TLS deployment overwhelmingly centers on one configuration (source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29005, weight 0.75), any negotiated group other than X25519MLKEM768 on a modern stack is either a deliberate policy choice or a stale configuration. Sector-level studies add that roughly half of the analyzed domains still have no post-quantum readiness at all (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16473, weight 0.75), so the long tail remains the actual frontier even as the head, dominated by a handful of providers, appears nearly done (source: https://cacm.acm.org/news/post-quantum-tls-finished-the-easy-half/, weight 0.89).
