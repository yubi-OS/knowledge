# 08 - Market Context

Scope: the SMB AI-automation market Steady Orbit Systems operates in: what small and mid-sized businesses buy, typical service shapes, AI consultancy pricing models, and delivery risks, with Cloudflare Workers as the delivery-platform context.

Source-class note: this is the corpus's web-research doc, and the dig came back mostly weak: 10 of 12 collected results score below 0.5 on the noul metric (vendor blogs, agency-network listicles, a forum thread). Every weak-backed claim below is labeled. The strongest backing in this doc is Cloudflare's own documentation (0.84-0.95) and the company's own public terms and FAQ.

## The SMB AI-automation service shape

The services Steady Orbit sells match the pattern the dig surfaced for the segment. A 2026 roundup describes the small-business AI stack as "low-code workflow builders with AI-powered decision-making to eliminate repetitive tasks across operations, marketing, and client delivery," naming Zapier as "the fastest to deploy for simple integrations between popular SaaS tools" and Make and n8n as "the most flexible automation for teams willing to invest in setup time," with agentic platforms like Relevance AI and n8n AI Agents as the newer layer (source: https://aismartventures.com/posts/best-ai-automation-tools-for-small-business-in-2026/, weak backing, w 0.09). A payments-company page frames the adjacent spend on marketing automation for small businesses (source: https://squareup.com/marketing, weak backing, w 0.33). Steady Orbit's own service list (websites, CRM and lead capture, workflows and automation, AI agents) sits squarely in this shape, with the company's site explicitly saying it prefers "improving and connecting what already works before replacing systems unnecessarily," i.e., it integrates the same tools rather than replacing them (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13).

## AI consultancy pricing models

The pricing-model taxonomy the dig surfaced is consistent across several sources, all weak-backed: fixed project, hourly, retainer, usage-based, productized, and hybrid models, with consulting engagements leaning toward hourly or project fees and ongoing automation services toward retainers (source: https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/, weak backing, w 0.11; https://www.orientsoftware.com/blog/ai-consultant-hourly-rate/, weak backing, w 0.22). The ranges quoted vary widely by source and are presented here only as the market's self-published picture, each with its weak label: setup projects from $2,500 to $15,000+ with monthly retainers from $500 to $5,000+ and hourly consulting of $150 to $450 per hour at the high end of one guide (source: https://digitalagencynetwork.com/ai-agency-pricing/, weak backing, w 0.19); standard retainers of $5,000 to $15,000 and comprehensive engagements of $15,000 to $50,000 per month in another (source: https://bosio.digital/articles/ai-consulting-cost-guide, weak backing, w 0.24); boutique single-workflow retainers around $2,500 per month up to $15,000 per month full-service in a third (source: https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-consulting-rates-pricing, weak backing, w 0.17).

Against that self-published spread, Steady Orbit's public posture is deliberately different: "custom quotes per engagement; no published price list," with scoping before commitment and quotes as estimates unless a fixed fee is agreed in writing (sources: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16; https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/terms/, weak backing, w 0.11). Its free front door, a 3-minute diagnostic and a free 30-minute strategy call that ends in "a plan you can act on... whether you work with us or not," is a discovery-first funnel rather than a rate-card funnel (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/booking/, weak backing, w 0.17). One surfaced guide's own advice aligns with that posture: "request a discovery engagement before a build engagement" (source: https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-consulting-rates-pricing, weak backing, w 0.17).

## Cloudflare Workers as the delivery platform

The strongest-grounded context in this corpus is the platform Steady Orbit builds on. Cloudflare Workers is serverless compute on Cloudflare's global network (source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/, w 0.84). Durable Objects provide single-location strongly consistent stateful coordination, the primitive behind the worker's task queue and per-object state (source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/what-are-durable-objects/, w 0.95; https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/, w 0.94). Workers AI exposes Llama-family models through bindings, which is how the automations engine runs its 8b/70b/guard model routes without self-hosting inference (source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/configuration/bindings/, w 0.94; https://blog.cloudflare.com/meta-llama-3-available-on-cloudflare-workers-ai/, w 0.87). For a small consultancy, this stack matters commercially: one edge runtime serves the marketing site, the AI demos, the API relays, and the automation control plane, which is exactly the shape of the steady-orbit worker documented in doc 02 (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/AGENT.md, weak backing, w 0.28).

## Delivery risks in the segment

Three risk classes recur in the segment's self-published guidance, and all are weak-backed as market claims: scope ambiguity (one guide's advice: "get a written scope of work before signing any fixed-fee contract; 'AI automation project' is not a scope") (source: https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-consulting-rates-pricing, weak backing, w 0.17); retainer-versus-project mismatch ("Retainers make less sense when the underlying work is actually a finite project being repack[aged]") (source: https://bosio.digital/articles/ai-consulting-cost-guide, weak backing, w 0.24); and tool churn that makes outcome-based or hybrid pricing attractive but harder to promise (source: https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/, weak backing, w 0.11).

Steady Orbit's public answers to those risks are contractual and architectural rather than marketing claims. Contractually: written agreements define scope before work, the client owns final deliverables on full payment, and "we design systems so you're not unnecessarily locked into SOS" (sources: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/terms/, weak backing, w 0.11; https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13). Architecturally: the lead machine ships with refuse-to-claim guards and fails closed when a credential is missing rather than guessing, and every automation action passes a deterministic gate with independent verification before it counts as done (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/jev-automations-2026-10-01.md, w 0.59; internal-record claim). The honesty stance extends to sales: the booking FAQ promises to say when the bottleneck is not AI-fixable (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/booking/, weak backing, w 0.17).

## Gaps in this picture

The market-size numbers often cited for SMB AI adoption did not survive weighting in this dig: the results carrying them are listicle-grade aggregators (0.06-0.11), and this corpus declines to quote market-size figures from those sources. What stands on strong ground here is only the platform layer (Cloudflare's own docs) and the company's own published posture; the rest is labeled weak on purpose.
