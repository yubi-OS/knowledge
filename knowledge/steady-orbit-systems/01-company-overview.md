# 01 - Company Overview

Scope: Steady Orbit Systems' positioning ("AI automation for small and mid-sized businesses"), its service lines, the design-build-launch-handoff delivery model, founders, contact and pricing posture, all grounded in the company's own public surfaces.

Source-class note: per this corpus's mint directive, the company's own public surfaces (llms.txt, the marketing site, and its pages) are the primary grounding layer for company facts. The noul decision model scores vendor-owned marketing surfaces low by design, so most site-copy claims below carry weak weights (< 0.5) and are labeled as such; the founders page is the one site page the model scored above 0.5.

## Positioning

Steady Orbit Systems describes itself as "AI automation for small and mid-sized businesses," with the one-line promise: "We design the system, build it, launch it, and hand over the controls. Then it runs itself" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16). The homepage frames the pain the same way: "Stop experimenting with AI. Start making it work," followed by "We find where your business is losing customers, wasting time, or getting stuck. Then we build the right fix, from websites and workflows to automation and AI agents" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13). Its legal terms describe the entity as "a design and automation consultancy based in California" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/terms/, weak backing, w 0.11).

The positioning deliberately targets the small and mid-sized business (SMB) segment rather than enterprise buyers, and the funnel is built around a free diagnostic and a free 30-minute call rather than a published product catalog (sources: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, w 0.16; https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/booking/, weak backing, w 0.17).

## Service lines

The public services block lists four lines (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13):

1. Websites & Visibility: conversion-focused, mobile-ready websites with tracking, forms, search foundations and lead capture; SEO and Google Business Profile work for local visibility; and AEO / AI Search Visibility, described as making the business easier for AI-powered search and answer engines to understand and surface.
2. CRM & Lead Capture: bringing leads from forms, calls, booking and other sources into one pipeline; routing and automated follow-up; and tracking where leads came from and what happened next.
3. Workflows & Automation: removing copy-paste work and repeated follow-up, integrating the software the business already relies on, and building triggers, handoffs, routing and fallback rules "so work keeps progressing without someone babysitting it."
4. AI Agents & Custom Tools: an AI website agent (answer questions, capture and qualify leads, connect to booking, hand off to the team), an AI receptionist (answer calls, qualify callers, book appointments, route calls, produce call notes), and custom apps, portals, dashboards and internal tools.

The llms.txt summary compresses the same list to "websites, workflow automation, and custom AI agents," and adds two showcase surfaces: the Systems Lab ("live, interactive demos: AI receptionists for a barber shop, auto shop, and car wash") and the Revenue Blind Spot diagnostic ("a free diagnostic that finds the gaps costing a business revenue") (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16).

## Delivery model: design, build, launch, handoff

The delivery model is stated in the llms.txt tagline (design, build, launch, hand over) and elaborated in the FAQ. Asked "What happens after you build it?", the site answers: "We test it, document it, help your team use it, and make sure you understand what you own. Ongoing support and optimization are available when you need them." On timelines: "Most projects are generally completed within 30-60 days, depending on scope, integrations, and complexity. We define the expected timeline before the build starts." On lock-in: "Do I own what SOS builds for my business? Yes. You keep ownership of your accounts, data, documentation, and the work we build for you where applicable. We design systems so you're not unnecessarily locked into SOS" (all from https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13).

The booking page operationalizes the front end of this model as a free 30-minute strategy call structured in three 10-minute blocks: "Your bottleneck, mapped," "What AI can fix," and "A plan you can act on," with the promise "You leave with clear next steps for your business, whether you work with us or not" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/booking/, weak backing, w 0.17). The terms back the handoff promise contractually: "On full payment, you own the final custom deliverables created for you under the Agreement (including designs, copy, and automation built for your business)," while Steady Orbit retains ownership of pre-existing tools, templates and know-how and grants a perpetual license to use them as embedded in the deliverables (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/terms/, weak backing, w 0.11).

## Founders

Two co-founders are named publicly. Michael Valdez is listed as Co-Founder, Strategy & Client Operations and "Creator of the Revenue Blind Spot"; the bio says he is "a Mexican-American entrepreneur" who "spent ten years leading security operations and teams large and small across Silicon Valley tech campuses" before co-founding SOS. Shant Tchatalbachian is listed as Co-Founder, Technical Architecture & Engineering and "Creator of Systems Lab," turning "complex technical requirements into systems that are practical, secure, and built to last" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/founders/, w 0.64, the strongest-scoring site page). llms.txt carries the shorter role titles: "Shant Tchatalbachian (Technical Lead, co-founder) and Michael Valdez (Project Manager, co-founder)" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16).

## Contact and pricing posture

Contact is mike@steadyorbitsystems.com; outbound transactional mail is sent from site@axel.steadyorbitsystems.ai. Pricing is "custom quotes per engagement; no published price list" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16). The FAQ aligns: "Pricing depends on the problem, scope, integrations, and complexity. We scope the work first so you know what is being built and what it will cost before committing" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/, weak backing, w 0.13). Terms add the payment mechanics: quotes are estimates unless an agreement states a fixed fee, invoices are payable within 15 days, late payments may accrue 1.5% per month, and work may pause on overdue accounts after written notice (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/terms/, weak backing, w 0.11).

## The cited statistic

The site's single quoted third-party statistic is: "Up to 85% of customers whose calls go unanswered will not call back, often opting to reach out to a competitor instead," attributed to CallRail's "From Conversations to Conversions" analysis of 1.1 million leads. llms.txt lists it under "Verified facts for citation," making it the company's own curated external claim (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/llms.txt, weak backing, w 0.16).

## What the public record does not say

No revenue figures, client lists, or private business details appear on any public surface, and this corpus deliberately does not include them. There is also no published brand guide or formal "about the company" history page; the founders page and llms.txt are the fullest public self-descriptions. The founders page closes with the company's stated operating philosophy: "AI should improve the systems you already use, not become another system you have to manage" (source: https://steady-orbit.systems-a.workers.dev/founders/, w 0.64).
