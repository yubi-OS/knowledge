# 05. Measured ROI versus illustrative examples

Scope: Separating measured ROI from illustrative worked examples with placeholder numbers, and the labeling discipline that keeps placeholders from escaping their label.

## Two artifact classes, never mixed

The yubiOS model defines measured ROI as every number in the formula tracing to a named evidence source from a real pilot customer's real baseline and a real pilot run, labeled explicitly as measured wherever shown. An illustrative example is a worked calculation using placeholder numbers to show how the formula works, with every placeholder marked as illustrative, not customer data. Illustrative examples exist to explain the model to a prospect before they commit to a pilot; they must never be presented as evidence of actual savings (internal reference: yubi-OS/yubiOS refs/customer-roi-model-2026-07-25.md).

The model goes one step further and declines to embed an illustrative example at all, on the grounds that plausible-looking placeholder figures risk being mistaken for real data if copied out of context. If a walkthrough is wanted for sales conversations, it should be built as a clearly-labeled separate artifact (internal reference: yubi-OS/yubiOS refs/customer-roi-model-2026-07-25.md).

## How regulated industries label hypothetical numbers

Financial services solved this problem with hard rules, and the rules are instructive. National Futures Association rules on hypothetical performance require that the disclaimer addressing the limitations of hypothetical results, and the dangers of undue reliance on them, be displayed as prominently as the hypothetical results themselves (NFA Rulebook, https://www.nfa.futures.org/rulebooksql/rules.aspx?RuleID=9025&Section=9, jev weight 0.57). FINRA's calculator disclaimer states that its model is a rough approximation of future financial performance and that results are hypothetical and may not reflect actual outcomes (FINRA, Tools and Calculators Disclaimer, https://www.finra.org/investors/tools-and-calculators/tools-and-calculators-disclaimer, jev weight 0.09, weak backing). An enterprise ROI calculator takes the same posture, declaring every figure it produces, including estimated year 1 return, to be a hypothetical output of an educational and illustrative estimation tool (EETech, ROI Calculator Disclaimer, https://www.eetech.com/disclaimer-roi-calculator, jev weight 0.10, weak backing).

The strong-source lesson from these is prominence and equivalence: the label must be as visible as the numbers, not a footnote a reader can skip. A weak-backing secondary industry of disclaimer template vendors confirms the norm that results vary and that any outcome shown is not typical of other clients' experience (TermsFeed, https://www.termsfeed.com/blog/testimonial-disclaimer-sample/, jev weight 0.14, weak backing).

## Testimonial and case-study discipline

The same boundary applies when real numbers exist but generalize beyond themselves. US advertising practice expects a testimonial claiming a specific result, for example a customer doubled revenue or saved 20 hours a week, to either show that result is typical or carry a clear results disclaimer (ProofShow, https://proofshow.com/blog/how-to-add-a-results-disclaimer-to-a-testimonial-that-claims-specific-outcomes, jev weight 0.29, weak backing). Buyer-side analysis of vendor case studies makes the distinction operational: case study material decodes into what is real versus what is marketing, and questions surface the difference (SF Labs, https://sfailabs.com/guides/decoding-the-ai-agency-case-study-whats-real-vs-whats-marketing, jev weight 0.26, weak backing). Marketing case studies written without verified results get exposed as fiction, so the label is not just compliance, it is durability under scrutiny (Consultants Experts, https://consultantsexperts.com/marketing-case-studies-separate-fact-from-fiction/, jev weight 0.17, weak backing).

## Practical rules for the boundary

Three rules fall out of the evidence. First, provenance tag: every number in any ROI artifact carries a tag, measured, meaning traceable to a named pilot evidence source, or illustrative, meaning placeholder. Second, no migration: an illustrative number may never be promoted into a measured claim by any path except a real pilot measurement replacing it. Third, no embedding: worked examples live in separate artifacts with their own prominent labels, sized and placed so the label cannot be cropped away, following the prominence norm from regulated disclosure practice (NFA Rulebook, https://www.nfa.futures.org/rulebooksql/rules.aspx?RuleID=9025&Section=9, jev weight 0.57). The ROI concept itself is simple enough to teach with placeholders, and that is all placeholders are for: ROI is a percentage financial ratio measuring profit generated relative to investment cost (Investopedia, https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/returnoninvestment.asp, jev weight 0.53).
