# 01. ROI formula structure for infrastructure products

Scope: How cost-of-alternative ROI formulas are structured for infrastructure products, including per-line-item contributions, baseline minus measured arithmetic, common-unit conversion, and netting the cost of the offer itself.

## The standard formula skeleton

The most common enterprise software ROI formula is a ratio: ROI equals total benefits minus total costs, divided by total costs, times 100 (Atiba, https://www.atiba.com/enterprise-software-roi-calculator-how-to-measure-and-maximize-business-value/, jev weight 0.45, weak backing). Investopedia defines ROI as a financial ratio measuring profit generated relative to investment cost, expressed as a percentage (https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/returnoninvestment.asp, jev weight 0.53). The ratio form is fine for a one-number summary, but it hides the decomposition buyers actually audit. A cost-of-alternative model built for pilots does the opposite: it decomposes savings into named line items so each one can be validated or invalidated independently.

## Per-line-item contributions beat single-number estimates

The yubiOS customer ROI model (internal reference: yubi-OS/yubiOS refs/customer-roi-model-2026-07-25.md) uses this structure explicitly: for each line item, the ROI contribution is the baseline cost or time minus the pilot-measured cost or time, converted to a common unit. Total pilot ROI is the sum of per-line-item contributions minus the recurring cost of the pilot offer itself. The five line items it reuses from the pilot collateral worksheet (OMN-84) are: current root-of-trust cost per device, incident response time for a lost or compromised credential, time to onboard and enroll a new device, audit and compliance evidence burden, and the recurring cost of the offer. This per-item structure matters because it makes the model falsifiable line by line: a pilot can improve enrollment time and worsen incident response simultaneously, and the formula records both rather than blending them into one opaque number.

Commercial calculators converge on the same shape even when they do not formalize it. ERP ROI calculators ask buyers to estimate cost savings, productivity gains, and business benefits as separate inputs before producing a single return estimate (Acumatica, https://www.acumatica.com/erp-roi-calculator/, jev weight 0.47, weak backing). Automation calculators convert time saved into dollars using an hourly rate, then compare it to software costs, with ROI computed from first-year net savings after subtracting recurring tool costs and one-time setup (AI Agent Store, https://aiagentstore.ai/free-tools/automation-roi-time-saved-calculator/, jev weight 0.24, weak backing). The netting step, subtracting the cost of the solution from gross savings, is a constant across these structures.

## Cost of inaction as the mirror image

A complementary framing prices the status quo instead of the improvement. Cisco commissioned Forrester Consulting to survey 718 global IT decision makers in June 2026 on the cost of inaction in IT infrastructure modernization, arguing that delayed modernization carries a quantifiable cost of its own (https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/services/professional/forrester-the-cost-of-inaction.html, jev weight 0.62). Dedicated cost-of-inaction calculators for IT modernization and technical debt estimate what delayed change costs per year (Modern Service Ops, https://modernserviceops.com/cost-of-inaction, jev weight 0.32, weak backing), and sales methodology material pitches cost of inaction as the number that shows a client what delay costs (ProposalCraft, https://proposalcraft.io/resources/tools/cost-of-inaction-calculator, jev weight 0.40, weak backing). For a pilot ROI model, cost of inaction frames the baseline side of each line item: the baseline is not zero, it is the running cost of the current approach.

## Converting to a common unit

Per-line-item contributions must land in one unit before they can be summed. The cleanest discipline is to convert time-based items to dollars using the customer's own stated hourly cost for the relevant staff, not a vendor-invented industry-average rate. The yubiOS model states this explicitly: the customer's internal hourly cost rate is a required baseline input precisely so the formula does not guess a labor rate (internal reference: yubi-OS/yubiOS refs/customer-roi-model-2026-07-25.md). This also keeps time items honest: a time saving only becomes money under an assumption the customer already owns.

## What the formula must not absorb

Two structural cautions follow from the evidence. First, a benefits taxonomy that mixes direct cost savings with indirect revenue impact invites unverifiable inputs; enterprise ROI guidance that lists labor savings and tool consolidation alongside faster sales cycles should be narrowed to items the pilot can actually measure (Atiba, https://www.atiba.com/enterprise-software-roi-calculator-how-to-measure-and-maximize-business-value/, jev weight 0.45, weak backing). Second, ROI percentages without the underlying line items cannot be audited, so a pilot model should always ship the decomposition alongside any single summary figure (Investopedia, https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/returnoninvestment.asp, jev weight 0.53).
