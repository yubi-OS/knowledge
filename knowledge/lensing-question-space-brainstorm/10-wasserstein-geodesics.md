## Benamou-Brenier: dynamic optimal transport

The Benamou-Brenier formulation recasts optimal transport as a least-action problem: find the velocity field and density evolution minimizing kinetic energy subject to the continuity equation, yielding the W2 geodesic between two distributions [https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-99-4867-3_11, jev weight 0.81]. The formulation extends: a Benamou-Brenier structure exists in martingale optimal transport [https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01493, jev weight 0.95], on sub-Riemannian manifolds under specific geometric conditions [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20959, jev weight 0.88], and the passage from Knothe's rearrangement to Brenier's map documents the coordinate-wise-to-true-OT transition [http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1099v1, jev weight 0.91]. Emergent-mind summaries provide orientation on the dynamic formulation [https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/dynamic-optimal-transport, jev weight 0.33; https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/benamou-brenier-formulation, jev weight 0.20, weak backing: both].

## Why the flag stands

The honesty constraint is explicit: there is no general theorem that data exhibits optical caustics (the source document records that the deep-research pass was blunt about this). What the transport channel does have is the singularity structure of the map itself: the singularity set of optimal transport maps, where the Brenier map loses regularity, is documented in the transport literature [https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0965542522080097, jev weight 0.78; https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-76798-3_4.pdf?pdf=inline+link, jev weight 0.87, cited in doc 07], and optimal-transport regularity theory characterizes when maps degenerate [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37847, jev weight 0.30, weak backing]. These singularities are the transport analog of caustics: the place where the map from source to target measure loses information.
