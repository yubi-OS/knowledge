# 05 - The Rayleigh forms that do not fit: shear flow, bubbles, damping

**Scope:** the Rayleigh forms that do not fit a corpus audit instrument: the inviscid shear-flow stability equation, the Rayleigh-Plesset bubble ODE, and Rayleigh damping, and why they are excluded.

## Rayleigh's stability equation and why it cannot enter

In fluid dynamics, Rayleigh's equation is a linear ordinary differential equation for the hydrodynamic stability of a parallel, incompressible, inviscid shear flow (weight 0.30, weak backing, [Wikipedia, Rayleigh's equation (fluid dynamics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh%27s_equation_(fluid_dynamics))). MIT course material on the subject states the classical result that comes out of it: for instability it is necessary that U'' (the second derivative of the velocity profile) is positive for some y and negative elsewhere, in other words the velocity profile must have an inflection point inside the flow (weight 0.92, [MIT OCW, Advanced Fluid Dynamics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/1-63-advanced-fluid-dynamics-of-the-environment-fall-2002/9e69490d9643ab34924a098737183595_53Rayleigh.pdf); the same notes collected weight 0.67 in a second pass, [MIT fluids module](https://web.mit.edu/fluids-modules/www/instability/5-3Rayleigh.pdf)). Lecture handouts on inflection-point instability derive the same criterion from first principles for a parallel shear flow U(x) (weight 0.70, [Colorado ASTR 5410 handout 7](http://lcd-www.colorado.edu/~axbr9098/teach/ASTR_5410/lectures/7_Inflection_Pt_Inst/notes.pdf)).

The reason this form does not fit a corpus audit instrument is structural, not aesthetic. The equation needs a velocity profile, a wavenumber, and real analysis; it is an eigenvalue problem for a flow field. A corpus instrument has no flow field. The inputs of the instrument are a bit matrix, an embedding, and a cut structure; there is no quantity anywhere in the pipeline for which "inflection point of the profile" is even defined. The exclusion verdict is the same one the project already recorded for the physics negatives (the GL, Navier-Stokes, and Landau radius negatives): no physical field enters ranking.

This is worth being precise about because the name collision is real. "Rayleigh's equation" is a name shared by a variational eigenvalue identity (the Rayleigh quotient), a fluid stability ODE, a bubble dynamics ODE, and a damping ansatz. Only the first fits the instrument's mathematics. The dig confirms the fluid form is a fully separate object with its own literature and its own theorem (the inflection-point criterion above), which is exactly why it cannot be silently conflated with the quotient.

## Rayleigh-Plesset: an ODE about bubbles

The Rayleigh-Plesset equation is a nonlinear ordinary differential equation governing the radial motion of a spherical bubble, balancing the inertia of the surrounding liquid against pressure differences, surface tension, and viscous forces (weight 0.13, weak backing, [Wikipedia, Rayleigh-Plesset equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh%E2%80%93Plesset_equation)). A 2024 Springer chapter constructs the Rayleigh-Plesset equation for a spherical bubble and studies the effects of liquid compressibility on the equation's results (weight 0.96, [Springer, Equations of Bubble Dynamics](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-75041-0_2)). Reference-level treatments describe it as the standard framework applied to the study of cavitation bubbles (weight 0.44, weak backing, [HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Rayleigh%E2%80%93Plesset_equation); also weight 0.20, weak backing, [Bohrium sciencepedia](https://www.bohrium.com/en/sciencepedia/feynman/fluid_mechanics_undergraduate-bubble_dynamics_and_Rayleigh-Plesset_equation)).

The exclusion here is the same in kind: it is a physical ODE with time as its independent variable and material constants (surface tension, viscosity) as its parameters. Nothing in a corpus audit consumes or produces such a quantity. An ODE cannot be evaluated on a bit matrix, and its solution is a bubble radius history, not a score, a bound, or a certificate.

## Rayleigh damping: a note on evidence

The dig surfaced no authoritative source for the Rayleigh damping form under the queries run; no result in the archive for this subtopic addresses damping. The exclusion verdict for the damping form therefore rests on the project's recorded negative alone, without external backing in this corpus, and the formula itself is not restated here because nothing in the dig supports a characterization of it. This is recorded as a known gap rather than papered over: a claim with no source is deleted, not softened.

## What the exclusions protect

The value of recording the exclusions precisely is that the instrument's scope manifest can state them. The non-claims list explicitly says no Rayleigh stability, no Rayleigh-Plesset, and no damping term is used anywhere in the shipped code or proofs. Two results in the dig were off-topic noise and are recorded to mark evidence boundaries: a Stack Overflow question about browser image caching (weight 0.02, [stackoverflow.com](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/126772/how-to-force-a-web-browser-not-to-cache-images)) and a cosmology preprint that borrows the Rayleigh-Plesset equation for an analogy (weight 0.05, weak backing, [hal.science](https://hal.science/hal-02467421)). Neither supports a claim in this doc.
