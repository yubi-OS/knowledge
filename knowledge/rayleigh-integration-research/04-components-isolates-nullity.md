# 04 - Nullity, components, and isolates: the exact disconnection facts

**Scope:** Laplacian nullity equals the number of connected components, isolates as size-1 components, and the use of lambda_2 = 0 as an exact disconnection certificate in spectral clustering prior art.

## The multiplicity theorem

The core identity is clean: the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of a graph Laplacian equals the number of connected components of the graph. Oxford lecture notes on spectral graph theory and clustering state it as a stronger result attached to the basic fact that a connected graph has a strictly positive second eigenvalue: "the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue is equal to the number of connected components" (weight 0.79, [Oxford CDT lecture 6](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~cucuring/SpectralGraphTheory_Clustering_CDT.pdf)). The Wikipedia algebraic connectivity article states the same identity as a corollary: the number of times 0 appears as an eigenvalue in the Laplacian is the number of connected components in the graph (weight 0.29, weak backing, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity)).

Two University of Chicago REU papers work the same ground from first principles: Marsden's 2013 paper studies the eigenvalues of the Laplacian and their relationship to graph structure, including what multiplicities indicate about components (weight 0.40, weak backing, [Marsden REU 2013](https://math.uchicago.edu/~may/REU2013/REUPapers/Marsden.pdf)), and Walchessen's 2019 paper explicitly covers "the connection between connected components and eigenvalues of the Laplacian" as one of its sections (weight 0.26, weak backing, [Walchessen REU 2019](https://math.uchicago.edu/~may/REU2019/REUPapers/Walchessen.pdf)). The convergence of a graduate lecture, an encyclopedia, and two undergraduate research papers on the same identity is the reason the instrument treats it as exact rather than approximate.

## Isolates as the degenerate component

An isolate is a vertex with no edges, which is a component of size 1. In nullity terms, each isolate contributes its own zero eigenvalue, so counting isolates and counting components are the same computation at different granularity. This is why an instrument can report both: the component list (sorted sizes) tells whether the corpus is one connected blob or several islands, and the isolate count tells whether any document is completely cut off from the similarity graph.

The identity also has a directionality worth stating: it is an iff. Zero eigenvalue multiplicity equals component count, and lambda_2 > 0 if and only if the graph is connected. So a lambda_2 of exactly 0 is not a heuristic for disconnection, it is the disconnection certificate itself.

## Prior art: spectral clustering reads the same spectrum

The spectral clustering literature uses exactly these facts as its foundation. Step-by-step teaching material notes that the lowest eigenvalue of the Laplacian system is always 0, corresponding to the constant eigenvector, and that the second lowest eigenvalue with its eigenvector, the Fiedler vector, carries the connectivity information (weight 0.41, weak backing, [changyaochen.github.io](https://changyaochen.github.io/spectral-clustering/)). A machine learning course page describes spectral clustering as a graph-based technique that discovers clusters with complex, non-convex shapes that K-Means cannot handle (weight 0.32, weak backing, [machinelearning101](https://machinelearning101.com/modules/clustering/spectral-clustering)). The Wikipedia article adds a caveat that matters for threshold-built similarity graphs: when the graph is constructed with a hard connectivity criterion, that is binary adjacency based on a distance threshold, and no normalization is applied to the Laplacian, the eigenstructure behaves differently than in the normalized case (weight 0.16, weak backing, [Wikipedia, Spectral clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_clustering)).

That caveat is not academic. The instrument's isolation graph is threshold-built (a chord cutoff on the sphere), binary, and unnormalized. The prior art's warning is that in exactly this regime the unnormalized Laplacian's zero-eigenvalue multiplicity remains the reliable signal, while softer readings (gap sizes, eigenvector shapes near the threshold) are less trustworthy. This is consistent with the instrument's choice to hard-code the exact integer counts as the primary output and treat float eigenvalue estimates as secondary.

## What is exact and what is not

The exact objects in this subtopic are integer counts: components (by BFS), isolates (components of size 1), sorted component sizes. The identity linking them to the spectrum (nullity = component count) is exact but, in the instrument's Lean scope, stated rather than proved: the scope manifest explicitly lists "nullity = number of components" among the non-claims, because its proof needs finite-dimensional spectral machinery that core Lean does not carry. What the Lean file does prove is the supporting algebra: constants sit in the kernel of the quadratic form, and a 0/1 indicator of a single untouched vertex contributes nothing to the form, which is the algebraic seed the component facts grow from (see doc 07).

One result in the dig was pure noise and is recorded to mark evidence boundaries: a graphing calculator landing page collected weight 0.42 from the model and a "Spectral" film disambiguation page collected 0.06 (weights recorded in the archive; neither supports any claim here).
