# 01 - The Rayleigh quotient and the variational characterization of eigenvalues

**Scope:** the Rayleigh quotient R(x) = x^T A x / x^T x and the Rayleigh-Ritz / Courant-Fischer variational characterization of eigenvalues: the mathematical base the instrument's readings stand on.

## The quotient itself

For a Hermitian (real symmetric) matrix A and a nonzero vector x, the Rayleigh quotient is the scalar R(x) = x^T A x / x^T x. The Utah numerical-analysis course notes define exactly this object and use it as the entry point to eigenvalue theory (weight 0.66, [Utah math6610 lecture notes](https://www.sci.utah.edu/~akil/docs/courses/2020fall/math6610/lec04.pdf)). Because the quotient is a ratio of two quadratic forms, it is scale invariant: multiplying x by any nonzero constant leaves R(x) unchanged. That invariance is what later lets an instrument evaluate the quotient on unit vectors or on raw indicator vectors without changing the reading.

## Courant-Fischer: eigenvalues as an optimization problem

The Courant-Fischer theorem gives a variational formulation of the eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix and is explicitly pitched as useful for obtaining bounds on eigenvalues (weight 0.84, [MIT OCW scribe notes, 18.409](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-409-topics-in-theoretical-computer-science-an-algorithmists-toolkit-fall-2009/535add3f6457cc13e51d9774f16bf48f_MIT18_409F09_scribe3.pdf)). The same material is taught in the MMiDS textbook, which characterizes eigenvalues in terms of optimization problems and notes the idea had already been met in the context of the SVD (weight 0.79, [MMiDS chapter 5.3](https://mmids-textbook.github.io/chap05_specgraph/03_extremal/roch-mmids-specgraph-extremal.html)).

The maximization side is the part an instrument uses directly. Spielman's Spectral and Algebraic Graph Theory text states that the vectors which maximize the Rayleigh quotient are exactly the eigenvectors of the largest eigenvalue of M, and that the theorem supplies a similar characterization of all the eigenvalues (weight 0.65, [Spielman SAGT, Courant-Fischer chapter](https://jasoncantarella.com/downloads/sagt-courant-fischer.pdf)). In other words, lambda_max = max R(x) is not an analogy but an identity, and every "best direction" reading of a covariance matrix is a Rayleigh quotient evaluation.

The Wikipedia min-max article describes the same result in its general form, the Courant-Fischer-Weyl min-max principle, as a variational characterization of eigenvalues of compact Hermitian operators on Hilbert spaces (weight 0.15, weak backing, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min-max_theorem)). The functional-analytic generality is not needed for finite corpora, so the project treats the finite-dimensional statement as the load-bearing one.

## Rayleigh-Ritz: what a restricted frame buys you

The Rayleigh-Ritz method is the practical inverse of the maximization principle: pick a finite basis, project the operator onto it, and read eigenvalue estimates off the small matrix. MathWorld describes the technique as computing eigenfunctions and eigenvalues by requiring a quadratic functional to take a stationary value subject to a normalization condition and boundary conditions (weight 0.75, [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Rayleigh-RitzVariationalTechnique.html)). In mechanical vibrations, the same construction is standard: each eigenvalue produced by discretizing the displacement variational principle with Rayleigh-Ritz is an upper bound to the corresponding exact eigenvalue (weight 0.75, [Stanford AA242B course notes](https://web.stanford.edu/group/frg/course_work/AA242B/CA-AA242B-Ch5.pdf)).

Two recent results sharpen what the method guarantees. A 2022 arXiv paper gives a simple proof of the well known fact that approximate eigenvalues provided by the Rayleigh-Ritz variational method are increasingly accurate upper bounds to the exact ones, working from the variational principle itself (weight 0.88, [arXiv:2206.05122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05122)). And a 2024 paper from the UC Davis group establishes existence and variational characterization results for 2D-eigenvalues, connecting the 2D eigenvalue problem to eigenvalue optimization (weight 0.77, [Lu, Su, Bai](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~bai/publications/LuSuBai24.pdf)).

The upper-bound direction matters most for the instrument. When a frozen set of axes (a PCA frame computed once, on a baseline corpus) is applied to a new covariance matrix, the quotient of the new matrix restricted to those axes cannot exceed the new matrix's top eigenvalue sum. The frozen frame is a test subspace, and Rayleigh-Ritz theory says test values sit at or below the true extremal values. The gap between the frozen-frame reading and the true top-k sum is therefore a signed, monotone quantity: 0 on the baseline frame itself, growing as the corpus drifts away from the frame.

## Why this form fits a corpus audit instrument

The variational characterization needs exactly three things the instrument has: a symmetric matrix (the bit covariance), vectors to evaluate (the frozen axes), and scalar arithmetic. It needs no physical field, no continuum, and no analysis beyond finite-dimensional linear algebra. The physics-flavored variational statements exist too: quantum mechanics teaches that the ground state has the lowest possible energy, so varying a test wavefunction downward gives estimates of the ground state energy (weight 0.61, [UCSD physics 130a notes](https://quantummechanics.ucsd.edu/ph130a/130_notes/node375.html)). That is the same principle in a different coat, and it confirms that the quotient-based reading is a variational principle in the standard sense, not an invented heuristic.

The aggregator-level description of Rayleigh-Ritz as estimating eigenvalues of a vibrating structure through a reduced-order model, with accuracy depending strongly on the choice of basis (weight 0.37, weak backing, [ScienceDirect topics page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/rayleigh-ritz-method)), is consistent with the above but adds nothing the primary lecture notes do not already establish.
