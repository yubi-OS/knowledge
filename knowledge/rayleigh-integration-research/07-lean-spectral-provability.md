# 07 - What core Lean proves and what needs Mathlib: the provability split

**Scope:** what is elementary algebra provable in core Lean without Mathlib (PSD, cut identities, rational Rayleigh-Ritz witnesses) versus what needs the spectral theorem or Mathlib.

## The two sides of the split

The instrument's Lean development draws a hard line. On one side sit statements that are elementary algebra over integers and rationals: a quadratic form is nonnegative on the Laplacian (positive semidefiniteness by expansion), constants sit in the kernel, scaling and shifting the test vector scales and shifts the form, the quadratic form of a 0/1 indicator equals the cut size, and an isolated vertex contributes nothing. On the other side sit statements that need finite-dimensional spectral theory: the spectral theorem itself, the identity nullity = number of components, the Ky Fan maximum principle, and Cheeger's inequality. The first side is proved in core Lean; the second side is stated, not proved.

The split is grounded in what the proof assistants actually provide. Lean 4 is a fully extensible interactive theorem prover and functional programming language with an expressive logical foundation for writing mathematical specifications and proofs and formally verified programs (weight 0.94, [Lean 4 paper, lean-lang.org](https://lean-lang.org/papers/lean4.pdf)). Mathlib, its mathematics library, is a user-maintained community library (weight 0.88, [github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4)) that carries the spectral machinery: the matrix spectrum module documents the diagonalization theorem, the spectral theorem for matrices, and the fact that a Hermitian matrix can be diagonalized by a change of basis (weight 0.79, [Mathlib.Analysis.Matrix.Spectrum](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.html)). The library's scope page confirms the analysis-heavy coverage (normed spaces, finite-dimensional completeness, and so on) that spectral statements lean on (weight 0.80, [Mathematics in mathlib](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib-overview.html)).

Depending on Mathlib is a real dependency cost: formalizations of the spectral theorem against Mathlib exist as separate projects, such as a Lean 4 development of the spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators prepared for submission (weight 0.53, [github.com/savarin/lean-spectral-theory](https://github.com/savarin/lean-spectral-theory)). A third-party atlas records the exact Mathlib declaration name for the finite-dimensional spectral theorem (weight 0.34, weak backing, [proofatlas.ai](https://www.proofatlas.ai/library-theorems/finite-dimensional-spectral-theorem/)). Pulling any of this in would expand the audit's trusted base from the Lean kernel to a large community library, which is precisely what the no-Mathlib discipline avoids.

## What the cut identities give for free

The elementary side is strong enough to carry the load the instrument actually needs. The quadratic form x^T L x of a 0/1 indicator vector equals the cut size of the corresponding set; that is expansion arithmetic, provable by cases on edges. The witness construction for the Rayleigh-Ritz bound is more of the same: with x = n times the indicator of S minus k times the indicator of its complement (n the corpus size, k the cut side), the numerator x^T L x equals n squared times the cut size, and the denominator equals n times k times (n minus k). Both computations are rational, exact, and within reach of core Lean tactics, including decided finite cases.

The practical consequence is the bound_holds check's semantics. The float estimate of lambda_2 is produced by power iteration; the rational witness is produced by exact arithmetic. If the float ever exceeds the rational witness, the float is wrong, not the inequality. The Lean proofs guarantee the inequality's direction; the runtime check merely guards the floating-point pipeline against violating what has been proved.

## Version discipline and the core toolchain

The toolchain fact that makes "core Lean" a precise claim is the version pin. The standard Lean 4 tutorial targets a specific compiler version, currently stated as Lean 4.33.0 (weight 0.09, weak backing, [Theorem Proving in Lean 4](https://leanprover.github.io/theorem_proving_in_lean4/)), and the instrument's CI compiles the proof file against that pinned version, asserts the file contains no sorry, validates the scope manifest, and checks the printed set of axioms. Teaching material for Lean 4 tactics distinguishes core tactics from Mathlib tactics (weight 0.18, weak backing, [lean4.dev](https://lean4.dev/tactics)), which is the same split at the tactic level: the instrument's proof file restricts itself to the core side.

The scope manifest is the honesty document. It lists what is not proved alongside what is: the nullity identity, the float Fiedler value, Ky Fan itself, semantic claims, and the fluid equations are named as non-claims. A CI step validates the manifest, so the boundary between proved and stated is itself machine-checked rather than prose-only.

## What the split costs and why it is worth it

The cost is that some true statements about the instrument's outputs are only stated, never proved in-repo: in particular the component-counting identity and everything spectral. The benefit is that everything that is proved rests on nothing but the Lean kernel and the pinned compiler, with an explicit, CI-checked non-claims list covering the rest. A third-party proof interface project illustrates the general Lean ecosystem tooling around such files (weight 0.30, weak backing, [github.com/Paper-Proof/paperproof](https://github.com/Paper-Proof/paperproof)); a university lecture series on Lean 4 for formal proof covers the propositions-as-types foundation and basic tactics the elementary side uses (weight 0.53, [BIMSA](https://bimsa.net/activity/AI-MatRes/)).

One dig result is a complete name collision and is recorded to mark evidence boundaries: the encyclopedia article on the drug "lean" collected weights 0.71 and 0.03 from the model (weights recorded in the archive; it supports no claim here, and its high weight is a known weighting artifact).
