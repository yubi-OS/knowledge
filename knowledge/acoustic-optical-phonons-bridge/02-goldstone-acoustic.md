# Goldstone modes and the acoustic sum rule

**Scope:** acoustic phonons as the Goldstone modes of spontaneously broken continuous translation symmetry, the acoustic sum rule (ASR) as the exact algebraic statement of that breaking, and the practical numerical status of the ASR in computed force constants.

## Broken translation symmetry and soft Goldstone modes

A crystal is a system with continuous translation symmetry spontaneously broken down to a discrete lattice. Course material on phonons at low temperatures states the identification plainly: "Crystal: continuous translation symmetry spontaneously broken. soft Goldstone modes. invariant under a constant shift" ([Phonons at low temperatures, DRT-BT2024 lecture notes](https://indico.in2p3.fr/event/30801/contributions/131359/attachments/83971/125137/Phonons_DRTBT2024.pdf), jev weight 0.715). The same framing appears in the peer-reviewed literature on holographic fluids, where the spontaneous breaking of spatial translations and the Goldstone nature of acoustic phonons are treated together ([EPJC, Transverse Goldstone mode in holographic fluids with broken translations](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-10430-w), jev weight 0.533).

The scope of the identification is wider than the acoustic branches alone. A 2019 status review reports that "both acoustic and optical phonons may be described as arising from a spontaneous breaking of a global symmetry" ([physica status solidi b, Higgs and Goldstone Modes in Crystalline Solids, 10.1002/pssb.201900443](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pssb.201900443), jev weight 0.935). This extends the textbook picture, in which only the gapless acoustic modes are Goldstone modes.

## The acoustic sum rule as translational invariance

The algebraic statement behind the Goldstone zero is the acoustic sum rule. A detailed derivation note builds the dynamical matrix D(q) from the interatomic force constants and shows what translational invariance forces: "the total energy cannot change if the entire crystal is rigidly translated", which requires the force-constant rows to sum to zero, so that the mass-weighted rigid-translation vector is annihilated by the dynamical matrix at q = 0 ([Nanotheochem, The dynamical matrix and the acoustic sum rule](https://nanotheochem.github.io/the-grid/physics/dynamical-matrix-asr), jev weight 0.685). Three orthogonal choices of translation direction give three zero eigenvalues: the three acoustic branches going to omega = 0 as q goes to 0. The same note makes the Goldstone connection explicit: "a uniform shift is a Goldstone mode of broken continuous translational symmetry, and the acoustic phonons are its finite-q dispersion, omega ~ c_s |q| with c_s the sound velocities" (Nanotheochem, weight 0.685).

Lecture material on force constants and phonon dispersion states the constraint in the same terms: the crystal energy must be invariant under a global translation of the whole crystal, and this imposes a constraint that makes the acoustic frequencies vanish at Gamma ([Ghosez, Interatomic force constants and phonon dispersion curves](https://abinit14.sciencesconf.org/data/program/Lecture_May14_Ghosez_PhonDisp_v1.pdf), jev weight 0.777). Software documentation for the LetzElPhC code lists the ASR as rules obeyed by the force-constant matrix, "derived from the invariance of the total energy under global translation" ([LetzElPhC theory page](https://yambo-code.github.io/LetzElPhC/theory/asr/), jev weight 0.679).

## The ASR in numerical practice

The ASR is exact for the true force constants, but "violated by essentially every set you actually compute", so production phonon codes re-impose it numerically (Nanotheochem, weight 0.685). Hands-on material for density-functional perturbation theory codes treats "Acoustic sum rule at Gamma" as a standard correction step, precisely because computed interatomic force constants carry numerical inaccuracies that break the rule ([QE 2019 hands-on session, DFPT](http://qe2019.ijs.si/talks/handson-day3-DFPT.pdf), jev weight 0.702). The CASTEP phonon guide describes the fix from the other direction: provided the amplitude of the symmetry violation is not too large, a transformation can be applied to the computed dynamical or force-constant matrix so that it exactly satisfies the required conditions ([CASTEP phonon guide, running phonon calculations](https://www.tcm.phy.cam.ac.uk/castep/Phonons_Guide/2-sec:examples.html), jev weight 0.800).

## Beyond translation: the Born-Huang conditions

Translational invariance is only the first of the Born-Huang invariance conditions on the force constants, which follow from conserving total linear and angular momentum. The rotational-invariance and vanishing-stress (Huang) conditions become binding, rather than automatic, in low-dimensional systems, where they govern the flexural branch; for bulk crystals the translational ASR is the condition that matters in practice ([Nanotheochem](https://nanotheochem.github.io/the-grid/physics/dynamical-matrix-asr), jev weight 0.685).

## Why this matters as a structural gate

The chain of statements is tight and each link is independently sourced: broken continuous translation symmetry implies soft Goldstone modes invariant under a constant shift (DRT-BT2024, weight 0.715); the ASR is the exact force-constant statement of that invariance (Nanotheochem, weight 0.685; Ghosez, weight 0.777); the ASR forces three zero eigenvalues at Gamma, which are the acoustic branches (Nanotheochem, weight 0.685); and the identification of phonons with symmetry-breaking modes extends, in the modern reading, to optical phonons as well (pssb 201900443, weight 0.935). Any framework that borrows the "gapless protected mode" structure of acoustic phonons inherits exactly these algebraic obligations.
