# LO-TO splitting and the Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relation

**Scope:** why longitudinal and transverse optical phonons split in polar crystals, the role of the macroscopic depolarizing field, the dielectric constants that enter the polar coupling, and the Reststrahlen band that the splitting opens.

## Polarization of optical vibrations

In polar semiconductor crystals the optical vibration is polar: "the atoms in a lattice vibrate against each other, which can produce polarization effects", and the lattice polarization P_Lat is proportional to the lattice displacement U with a proportionality built from the Frohlich constant, in which the vacuum dielectric constant, the static dielectric permittivity epsilon_s, and the high-frequency dielectric permittivity epsilon_inf all appear ([TU Wien dissertation chapter, Polar Optical Phonon Scattering](https://www.iue.tuwien.ac.at/phd/milovanovic/diss_htmlse12.html), jev weight 0.785). Electrons in such crystals are scattered through the interaction with the Coulomb field of these lattice polarization waves, and electron-LO-phonon scattering is the dominant intersubband scattering mechanism for separated subbands in polar semiconductors (TU Wien, weight 0.785).

## The macroscopic field of the longitudinal mode

The splitting mechanism is electrostatic and selective: for longitudinal optical vibrations there is an extra restoring force due to the polarization field generated by the vibration (TU Wien, weight 0.785). Survey material on LO phonons records the observable consequence: LO-LO splitting has been observed on phonon sidebands "due to the interaction with the macroscopic longitudinal optical phonon electric field", in materials including CdS and ZnO ([ScienceDirect topics, LO Phonon](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemistry/lo-phonon), jev weight 0.521). The asymmetry is the point: the longitudinal displacement builds a macroscopic depolarizing field along the displacement, the transverse displacement does not, so the LO branch is pushed above the TO branch in polar materials.

## Consequences in the dielectric function

The LO-TO splitting has a direct optical signature. In the Reststrahlen band between the transverse optical (TO) and longitudinal optical (LO) phonon frequencies, the real part of the dielectric function is negative, which localizes electric fields at the crystal surface and produces surface phonon polaritons ([Nature Communications, Hybrid longitudinal-transverse phonon polaritons, 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09414-4), jev weight 0.964). In that same window only transverse optical phonons participate in the creation of phonon polaritons, while electrical currents mainly generate longitudinal optical phonons; the two families are coupled only weakly in ordinary crystals (Nature Communications, weight 0.964).

The separation is not absolute. Using long-cell polytypes of silicon carbide, strong coupling between transverse phonon polaritons and zone-folded longitudinal optical phonons has been demonstrated, with a microscopic theory predicting hybrid longitudinal-transverse excitations and an experimental observation obtained by tuning a nanopillar-array resonance through the folded LO mode, producing a clear spectral anti-crossing (Nature Communications, weight 0.964).

## Doping and the splitting

The splitting is also a probe of electronic structure: recent work studies electron-phonon interaction and longitudinal-transverse phonon splitting in doped semiconductors ([arXiv:2202.02835](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.02835v4), jev weight 0.897), where the free-carrier response modifies the dielectric background that the polar mode sees.

## The Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relation

The standard relation connecting the two frequencies to the two dielectric constants is the Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relation, which links the LO and TO phonon frequencies of a polar mode to the static and high-frequency dielectric permittivities ([Wikipedia, Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyddane%E2%80%93Sachs%E2%80%93Teller_relation), jev weight 0.242). The two permittivities entering the relation are exactly the epsilon_s and epsilon_inf that appear in the Frohlich coupling of the polar optical phonon (TU Wien, weight 0.785). Generalized forms of the Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relationship for polar vibrations have been derived for low-symmetry crystal systems such as monoclinic and triclinic materials ([ResearchGate, The Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relationship for polar vibrations in materials with monoclinic and triclinic crystal systems](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/296196866_The_Lyddane-Sachs-Teller_relationship_for_polar_vibrations_in_materials_with_monoclinic_and_triclinic_crystal_systems), jev weight 0.430); the reader should note that in this corpus the canonical single-formula statement is backed by a low-jev-weight source and the generalized literature, so the structural claim (a frequency ratio fixed by a permittivity ratio) is well supported while exact numerical factors should be re-verified against a primary source before use.

## Summary of the mechanism chain

Polar optical vibration produces lattice polarization (TU Wien, weight 0.785); the longitudinal component of that polarization builds a macroscopic electric field that acts back on the mode as an extra restoring force (TU Wien, weight 0.785; ScienceDirect, weight 0.521); the restoring force raises the LO frequency above the TO frequency; the interval between them is the Reststrahlen band where the dielectric function's real part is negative (Nature Communications, weight 0.964); and the endpoint frequencies are tied to the static and high-frequency permittivities by the Lyddane-Sachs-Teller relation (Wikipedia, weight 0.242; TU Wien, weight 0.785).
