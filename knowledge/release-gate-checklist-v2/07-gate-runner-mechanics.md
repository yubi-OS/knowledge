# Gate runner mechanics: automated versus human

Scope: which launch gates a machine can run and which need a human decision, and how delivery platforms encode that split.

## The mechanical split

A gate inventory splits cleanly along one line: gates whose evidence is machine-checkable, and gates whose evidence is a human relationship. Delivery-tooling documentation shows the machine side in detail. Azure Pipelines supports manual deployment approvals per stage, where the deployment is paused at each point approval is required until the specified approver grants approval, rejects the release, or reassigns it (w=0.957, high backing) [https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/pipelines/release/approvals/approvals?view=azure-devops]. A manual approval check on an environment ensures deployment only happens after the designated user reviews the changes being deployed (w=0.925, high backing) [https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/pipelines/process/approvals?view=azure-devops]. These are human decisions embedded in machine plumbing: the platform enforces that the human acts, but the decision itself is human.

Release gates push further toward automation: using release gates, you can specify application health criteria that must be met before a release is promoted to the next environment (w=0.720, high backing) [https://microsoftlearning.github.io/AZ400-DesigningandImplementingMicrosoftDevOpsSolutions/Instructions/Labs/AZ400_M03_L08_Control_Deployments_using_Release_Gates.html], and the combination of manual approvals, gates, and manual intervention configures all pre-deployment requirements for a workflow (w=0.894, high backing) [https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/pipelines/release/deploy-using-approvals?view=azure-devops]. Octopus Deploy's manual intervention step pauses a deployment to wait for a member of a specified team to allow it to proceed or abort it (w=0.835, high backing) [https://octopus.com/docs/projects/built-in-step-templates/manual-intervention-and-approvals]. ArgoCD implements the same patterns with sync windows, manual sync policies, and Git-based approvals (w=0.514, high backing) [https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-26-argocd-approval-workflows-deployments/view].

## What automation replaces, and what it cannot

The fully automated end of the spectrum is described by automated release management: it replaces manual review and approval steps with automated quality gates, policy enforcement, and deployment orchestration, evaluating each change against defined criteria rather than routing decisions through a central committee (w=0.518, high backing) [https://www.harness.io/blog/automated-release-management-from-cabs-to-continuous-delivery]. Promotion design guidance makes the boundary precise: automated gates are objective and measurable (tests pass, coverage above a threshold, no vulnerabilities), manual approvals are required only for clearly defined high-risk changes, the approval process is documented with decision criteria, and an audit trail exists for all approvals (w=0.800, high backing) [https://archman.dev/docs/delivery-engineering/ci-cd-pipelines/promotion-approvals-gates].

Mapping this onto a launch inventory: CI-run engineering gates (test suites, build and signing verification, negative tests) are automatable end to end. Commercial gates, an auditor engagement, discovery interviews, a pilot contract, a reference relationship, are not. No approval workflow can sign an NDA or sit through a discovery interview. The agent-side role for the non-automatable gates is tracking and evidence filing; the decision and the relationship stay with the commercial owner.

## Readiness gates as formal pass/fail decisions

The gate concept itself has formal definitions worth importing. A readiness gate is the formal pass/fail decision authorizing a release iteration to advance, with change management as its own distinct gate and documentation rigor growing toward the final gate (w=0.390, weak backing) [https://if4it.org/best-practices/release-management/release-readiness-gates-entry-and-exit-criteria-for-every-environment/]. Release readiness is framed as a measurable state, not a gut call: a release either satisfies objective quality gates or it does not (w=0.298, weak backing) [https://beefed.ai/en/release-readiness-checklist-dashboard]. The honest design note comes from scorecard practice: use numeric thresholds where useful, and written judgment where human context matters (w=0.310, weak backing) [https://qaskills.sh/skills/thetestingacademy/release-readiness-checklist]. A gate inventory that pretends the commercial gates are scoreable is misdesigned; one that marks them as human-judgment gates with evidence artifacts is honest.

## Go/no-go as a decision control

A formal go/no-go process is a decision control, not a meeting to rehash work: it is the organization's last line of defense, converting risk into simple, binary outcomes backed by evidence (w=0.324, weak backing) [https://beefed.ai/en/go-no-go-decision-framework-checklist]. Launch criteria are the explicit conditions a product must meet before launch, documented in advance and used as go/no-go decision points, aligning teams on what ready actually means (w=0.153, weak backing) [https://www.startups.com/lexicon/launch-criteria]. Readiness checklists connect the layers: strategy, customer evidence, product quality, engineering, go-to-market execution, ownership, risk, rollout, and post-launch measurement, with the rule that a task is not ready simply because someone marked it complete (w=0.332, weak backing) [https://selleo.com/blog/product-launch-checklist].

## The runner table

The practical artifact is a runner table with one row per gate: the gate, its runner (CI plus run-log fetch, or a named human role), and whether it is automatable. The engineering rows are fully automated. The audit row needs an external firm plus a commercial owner. The pricing and reference rows need the commercial owner and the customer's deployment owner. The agent's automatable contribution is fetching run logs, filing evidence artifacts, and keeping the planning documents in lockstep with gate status, which is exactly the tracking work the machine layer of a delivery pipeline is good at.
