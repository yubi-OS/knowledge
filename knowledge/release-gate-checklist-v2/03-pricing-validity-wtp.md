# Pricing validity: willingness-to-pay interviews

Scope: the willingness-to-pay interview layer of a pricing-validity gate: how many interviews, what counts as WTP evidence, and how transcripts become gate-grade proof.

## Why interviews gate pricing

A pricing-validity gate asks a specific question: does anyone actually pay the proposed tier? Quantitative pricing methods answer part of it. The Van Westendorp method reveals what customers will pay but not why, which is the gap customer development interviews address (w=0.581, high backing) [https://www.innovationprofessional.com/resources/methods/customer-development-interview.html]. Qualitative pricing research interviews are designed to surface willingness to pay, price anchors, and the emotional logic behind buying decisions, beyond what surveys can show (w=0.143, weak backing) [https://www.koji.so/docs/pricing-research-interviews]. A launch gate built on interviews is therefore claiming causality, not just a price point: the gate holds when the reason a customer pays is understood, not just the amount.

## Interview counts as a falsifiable criterion

A gate needs a number. SaaS pricing validation guidance describes a step-by-step path covering internal validation, customer interviews, and live sales tests (w=0.568, high backing) [https://www.willingnesstopay.com/resources/how-to-validate-new-saas-pricing-internal-validation-interviews-sales-test], and customer-validation frameworks describe the signals that prove validation has happened (w=0.321, weak backing) [https://www.koji.so/docs/customer-validation-guide]. A common gate formulation is a minimum count of completed discovery interviews with named prospects, plus a threshold subset of those who stated explicit willingness to pay at the proposed tier. The count is falsifiable, the names are checkable, and the threshold separates "we did research" from "the research cleared the bar."

## What counts as WTP evidence

Not all positive answers are evidence. Pricing interview methodology warns against asking unreliable pricing questions directly and recommends structuring interviews around value, urgency, and price sensitivity instead (w=0.228, weak backing) [https://letmira.com/blog/pricing-research-interviews-how-to-test-willingness-to-pay-without-asking-bad-pricing-questions]. B2B discovery frameworks interrogate past user actions, existing operational budgets, and manual workarounds rather than opinions about future features (w=0.206, weak backing) [https://innovation-creativity.com/b2b-discovery-interview-15-questions-with-script/]. A gate should therefore define WTP evidence behaviorally: statements anchored to budget, to a current spend line, or to a signed commitment, preserved verbatim, rather than a nod in a call.

The preservation standard matters. Practice guidance on converting interviews into requirements describes a process where every decision traces back to a specific customer statement, a verbatim quote rather than an opinion (w=0.285, weak backing) [https://blog.buildbetter.ai/how-to-turn-customer-interviews-into-a-prd/]. The same traceability standard applies to pricing evidence: gate-grade transcripts store the quote, the prospect, and the date, so an auditor can re-verify the claim without re-interviewing.

## Running the interviews

Customer-discovery pricing workshops provide the operating frame: run pricing interviews with early customers to validate the pricing strategy and understand true willingness to pay (w=0.388, weak backing) [https://www.glencoyne.com/guides/pricing-workshops-customer-discovery]. Structured B2B discovery formats run a defined question set across distinct phases, which keeps the 8th interview comparable to the 3rd (w=0.206, weak backing) [https://innovation-creativity.com/b2b-discovery-interview-15-questions-with-script/]. Validation frameworks additionally distinguish customer validation from discovery and enumerate the signals, including sales-led validation tests, that prove the validation has landed (w=0.321, weak backing) [https://www.koji.so/docs/customer-validation-guide].

## From interviews to the paid-pilot criterion

Interviews alone do not close a pricing-validity gate. The validation ladder runs internal validation, then interviews, then live sales tests (w=0.568, high backing) [https://www.willingnesstopay.com/resources/how-to-validate-new-saas-pricing-internal-validation-interviews-sales-test]. The live test is the paid pilot: a signed and invoiced contract at the proposed tier, running on the customer's own hardware, is the only evidence that WTP converts to revenue. The interview layer's job in the gate chain is to produce the named prospects and the verbatim WTP quotes that make the pilot predictable, and to leave an auditable record of who said what when the pilot decision is reviewed.

## Evidence artifacts

The gate's artifact set is: the transcript record with verbatim anonymized-or-consented quotes, the interview log with dates and prospect names, and a tracking item that moves to done exactly when the interview count criterion is met. Every quote in the record carries its source conversation, which is the same evidence discipline the audit and reference gates use: dated, attributable, re-verifiable.
