# 03. Tensor product periodic Fourier bases on flat coordinates

Scope: tensor product periodic Fourier bases on flat manifold coordinates: effective rank, degenerate zero columns, and span limits versus harmonic bases.

## The construction

Tensor product constructions are a standard way to build multivariate approximation bases: one paper considers an orthonormal basis generated by a tensor product of Fourier basis functions, half period cosine basis functions, and Chebyshev basis functions, applied to approximation problems in high dimensions on scattered points (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09174, weight 0.6577). When the underlying coordinates are angular and periodic, the natural building block is the periodic Fourier basis on each coordinate, combined by tensor product across coordinates.

A distinguishing property of Fourier bases is their frequency grid. The DFT basis is typically defined in terms of integral whole number frequencies, whereas a general Fourier basis for regression could use arbitrary frequency ratios (https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/249198/from-a-statistical-perspective-fourier-transform-vs-regression-with-fourier-bas, weak backing, weight 0.0427). This matters for span claims: a tensor product Fourier basis truncated at mode K contains only harmonics up to K on each coordinate, so any target needing mode K+1 on some coordinate is outside the span by construction.

## Effective rank and degenerate columns

A tensor product basis written as a design matrix can contain degenerate columns. The trigonometric identity sin(0 times x) equals 0 makes any such column identically zero, and zero multiplications in the tensor product can produce further zero columns; a design matrix with such columns has an effective rank below its nominal column count. This is not a hypothetical concern: ridge regression exists precisely because of collinearity among the columns of the design matrix. The ridge estimator deals with super collinearity among columns and employs a fix to resolve the (almost) singularity of X transpose X (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.09169, weight 0.7257), with Hoerl and Kennard credited for the 1970 formulation (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.09169, weight 0.7257). Ridge regression is also described as Tikhonov regularization for estimating coefficients in scenarios where variables are highly correlated (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_regression, weight 0.5468).

Ridge behavior is direction dependent: compared to least squares, ridge performs shrinkage, but not uniformly in all directions; it shrinks more along the principal component directions that correspond to low variance (https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/statlearn-s23/lectures/ridge.pdf, weight 0.7752). Standard practice is to interpret coefficient path plots as the tuning parameter lambda varies and to use a validation set to select lambda (https://cs229.stanford.edu/notes2021fall/lecture10-ridge-regression.pdf, weight 0.8009).

Conditioning of the design matrix is a first class benchmark quantity in its own right: regression with random feature matrices exhibits the double descent phenomenon, and that is an effect of the double descent behavior of the condition number (https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2110.11477/paper, weak backing, weight 0.1172).

## Fitting machinery

For nonlinear variants, the classical reference is NL2SOL, an adaptive nonlinear least squares algorithm catalogued in the ACM Collected Algorithms (https://netlib.org/toms/, weight 0.6251).

## Implications for benchmark design

1. Nominal column count overstates capacity. A tensor product basis with zero columns has an effective rank below its column count, and the ridge literature treats exactly this degeneracy as the problem regularization solves (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.09169, weight 0.7257). A benchmark comparing two arms should report effective rank, not nominal feature count.
2. Span truncation is the binding constraint, not conditioning. Ridge can redistribute weight across collinear columns (https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/statlearn-s23/lectures/ridge.pdf, weight 0.7752), but no regularization puts a missing harmonic into the span. A target whose required mode exceeds the truncation K is unreachable, and measured fit reflects only the reachable components.
3. Frequency grid choices are design decisions. Whether the basis uses integral frequencies only or arbitrary ratios changes what is in span (https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/249198/from-a-statistical-perspective-fourier-transform-vs-regression-with-fourier-bas, weak backing, weight 0.0427), so benchmark documentation should state the grid.
