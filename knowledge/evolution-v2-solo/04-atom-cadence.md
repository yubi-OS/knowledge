# 04. Single-action atom cadence (V7)

Scope: V7, the single-action atom cadence: at most one gated action per hourly cycle, a measured delta against a named ideal-state metric, a stay option, and a cumulative ledger that is monotone by construction.

## The rule

V7 proposes that each hourly machine cycle make at most one gated action. Every executed directive carries a measured delta, recorded as d_pre, d_post and delta, against a named ideal-state metric. A stay option always exists, so a cycle can legitimately decide to change nothing. The assertion delta >= 0 is made in code, as identity rather than measurement, and the cumulative ledger is monotone by construction. Blast radius per hour is one atom (source: the framing log, refs/evolution-v2-solo-2026-10-01.md).

The design borrows the commitment structure from behavior-change practice: commitment devices are tools that bridge the gap between a person's initial motivation to perform a behavior and the typical pattern of noncompliance as time goes on (https://besci.org/tactics/commitment-devices, weight 0.65, authoritative). Applied to a machine loop, the cadence itself is the commitment device: the hourly cycle commits the loop to at most one change, which caps both ambition and risk. Cadence-based practitioner writing makes the same framing for shipping work, though at blog grade (https://www.productivepatty.com/maximizing-productivity-using-commitment-devices-for-cadence-based-shipping, weight 0.32, weak). The engineering precedent is small-batch change control: an ACM Queue piece on commit discipline discusses when changes should be committed and criticizes large batches of unrelated changes (https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=1721964, weight 0.39, weak). Incremental improvement practice makes the risk argument directly: small process improvements are a pragmatic, low-risk path to meaningful and sustainable change (https://www.ssonetwork.com/continuous-improvement-process-improvement/columns/incremental-process-improvement, weight 0.29, weak).

## The ledger as the enforcement mechanism

The monotone ledger is what turns the cadence from a guideline into an invariant. Immutable audit log architecture defines the pattern: a cryptographically protected, append-only record of events or actions, structured so that once written, past entries cannot be altered or deleted (https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/immutable-audit-log, weight 0.26, weak). Applied to agent actions specifically, audit trail design for autonomous AI agents uses append-only stores, cryptographic chaining and verifiable records so an agent's actions can be replayed and checked (https://bitatlas.com/blog/agent-audit-logs-immutable-ledgers, weight 0.39, weak). Hash-chained append-only ledger implementations in relational databases show the same pattern as a concrete control surface for reconciliation audit trails (https://www.financialreconciliation.com/compliance-audit-trail-controls/immutable-append-only-ledger-design, weight 0.22, weak; https://www.financialreconciliation.com/compliance-audit-trail-controls/hash-chained-audit-logs-postgres, weight 0.36, weak). Append-only plus event-sourcing architecture references round out the pattern family (https://github.com/kb4ai/event-sourcing-append-log-immutable-architecture-pub-kb, weight 0.16, weak).

The framing log's contribution beyond these patterns is the combination: the ledger is not just tamper-evident after the fact, it is the thing that makes the assertion delta >= 0 an identity. If each entry carries d_pre and d_post and the cumulative sum is required monotone, an entry claiming a negative delta cannot be appended at all. Honesty is enforced by construction, not by review.

## Why one atom per cycle

The one-action cap is the blast radius control from the problem statement made operational. An hourly loop that can change several things per cycle multiplies the number of things that can be wrong at once and entangles causes. Capping at one atom means every outcome has exactly one candidate cause, and the ledger's delta measurement is attributable. The cost is throughput: the stress test of the finalist names this directly, atom cadence may starve multi-step work that legitimately needs several changes in one cycle (source: the framing log, see doc 07). The design answer, making multi-step work explicit multi-atom sequences, preserves the invariant at the price of explicitness.

## The ideal-state metric

Each atom's delta is measured against a named ideal-state metric. The log's open question acknowledges the metric choice is unresolved, with jev task success rate as the default anchor (source: the framing log). This is the same selection problem the calibration literature warns about: a scoring metric must be chosen so it measures what the loop claims, because a poorly chosen metric can be satisfied without the underlying behavior improving (https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/statlearn-s23/lectures/calibration.pdf, weight 0.85, authoritative, see doc 08).
