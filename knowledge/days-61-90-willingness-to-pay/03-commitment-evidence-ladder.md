# 03: The Commitment Evidence Ladder
Scope: The ladder of buyer commitment signals (letter of intent, paid pilot, paid deployment, renewal) and what each rung actually proves about willingness to pay.

## The ladder concept

Customer validation is structured as an evidence ladder: interviews and prototypes first, then commitments, paid pilots, repeat use, and scalable economics (100tasks.com/blog/customer-validation, w=0.35, weak backing). A parallel five-stage evidence ladder for climate tech names the rungs as patents, demonstrations, paid sales, renewals, and standardization (aisafetykorea.org/en/blog/climate-tech-evidence-ladder-patent-pilot-revenue-renewal-standardization, w=0.25, weak backing). Both ladders make the same structural claim: each rung reduces risk by a different amount, and the rungs are ordered by how much the buyer actually risks.

## What each rung proves

Opinion (interviews, surveys): the buyer says the problem is real. Proves interest, not willingness to pay. Commitment instruments (letter of intent, signed pilot scope, implementation call, written next step with the real buyer): the buyer spends political capital. Commitment tests should match the business model; in longer-cycle B2B sales this means a pilot scope, an implementation call, a letter of intent, or a written next step (dowhatmatter.com/guides/business-validation-plan, w=0.28, weak backing). Payment: the buyer spends real budget, the strongest pre-scale signal. Renewal: the buyer pays a second time after living with the product, which filters out novelty purchases.

## The paid pilot rung

The paid pilot is the first rung where budget moves. It is also the first rung where the seller can measure operations against real usage, which is why the days-61-90 plan attaches its entire measurement plan to a paid pilot and not to a LOI (session source doc, internal). But a paid pilot alone proves willingness to pay once, for a bounded scope, at a possibly discounted price. It does not prove repeatability or durability. That is what the renewal rung is for.

## Converting the pilot: pre-negotiate the next rung

The most operationally important finding in the dig: conversion should be pre-negotiated before the pilot starts. To convert a successful B2B pilot to a paid commercial contract, align at the beginning of the pilot on the expected annual contract value, contract term, and approximate signing timeline if the defined success criteria are met (b2blead.io/blog/b2b-pilot-program, w=0.27, weak backing). Structuring the pilot with defined success criteria, controlled scope, procurement preparation, and a pre-agreed conversion path is the practitioner-standard design (abovea.tech/insights-strategies/how-to-structure-paid-pilot-startup/, w=0.29, weak backing).

Without the pre-negotiated path, a successful pilot lands at the top of the ladder with no next rung: the customer has demonstrated WTP for a pilot price, but the seller has no committed mechanism to discover WTP for full deployment.

## What a LOI proves and what it does not

A letter of intent sits below payment on the ladder. It proves organizational intent and helps with board-level buy-in, but it is a documented intention, not budget allocation. Sources place LOIs as commitment instruments within validation plans rather than as evidence of payment behavior (dowhatmatter.com, w=0.28, weak backing). In the days-61-90 context, the design partner recruited in days 31-60 has presumably already expressed intent; the pilot's job is to move that partner from the intent rung to the payment rung.

## Renewal as the ceiling

Renewal is the only rung that measures satisfaction after the buyer has full information about the product's behavior in their environment. The five-stage ladders put renewals above paid sales precisely for this reason (aisafetykorea.org, w=0.25, weak backing). The days-61-90 plan's exit criteria stop at the day-90 decision; renewal evidence belongs to the phase after it, which is why the decision framework (doc 07) treats a second pilot, not a renewal, as the proceed outcome.
