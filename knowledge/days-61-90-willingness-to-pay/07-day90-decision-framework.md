# 07: The Day-90 Decision Framework
Scope: The four-path end-of-phase decision (proceed to second pilot, narrow the offer, change target segment, pause commercial hiring) and the evidence threshold that selects each.

## Decide from the readout, not before it

The days-61-90 plan refuses to pre-select an outcome: the decision is made from the actual day-90 readout, not predicted in advance (session source doc, internal). The general literature agrees that pilots fail not because the technology was unsuitable but because the pilot was never designed to produce a decision, and prescribes decision gates and milestone checkpoints as part of the pilot design (tractiontechnology.com/blog/how-to-run-a-successful-pilot-with-a-startup-frameworks-kpis, w=0.30, weak backing). The four-path framework is that decision gate, placed at day 90.

## The four paths and their evidence thresholds

Path 1, proceed to a second pilot: metrics met or exceeded the ROI baseline and the customer wants to continue (session source doc, internal). The evidence threshold is both-sided: measured metrics against the pre-registered worksheet, plus the customer's own stated intent. Neither alone suffices; good numbers with a reluctant customer means the engagement was mis-scoped, not that the offer scales.

Path 2, narrow the offer: the pilot succeeded technically but revealed the offer scope was too broad, for example support load concentrated in one area (session source doc, internal). The instrument that detects this is the root-cause categorization of the support log (doc 04). Narrowing is a legitimate success outcome: it converts pilot evidence into a sharper offer.

Path 3, change the target segment: the pilot customer profile did not match well, a recruitment mismatch from days 31-60 (session source doc, internal). The signal is good product behavior with poor economics or poor fit: the customer got value, but the operational burden or the price does not transfer to that segment's profile.

Path 4, pause commercial hiring: the pilot revealed the product is not yet ready for a second paying customer, for example B-VM-CTAP2-adjacent issues resurfacing in the field, or unsustainable support load at 25-50 nodes (session source doc, internal). Pausing is the honest outcome when the readout shows the product, not the offer, is the constraint.

## Pre-registered kill criteria

The strongest practitioner consensus in this dig: write the kill criteria down before the pilot starts. One page covering the workflow and its single accountable owner, the measured baseline, the success threshold that triggers a scale decision, the eval set, the permission line, and the two fields every team resists, the kill criteria and the extend conditions (gaiforbusiness.com/blog/ai-pilots-kill-criteria/, w=0.40, weak backing). A pilot that begins life with a pre-registered threshold can be killed without a fight, because the decision was pre-made (burakgaliba.com/blog/kill-your-ai-pilots, w=0.29, weak backing). The days-61-90 plan implements this by fixing the ROI baseline worksheet before the pilot and requiring the decision to reference it (session source doc, internal).

## Governance cadence

Keep, kill, and scale decisions belong on a fixed review cadence; at every quarterly review the board should require management to state which pilots are being kept, which are being extended under a named condition, and which are being killed with capital reallocated (stefanfinch.com/articles/when-to-kill-an-ai-pilot, w=0.34, weak backing). Ninety-day evaluation cycles are the standard rhythm for separating hype from results (padiso.co/blog/ship-or-kill-framework-ai-pilots/, w=0.36, weak backing). The days-61-90 phase is exactly one such cycle, and the day-90 decision is the terminal event of that cycle.

## Recording the decision

The exit criteria require one of the four decisions made and recorded (session source doc, internal). Recording matters because the decision feeds the next phase's plan and the OMN-79 revisit; an unrecorded decision re-litigates itself at every future gate. The record should name the path, the readout numbers that selected it, and the named condition if any pilot work is being extended rather than killed (stefanfinch.com, w=0.34, weak backing).
