# Design partner and early customer recruitment in the first 90 days

Scope: sourcing channels, warm outreach, recruitment cadence, and the commitment a design partner signs up for.

## What a design partner is

Andreessen Horowitz defines design partners as "the first few users of a company's software", and calls them "a key part of the early software development process" because they "provide valuable feedback on everything from product functionality to user experience, to pricing and packaging" (weight 0.50, a16z.com/a-framework-for-finding-a-design-partner/). The definition carries the commitment: a design partner is an early user who gives structured feedback across product and commercial dimensions, not a passive trial account.

A dedicated playbook guide covers the operational side: what design partners are, "how to recruit them, how to structure the commercial relationship, and how to run continu[ous]" collaboration (weight 0.35, weak backing, www.koji.so/docs/design-partner-program). The mention of a structured commercial relationship is the load-bearing detail: recruitment conversations should decide up front what the partner commits to (feedback cadence, references, possibly paid terms), because that is what distinguishes a design partner from a free pilot.

## Discovery interviews as the recruitment funnel

Recruitment runs on discovery conversations. A founder guide recommends that early-stage founders "run their first 10-20 discovery interviews live", arguing "the real-time depth is worth the scheduling effort" at that stage (weight 0.51, www.koji.so/blog/customer-discovery-interviews-startup-guide). The cadence implication for a 90-day plan is concrete: roughly 10 to 20 live interviews is the scale of funnel activity expected before design-partner selection, not a handful of calls.

Interview content is well-covered ground. A Lean B2B author consolidated "all the questions B2B entrepreneurs need to answer during the problem interview phase when doing early B2B customer discovery" into a master list (weight 0.46, weak backing, leanb2bbook.com/blog/b2b-customer-discovery-interview-questions-the-master-list/). A buying-committee guide extends this to B2B structure: run discovery "across buying committees", covering "engineers, procurement, C-suite", and triangulate evidence across those roles (weight 0.32, weak backing, lindeninnovation.com/customer-discovery-interviews-b2b/). Triangulation matters because a single-role conversation systematically misreads the buying decision.

## Sourcing and the warm-outreach reality

The surveyed material is thin on specific sourcing channels and does not endorse any single playbook for finding partners; the a16z framework focuses on selection criteria and fit rather than channel lists (weight 0.50, a16z.com/a-framework-for-finding-a-design-partner/). This gap is itself a finding for operational plans: outreach plans that reference a founder's network by name ("Jenny's network") without channels, targets, artifacts, and day targets are not a sourced plan. The workable skeleton is a table with channel, target, outreach artifact, day target, and owner per row, filled in before the recruitment window opens; leaving it unfilled past the early days of the plan is the failure mode to guard against.

A practical minimum bar used in venture plans is: at least two named warm conversations with a documented next step before the middle phase begins. That bar is consistent with the live-interview cadence above (10 to 20 interviews) filtered down to serious candidates.

## Cadence and commitments over the 90 days

Synthesizing the sources, a workable recruitment cadence inside a 90-day plan is:

1. Days 0-30: run the bulk of live discovery interviews (target 10-20), using a problem-interview question list and covering buying-committee roles (weight 0.51, koji.so; weight 0.46, weak backing, leanb2bbook.com; weight 0.32, weak backing, lindeninnovation.com).
2. Days 31-60: select and close 2 or more design partners with a structured commercial relationship defined at signature (weight 0.35, weak backing, koji.so/docs/design-partner-program).
3. Days 61-90: run the partner engagement as the pilot feedback source, feeding the end-of-window decision.

## Practical takeaways

- Treat design partners as structured early users giving feedback on product and pricing, with the commercial relationship written down (weight 0.50, a16z.com; weight 0.35, weak backing, koji.so).
- Run discovery interviews live, at a volume of 10-20 in the early phase (weight 0.51, koji.so).
- Cover the buying committee, not one role, and triangulate evidence across roles (weight 0.32, weak backing, lindeninnovation.com).
- Do not accept an unsourced network reference as an outreach plan; require channel, target, artifact, day target, and owner per row.
