# Day-90 outcome selection: proceed, narrow, pivot, pause

Scope: the four-way end-of-window decision (proceed, narrow, change segment, pause), the metrics that inform it, and pilot ROI readouts.

## The decision is the pilot's product

A pilot exists to produce a decision. A pilot-project guide frames it as moving "from assumptions to evidence", providing "quantitative and qualitative data to help you decide whether to proceed, pivot, or ..." (weight 0.42, weak backing, monday.com/blog/project-management/pilot-project/). An AI-pilot guide describes the end state explicitly: the phase "culminates in a clear decision: proceed to scaling, iterate with another pilot phase, pivot to a different approach, or stop", adding that "each outcome represents valid" learning (weight 0.14, weak backing, www.businessplusai.com/blog/the-ai-pilot-program-testing-before-committing-to-full-scale-implementatio). The four-way framing (proceed, iterate or narrow, pivot, stop or pause) is the standard outcome vocabulary.

An evaluation framework guide echoes it: the final evaluation exists to "compare results, gather feedback, and align on scale, redesign, or closeout" (weight 0.22, weak backing, www.incurvo.com/post-pilot-evaluation-decision-framework). Scale maps to proceed, redesign to narrow or pivot, closeout to pause.

## The decision must be pre-structured

The four-way choice should not be improvised at day 90. Pilot-structuring guidance says to "set success criteria, pick the right team, and make confident decisions" as part of program design, before results exist (weight 0.17, weak backing, successknocks.com/how-to-structure-a-pilot-program-for-enterprise-software/). Framework-level guidance provides structured resources "covering practical frameworks and actionable strategies" for pilot programs (weight 0.28, weak backing, www.lliconsultinggroup.com/static/resources/pilot-program-framework). The pattern: the outcome menu, the metrics, and the thresholds are defined when the pilot is designed, so the day-90 meeting selects among pre-committed options.

## Evidence discipline: use evaluation while it runs, not only after

Formal evaluation guidance emphasizes continuous use of findings. An Australian Treasury evaluation guide provides "step-by-step instructions for each stage, including advice on how evaluation findings can be used while you run the pilot and after it finishes" (weight 0.85, evaluation.treasury.gov.au/sites/evaluation.treasury.gov.au/files/2025-07/guide-evaluating-pilot-progr). The strongest-weighted source in this corpus's dig, it reframes the day-90 decision as the end of an evaluation process that has been feeding the plan throughout, rather than a single terminal judgment on anecdote.

For generative-AI pilots, the same discipline appears as KPI practice: set "strict KPI baselines" and run "rigorous post-launch reviews" before scaling, with the explicit warning about high enterprise failure rates when this is skipped (weight 0.20, weak backing, scc-comets.com/pilot-to-scale-in-generative-ai-kpi-baselines-and-post-launch-reviews). Baselines measured before the pilot started are what make the day-90 comparison meaningful.

## The ROI readout

The end-of-window readout should connect pilot evidence to economics, not just usage. The surveyed material supports the components: quantitative and qualitative data from the pilot itself (weight 0.42, weak backing, monday.com), pre-registered KPI baselines and post-launch review (weight 0.20, weak backing, scc-comets.com), and a structured comparison of results against the framework decided in advance (weight 0.22, weak backing, incurvo.com). A confidential readout format that pairs measured metrics with a revenue-and-cost model gives the decision-makers both halves: did the pilot work, and does the economics support the next commitment.

One caution the sources support: do not pre-select the outcome. Decision integrity requires the readout to feed a genuinely open choice among the four options; a plan that announces "proceed" before the metrics exist has converted its day-90 decision point into a presentation, the failure mode doc 03 warns about.

## Practical takeaways

- Design the four-way outcome menu (proceed, narrow or iterate, pivot, pause) when the pilot is designed, not at day 90 (weight 0.17, weak backing, successknocks.com; weight 0.14, weak backing, businessplusai.com).
- Record KPI baselines before the pilot starts so the post-pilot comparison is measurable (weight 0.20, weak backing, scc-comets.com).
- Use evaluation findings continuously during the pilot, not only at the end (weight 0.85, evaluation.treasury.gov.au).
- End the window with a structured readout: measured metrics plus economics, feeding a genuinely open choice among the four outcomes (weight 0.42, weak backing, monday.com; weight 0.22, weak backing, incurvo.com).
