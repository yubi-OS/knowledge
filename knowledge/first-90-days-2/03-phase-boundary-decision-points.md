# Decision points at phase boundaries: go/no-go checkpoints

Scope: how teams structure go/no-go checkpoints, what inputs a decision point takes, and what to do when the gate is not met on schedule.

## A go/no-go decision is a governance event, not a meeting

The clearest statement in the surveyed material: "A Go / No-Go decision is not a presentation. It is a structured governance event with defined criteria, decision rights, and consequences" (weight 0.46, weak backing, turn8.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/D1_How-to-Design-and-Run-a-Go-or-No-Go-Decision-Framework.pdf). The same source defines the success shape as "a Phase Gate Scorecard with pre-defined criteria, a structured review conducted with all required decision-makers", and archived phase deliverables (weight 0.36, weak backing, turn8.co/knowledge-hub/validation-design/how-to-design-and-run-a-go-or-no-go-decision-framework/).

Three inputs follow from that definition:

1. Pre-defined criteria fixed before the review, so the decision cannot be re-scoped mid-meeting.
2. Decision rights: who may say go or no-go, named in advance.
3. Consequences: what a no-go actually triggers, also named in advance.

Gate reviews operate the same way. A gate review is "a checkpoint that governs whether the project can proceed or not" and a "formal governance step" that confirms required processes and tasks were completed (weight 0.44, weak backing, monday.com/blog/project-management/gate-review/). A methodology guide for phase transitions requires teams to "formally evaluate whether success criteria for the current phase have been met, and make an explicit GO/NO-GO decision for phase transition" (weight 0.31, weak backing, tsmgeo.com/principles/).

## What the decision evaluates

The evaluation target is criteria satisfaction, not effort or enthusiasm. Phase-gate guidance stresses that "the gates handle funding and go/no-go decisions" separately from the delivery teams doing the work (weight 0.32, weak backing, simpliaxis.com/resources/phase-gate-in-project-management). Checklist-based practice operationalizes this: launch checklists enumerate "critical criteria to be met before launching a product, feature, or release" (weight 0.35, weak backing, assets-global.website-files.com/643daae49c6d183c6cefe3b7/6451a552a2facf30669af47e_Go%20No-Go%20Checklist.pdf), and production-readiness checklists assess both technical and organizational readiness before deployment (weight 0.37, weak backing, instituteprojectmanagement.com/blog/go-no-go-production-readiness-checklist/).

Checklists are also broadened across functions. One product-ops template is a "62-item cross-functional go/no-go checklist covering engineering, marketing, support, sales, and legal sign-off" (weight 0.27, weak backing, www.scaleproductops.com/templates/launch-readiness-checklist), and a SaaS launch guide frames the checklist as "a shared readiness system" connecting strategy, customer evidence, quality, go-to-market execution, ownership, risk, and rollout (weight 0.20, weak backing, selleo.com/blog/product-launch-checklist).

## Timing the checkpoint relative to the date

A practical scheduling rule from launch practice: "Confirm the launch date with product and engineering, and set a go/no-go checkpoint two weeks out" (weight 0.50, tented.ai/blog/product-launch-checklist). Setting the checkpoint before the critical date gives the team room to fix or defer without a same-day decision. In a 90-day plan this converts to: put the go/no-go review at a fixed offset before the phase boundary, not on the boundary itself.

## When the gate is not met on schedule

The surveyed material supports two behaviors when criteria are unmet at the checkpoint date:

- Do not lower the criteria to pass the gate. Because criteria were pre-defined with decision rights and consequences (weight 0.46, weak backing, turn8.co PDF), changing them at the boundary converts a governance event into a presentation, which is precisely what the framework forbids.
- Do not silently absorb the slip. The structured-review discipline requires "all Phase Two deliverables archived and access[ible]" (weight 0.36, weak backing, turn8.co), meaning a failed gate leaves an auditable record; the follow-on phase planning then starts from a documented miss rather than a faded baseline.

## Practical takeaways

- Write the scorecard, the decision rights, and the consequences before the first decision point; a go/no-go without consequences is a status update (weight 0.46, weak backing, turn8.co PDF).
- Make the decision explicit and binary at each boundary, based on success criteria for the completed phase (weight 0.31, weak backing, tsmgeo.com/principles/).
- Evaluate against cross-functional readiness evidence, not a single function's report (weight 0.27, weak backing, scaleproductops.com; weight 0.20, weak backing, selleo.com).
- Place the checkpoint a fixed interval before the boundary date, as launch practice does with a two-week go/no-go (weight 0.50, tented.ai).
