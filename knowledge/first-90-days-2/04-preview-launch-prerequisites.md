# Technical preview and pilot launch prerequisites

Scope: what must be true before external users touch a pre-release product, entry criteria, private beta versus technical preview.

## What a preview program is for

Vendor documentation is explicit that pre-release programs exist to obtain customer feedback. Microsoft's preview terms define previews as "preview, beta, or other prerelease features, services, software, or regions offered by Microsoft to obtain customer feedback" (weight 0.93, learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/licensing-preview-info). That framing sets the core prerequisite test: if the product cannot yet generate usable feedback from external users, it is not ready for any preview cohort.

## Evidence the preview follows prior validation

Mature delivery frameworks sequence previews behind validated discovery. The UK Government Service Manual's beta phase guidance states that by the beta stage the team will "have developed a strong understanding of your users' needs and, by the end of alpha, chosen a way of meeting those needs", so beta work concentrates on hardening the chosen approach (weight 0.83, www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-beta-phase-works). Applied to a 90-day plan, this makes user-need evidence an entry prerequisite, not a parallel track: a preview launched without prior need validation will produce feedback about the wrong thing.

Marketplace rules show that preview access itself can carry formal gatekeeping: Microsoft's game store requires that "Microsoft must approve all beta products and validate them for Submission Checks" (weight 0.75, learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/gdk/docs/store/policies/certification-requirements-beta-game-preview). The generalizable point is that preview entry can be a reviewed gate with validation steps, not a self-serve toggle.

## Containing the cohort

Private beta practice constrains exposure deliberately. A private beta strategy guide describes opening the product "to a curated group of users under invite, access codes, or a manual approval step", with a cohort "small enough that founders can read every" piece of feedback (weight 0.12, weak backing, www.stackmatix.com/blog/private-beta-launch-strategy). For a venture plan, the containment rule converts into a prerequisite: an access-control mechanism (invites, codes, or manual approval) must exist before day one of the preview.

## Pilot programs versus previews

A pilot and a preview are different commitments. An enterprise software selection playbook calls the proof of concept "the most misunderstood step in enterprise software selection", noting some teams treat it as an insurance policy rather than a structured test (weight 0.28, weak backing, umbrex.com/resources/enterprise-software-selection-playbook/proof-of-concept-and-pilot/). A pilot-structuring guide directs teams to "set success criteria, pick the right team, and make confident decisions" before the pilot starts (weight 0.17, weak backing, successknocks.com/how-to-structure-a-pilot-program-for-enterprise-software/).

The distinction that matters for a 90-day plan: a technical preview asks external users to try the product and give feedback; a paid pilot asks a customer to run the product under success criteria that produce a decision. They require different prerequisites, and a plan that collapses them will treat feedback as validation.

## Building the prerequisite list

The surveyed material supports a five-item minimum prerequisite list for opening a technical preview:

1. A stated purpose of obtaining customer feedback, aligned with vendor practice for preview programs (weight 0.93, learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/licensing-preview-info).
2. Documented user-need evidence from an earlier phase, per the alpha-before-beta sequence (weight 0.83, www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-beta-phase-works).
3. Any required external validation or approval steps completed before access is granted (weight 0.75, learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/gdk/docs/store/policies/certification-requirements-beta-game-preview).
4. A cohort-containment mechanism: invites, access codes, or manual approval (weight 0.12, weak backing, www.stackmatix.com/blog/private-beta-launch-strategy).
5. Written success criteria and a named decision process if the engagement is a paid pilot rather than a preview (weight 0.17, weak backing, successknocks.com).

Formal entry-criteria documents from the venture's own governance should take precedence over this generic list; this corpus documents the generic floor, not the specific plan.
