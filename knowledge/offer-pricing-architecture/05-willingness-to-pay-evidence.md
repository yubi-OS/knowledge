# 05. Willingness-to-pay evidence

**Scope:** gathering willingness-to-pay evidence before it exists: design partners at list price, substitution tests, behavioral buying signals versus survey answers.

## Why WTP evidence is hard before a market exists

Academic work is blunt about the reliability problem: measurement of willingness to pay for innovations often yields biased results, and research investigates the consumer-related characteristics and motives underlying that bias [source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372639024_Willingness_to_Pay_Measurement_Approaches) (jev weight 0.61). A second review frames the stakes: knowledge about a product's willingness-to-pay plays a crucial role in pricing decisions and new product development [source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242382759_A_Review_of_Methods_for_Measuring_Willingness-to-Pay) (jev weight 0.59). For an early-stage infrastructure product the implication is that stated-preference surveys are a weak instrument, and behavioral evidence has to be engineered on purpose.

## Survey methods and their limits

The two standard survey methods are Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger. The Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter is a survey-based method that uses four standard questions to identify the price points customers perceive as too cheap, a good value, expensive, and too expensive, producing an acceptable price range [source](https://www.surveymonkey.com/market-research/resources/van-westendorp-price-sensitivity-meter/) (jev weight 0.42, weak backing). Descriptions of the method agree on the four-question structure and its output of acceptable ranges [source](https://www.surveyking.com/help/van-westendorp-analysis) (jev weight 0.42, weak backing). The Gabor-Granger method estimates willingness to pay by testing price acceptance directly, iterating price points against purchase intent [source](https://www.surveyking.com/help/gabor-granger) (jev weight 0.34, weak backing).

These methods measure stated perception. They are useful for bounding a range before customer conversations, but they cannot substitute for a transaction, because the bias documented above applies most strongly to innovations where respondents have no experience to anchor on.

## Behavioral evidence instruments

The behavioral ladder runs from talk to money. B2B validation guidance describes five methods that produce progressively more reliable signals: design partners, letters of intent, paid pilots, cold outreach interviews, and conference validation, testing commitment from verbal interest through to revenue [source](https://www.userintuition.ai/reference-guides/b2b-idea-validation-cold-outreach-to-signal/) (jev weight 0.29, weak backing). At the top of that ladder sit the instruments with actual payment.

Design partners are the first such instrument. Andreessen Horowitz's framework describes design partners as the first few users who provide feedback on everything from product functionality to user experience, to pricing and packaging [source](https://a16z.com/a-framework-for-finding-a-design-partner/) (jev weight 0.72). Commercial structure varies: some design partners sign forward contracts as a commitment to the future product, while the decision to enter such contracts depends on the startup's situation [source](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/navigating-path-design-partners-startups-guide-vinay-chandrasekaran-rqgfc) (jev weight 0.35, weak backing).

## The substitution test

The sharpest behavioral instrument for a bundle offer is the substitution test: rather than asking whether customers would pay for a pre-flashed bundle, observe whether they buy the bundle or perform the substitute actions themselves (buying the bare component and doing the configuration work directly). The test is behavioral by construction, and its result is binary. A related survey answer ("would you buy this?") is not a substitute for it, per the bias findings above.

## Evidence rules for unvalidated pricing

Three rules make WTP evidence usable in a pricing architecture:

1. Label the instrument. Every pricing claim states whether it rests on a transaction, a pilot commitment, a survey, or nothing.
2. Prefer list-price behavior. A design partner paying list price for a small deployment outranks any discount-negotiated survey response.
3. Count revenue-grade events only. Signed contracts and paid pilots are evidence; expressions of interest are not, even though the commitment ladder acknowledges verbal interest as an early signal [source](https://www.userintuition.ai/reference-guides/b2b-idea-validation-cold-outreach-to-signal/) (jev weight 0.29, weak backing).
