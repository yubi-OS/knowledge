# 04. Pricing hypothesis discipline

**Scope:** treating unvalidated prices as falsifiable hypotheses: naming validation and invalidation criteria, paid pilots, and refusing to present guesses as facts.

## The assumption-testing frame

The business-design literature formalizes this: Testing Business Ideas integrates assumptions mapping with lean startup-style experiments on top of the Business Model Canvas and Value Proposition Canvas, so that ideas are tested through an experiment library before large commitments are made [source](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/testing-business-ideas-book) (jev weight 0.60). Applied to pricing, every number in an unvalidated price list is an assumption with the same structure: a stated expectation, a test that could falsify it, and an evidence threshold that converts it into a commitment.

Pricing research practice supports the same discipline. A pricing research case study describes an enterprise SaaS product team revamping a price structure as a structured research engagement rather than a debate [source](http://glg.it/case-studies/pricing-research/) (jev weight 0.33, weak backing). A/B price testing guides describe the loop as defining objectives, running the test, analyzing results, and making data-driven decisions [source](https://payproglobal.com/how-to/run-a-b-price-testing-for-saas/) (jev weight 0.31, weak backing).

## What makes a price hypothesis testable

A price hypothesis needs three named parts before any customer contact:

1. The number or shape (for example, per-device recurring tiered by fleet size).
2. The validation criterion, stated as an observable event (a design partner pays list price for a small fleet without a discount that erases the tier logic).
3. The invalidation criterion, stated equally observably (every prospect asks for a discount larger than the tier step, or none will sign before an unrelated gate closes).

The general validation literature backs the frame: startup validation guides describe testing concepts, gathering customer feedback, and confirming market fit before launch [source](https://www.hubspot.com/startups/scaling-smarter/how-to-validate-startup-idea) (jev weight 0.40, weak backing), and pricing is defined simply as the process whereby a business sets and displays the price at which it will sell its products and services [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pricing) (jev weight 0.55), a process that can be run as an experiment rather than an announcement.

## Paid pilots as the instrument

The paid pilot is the standard instrument when the product is not generally available. A practitioner guide to paid pilots walks through how to design, price, negotiate, and convert a paid pilot program, with pricing benchmarks, contract language, and lessons drawn from pilots that worked and pilots that did not [source](https://techhubcore.com/paid-pilots-for-startups/) (jev weight 0.39, weak backing). The design point that matters for hypothesis discipline is that a paid pilot differs from a free pilot precisely because payment is the signal being measured: an unpaid expression of interest is a survey answer, a paid engagement is behavior.

## What discipline forbids

Three prohibitions follow:

1. No unmarked numbers. A price stated without its hypothesis status reads as a commitment; an early-stage price list should label every figure as a hypothesis with its test.
2. No directional framing dressed as data. Absent fetched market data, a price can carry directional reasoning but must not borrow the authority of evidence it does not have.
3. No silent reclassification. When a test fails (a prospect will not sign at list), the hypothesis is updated or dropped, not renamed. The commitment ladder in B2B validation runs from verbal interest through to revenue, and only revenue-grade events count as validation [source](https://www.userintuition.ai/reference-guides/b2b-idea-validation-cold-outreach-to-signal/) (jev weight 0.29, weak backing).

## Recording the discipline

The discipline is auditable only if it is written down: each offer carries its hypothesis text, its validation and invalidation criteria, the pilot that will run the test, and the date the hypothesis was last revised. This turns a price list into an experiment log and keeps later pricing decisions anchored to what was actually tested rather than what was hoped.
