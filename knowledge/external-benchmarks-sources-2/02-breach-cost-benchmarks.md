# Breach cost benchmarks

Scope: cost of data breach benchmarks (IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025, phishing as initial attack vector) as directional status quo cost evidence, with annual refresh cadence.

## The primary source

The anchor source is IBM's Cost of a Data Breach series, published with the Ponemon Institute. The 2025 edition is documented by IBM directly: the X Force summary states the global average breach cost dropped to USD 4.44 million, down from USD 4.88 million the prior year, a 9 percent decrease (https://www.ibm.com/think/x-force/2025-cost-of-a-data-breach-navigating-ai, weight 0.9159, primary). The report PDF carries the same figure and attributes the decline to faster identification and containment (https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:75bd923e-e263-40c0-b62b-4b43da492394/original/as/cost-of-a-data-breach-report-2025.pdf, weight 0.8774, primary). IBM's insights page confirms the study covered 600 breached organizations (https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/data-matters/cost-of-a-data-breach, weight 0.8859, primary).

The same 2025 PDF notes that where an attacker used AI against an organization, including through phishing, the average breach cost was around USD 4.49 million, close to the overall average (https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:607b9590-38e0-4c91-b433-aa8a17f5b5e8/original/as/cost-of-a-data-breach-report-2025.pdf, weight 0.8859, primary). The report landing page is the canonical citation URL (https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach, weight 0.6584, primary; https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach?page=all, weight 0.7324, primary).

## The phishing vector figure

The register's specific claim is the cost of a breach where phishing was the initial attack vector. An industry tracker aggregating the IBM 2025 data states phishing attacks cost an average of USD 4.8 million and took 254 days to contain (https://databreachcost.com/attack-vector/phishing, weight 0.6372, moderately backed; it is an aggregator of the IBM report rather than the report itself, so treat it as a pointer to verify against the PDF). A security vendor blog drawing on the same report argues the inbox remains the entry point for the most common and damaging breaches, with phishing among the top initial vectors (https://abnormal.ai/blog/ibm-cost-of-a-data-breach-2025, weakly backed, weight 0.3686).

The register's own boundary applies here: the 4.8 million dollar figure supports a directional argument that credential phishing incidents are expensive industry wide. It does not support a claim that any specific customer would avoid that dollar figure. That requires the customer's own baseline data in an ROI model, not a benchmark average.

## Refresh signal found in this pass

A material refresh fact surfaced: IBM has already released a 2026 edition. The report page now titles as Cost of a Data Breach Report 2026 (https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach, weight 0.6584, primary), and the 2026 PDF states the study covered 602 organizations breached between March 2025 and February 2026 (https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:21111142-1251-4369-86fb-57b82f5bb108/original/as/cost-of-a-data-breach-report-2026.pdf, weight 0.8199, primary). This means the register's 2025 citation is already one edition behind as of this mint. The register's stated cadence, refresh when the next edition appears, has therefore been triggered, and any external facing document citing the 4.8 million dollar phishing figure should be re-pointed at the 2026 edition before the next use.

## What the benchmark is for

The purpose of the breach cost benchmark is to give pricing and market framing documents a citable, dated, externally sourced number for the cost of the status quo, the world where phishing-driven credential compromise continues unchecked. It is the cheapest form of honest motivation available: it requires no claim about the product, only about the industry baseline. Its failure mode, guarded against by the register, is the silent slide from "breaches involving phishing are expensive industry wide" to "our product saves customers 4.8 million dollars," which the source does not support.

## Weak spots

The phishing vector breakdown is secondhand in this pass: the 4.8 million figure reached the register through an aggregator and a vendor blog, both drawing on the IBM PDF, and the exact per-vector table should be confirmed directly in the primary PDF at next refresh. The aggregator page carried weight 0.6372, which clears the 0.5 authoritative threshold, but the underlying PDF pages carry higher weights and should be the citation of record.
