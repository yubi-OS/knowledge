## FCS: the identity and the per-degree lifetime

Fluorescence correlation spectroscopy is a statistical analysis, via time correlation, of stationary fluctuations of fluorescence intensity [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence_correlation_spectroscopy, weight 0.08, weak backing]. Applied literature confirms FCS measures rotational diffusion of macromolecules and presents pulsed-interleaved excitation schemes for it [sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0009261411008244, weight 0.95]. Reviews document FCS as a routine method for diffusion coefficients, chemical rate constants, molecular concentrations, and fluorescence brightness [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3244056, weight 0.89; frontiersin.org/journals/physics, weight 0.93]. Rotational diffusion tensors are determined experimentally through fluorescence anisotropy, dielectric spectroscopy, NMR relaxation, and related methods [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_diffusion, weight 0.09, weak backing].

The synthesis document's identity claim: the rotational-diffusion correlation on the sphere, C_l(t) = exp(-l(l+1) D_r t), is exactly the program's heat-kernel defocus E_l(t) = E_l(0) exp(-2l(l+1)t) with D_r = 2. The defocus operator is the rotational-diffusion propagator, zero new parameters. It follows that there is no single characteristic time: the lifetime is per spherical degree, tau_l = 1/(2l(l+1)). The document notes the program's two existing angular-resolution estimates (L = 3 harmonic truncation and the 0.095 sparse-cell chordal radius) should agree to order of magnitude but currently do not because their time units have never been tied. That unit reconciliation is the document's cheapest and most prerequisite open follow-up.

## Triplet kinetics and the first-order identity

Triplet dynamics underlie both room-temperature phosphorescence and thermally activated delayed fluorescence; recent work reports model emitters with simultaneous delayed fluorescence and phosphorescence components [nature.com/articles/s41377-025-02063-x, weight 0.96; pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12756230, weight 0.89]. The prompt/delayed intensity split I(t) = A_p exp(-pt) + A_d exp(-dt) corresponds to the two-block spectral truncation the program already measures, with delayed emission carrying the low-degree spectral mass and prompt emission the high-degree mass.
