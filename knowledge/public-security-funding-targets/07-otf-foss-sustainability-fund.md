# Open Technology Fund and the FOSS Sustainability Fund

Scope: Open Technology Fund and its FOSS sustainability work: mission framing (internet freedom, at-risk users), funding cycles, eligibility, and fit limits for general security projects.

## What OTF funds

The Open Technology Fund describes itself as supporting internet freedom worldwide. Its primary vehicle is the Internet Freedom Fund: "The Internet Freedom Fund (IFF) is OTF's primary way to support projects advancing Internet freedom for users in repressive environments. Through the Internet Freedom Fund, we support development, research, and implementation projects creating or promoting the use of tools that allow users, including journalists, human rights defenders, activists, and regular people, to circumvent Internet [censorship]" (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/internet-freedom-fund, weight 0.75). OTF's main site adds targeted rapid-response instruments, such as urgent digital security support during "a clear, time-sensitive, state-sponsored digital emergency" and user-cost support for "large-scale, open source VPN or anti-censorship tool[s] that work in repressive information contexts" (https://www.opentech.fund/, weight 0.59). The IFF page on the main site confirms it is "the primary opportunity through which Open Technology Fund supports innovative global internet freedom projects," including technology development, applied research, convenings, and digital security projects (https://www.opentech.fund/funds/internet-freedom-fund/, weight 0.49).

## The FOSS Sustainability Fund

OTF's Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) Sustainability Fund has its own distinct thesis: "For the continued iteration and innovation of the open source internet freedom tech stack, there are foundational requirements around maintenance, quality documentation, interoperability, and reproducibility that must be met by projects committed to long-term sustainability and resilience" (https://www.opentech.fund/funds/free-and-open-source-software-sustainability-fund/, weight 0.65).

The application guidebook is explicit about the funding model: "Rather than providing ongoing core funding, this Fund invests in a project's current and future sustainability. We pay special attention to applications that emphasize activities investing in a project's long-term stability," and it asks whether "the proposed effort have plans for sustainability beyond OTF support" (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/free-and-open-source-software-foss-sustainability-fund, weight 0.75).

Three structural requirements follow from those two sources:

1. The deliverable must strengthen the open source internet freedom tech stack, not a general-purpose security project.
2. The proposal must name maintenance, documentation, interoperability, and reproducibility work, because those are the fund's stated foundational requirements (https://www.opentech.fund/funds/free-and-open-source-software-sustainability-fund/, weight 0.65).
3. The application must show a sustainability path beyond OTF support; one-off grants with no exit story are explicitly deprioritized (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/free-and-open-source-software-foss-sustainability-fund, weight 0.75).

## Cycles and re-verification discipline

This dig surfaced the funds' standing criteria pages but no currently open 2026 solicitation window in the collected material. OTF funds run on solicitation cycles that open and close on their own calendar, so the deadline state must be read from docs.opentech.fund immediately before any application decision. The authoritative surfaces to check are the guidebook pages for the FOSS Sustainability Fund (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/free-and-open-source-software-foss-sustainability-fund, weight 0.75) and the Internet Freedom Fund (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/internet-freedom-fund, weight 0.75).

## Fit assessment: conditional by design

OTF's mission framing is narrower than "security." The funded user is the person in a repressive environment: journalists, human rights defenders, activists (https://docs.opentech.fund/otf-application-guidebook/our-funds-and-fellowships/internet-freedom-fund, weight 0.75). A general security-hardening project with no named at-risk-user constituency does not map to that mission, and forcing a mapping would be exactly the kind of funder-theme contortion a screening process exists to prevent.

The fit test, applied strictly:

1. PASS only if a specific, named use case for at-risk users exists in the project's own work, for example authentication or secure-communication tooling whose threat model is built around journalists or activists, with a public deliverable scoped to that constituency.
2. FAIL if the honest framing is "we build security infrastructure and some at-risk users might benefit." That framing is a generic security pitch wearing an internet-freedom costume, and the fund's own criteria (sustainability of the internet freedom tech stack, https://www.opentech.fund/funds/free-and-open-source-software-sustainability-fund/, weight 0.65) would not be met by it.
3. The sustainability-plan requirement cuts both ways: a project with a real maintenance roadmap and documented reproducibility story clears it more credibly than a proposal invented for the call.

## Practical conclusion

Keep OTF in the backlog behind programs whose remit matches the project's actual shape, and pursue it only when a genuine at-risk-user deliverable exists to scope to. Otherwise skip rather than force a fit. Before any decision, re-read the two guidebook pages (both weight 0.75) for the current cycle's dates and criteria.
