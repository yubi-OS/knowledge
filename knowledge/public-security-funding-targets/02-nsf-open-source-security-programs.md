# NSF programs that fund open-source security work

Scope: US National Science Foundation programs that fund open-source security research and maintenance (SaTC, Transition to Practice), their eligibility and open-source relevance.

## SaTC 2.0: the flagship cybersecurity research program

Security, Privacy, and Trust in Cyberspace (SaTC) is NSF's flagship cybersecurity research program. The SaTC 2.0 solicitation "re-envisions the high-level goals of NSF's flagship Secure and Trustworthy Cyberspace (SaTC) program in the context of the current and future cyber ecosystem" (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-satc-us-national-science-foundation-security-privacy-trust/nsf25-515/solicitation, weight 0.67).

The program's own framing: SaTC "supports interdisciplinary research and education to develop a secure, resilient and trustworthy global cyber ecosystem by addressing vulnerabilities, improving trust in cyber systems and cultivating a well-trained cybersecurity workforce" (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-satc-us-national-science-foundation-security-privacy-trust, weight 0.94; mirrored at https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/satc-20-security-privacy-trust-cyberspace, weight 0.93).

Three structural facts matter for an applicant:

1. SaTC is a long-running investment area, begun in FY 2012, that "continuously evolves to address new cybersecurity threats" and is aligned with the 2023 Federal Cybersecurity Research and Development Strategic Plan (https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/60_fy2025.pdf, weight 0.87).
2. SaTC 2.0 spans multiple NSF directorates: Computer and Information Science and Engineering (CISE), Mathematical and Physical Sciences (MPS), Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences (SBE), and STEM Education (EDU) (https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/357554, weight 0.75).
3. NSF grants are subject to the agency's award terms and conditions and to updated research security policies (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-ttp-national-science-foundation-translation-practice/nsf25-540/solicitation, weight 0.97). Applicants should read the solicitation and the Proposal and Award Policies and Procedures Guide before drafting anything.

## Transition to Practice (TTP): the open-source-relevant line

NSF's Translation to Practice program is run by the Directorate for Technology, Innovation and Partnerships, which partners across sectors to advance technology translation and development, regional innovation, and workforce preparation (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-ttp-national-science-foundation-translation-practice, weight 0.89). The program's stated purpose is that translation "ensures that the insights and innovations developed through scientific study and experimentation have tangible, positive impacts for the Nation," including economic growth, national security, and global competitiveness (https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/01db6b26-98f3-43cc-ba54-f38ee97aefdb, weight 0.72).

The key fact for an open-source security project is in the TTP program materials: the TTP-Translate (TTP-T) variant "does not require previous NSF funding but begins with existing use-inspired research," and the types of activities supported "could include (but not limited to): Prototype development and testing, Process optimization, Community-driven open-source development, Standards creation, etc." (https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/TTP-Webinar-072225-Final-508c.pdf, weight 0.94).

Community-driven open-source development is therefore an explicitly named supportable activity under a current NSF solicitation, and the TTP-T track is reachable without a prior NSF award.

## Fit assessment for open-source security work

The fit is conditional and depends on which track an applicant targets:

- A research-shaped open-source security project (for example, measured boot and attestation research, or supply-chain verification tooling) can plausibly anchor a SaTC proposal, since SaTC funds vulnerability and trust research (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-satc-us-national-science-foundation-security-privacy-trust, weight 0.94). But SaTC proposals are research proposals: they are led by institutions, reviewed as science, and not maintainer grants.
- A maintenance or hardening deliverable fits TTP-Translate better than SaTC, because TTP-T names community-driven open-source development as fundable work (https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/TTP-Webinar-072225-Final-508c.pdf, weight 0.94). The applicant still needs a use-inspired research basis to start from.

## Practical conclusion

NSF is a high-friction, research-gated route: proposals follow the PAPPG, run through institutional or small-business structures, and are reviewed against solicitation goals rather than a project's own readiness gates. It belongs late in any application backlog, behind foundation-style programs that fund scoped deliverables directly. Re-verify open solicitations and deadlines on nsf.gov and simpler.grants.gov before any application; both SaTC 2.0 (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-satc-us-national-science-foundation-security-privacy-trust, weight 0.94) and TTP (https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf-ttp-national-science-foundation-translation-practice, weight 0.89) are live program pages as of this dig.
