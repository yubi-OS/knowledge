# Sovereign Tech Fund and Sovereign Tech Agency: maintenance funding with a prototype exclusion

Scope: Sovereign Tech Fund / Sovereign Tech Agency: funding focus on maintenance of critical open digital infrastructure, eligibility, prototype exclusion, and application cycles.

## What the agency is and what it funds

The Sovereign Tech Agency describes its mission in terms of digital infrastructure: "Digital infrastructure emerges from an open source ecosystem that makes it possible to reuse, quickly distribute, and maintain digital technologies. Sustaining this ecosystem protects Germany's and Europe's digital sovereignty, capacity for innovation, and economic competitiveness today and in the future" (https://www.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.63). The agency "supports the development, improvement, and maintenance of open digital infrastructure" (https://apply.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.55), and its fund framing puts it plainly: "It is time to invest in digital commons, volunteer communities and the open source ecosystem" (https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/fund, weight 0.57).

## The defining feature: maintenance, not prototypes

An independent case study published by the European Commission's Open Source Observatory states the program's defining innovation directly: "The Sovereign Tech Fund introduces two notable innovations to the way public institutions support open source software. First, it shifts the focus of public funding away from innovation and prototypes toward the long-term maintenance of open digital infrastructure" (https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/document/funding-open-source-case-study-sovereign-tech-fund, weight 0.89).

That sentence is the single most important fact in this corpus for screening purposes: a funding body that explicitly funds maintenance rather than prototypes will screen out a pre-launch, prototype-stage project by design. The exclusion is not a quirk of the application; it is the program's thesis.

## Program surface

The agency runs more than one program line:

- The Sovereign Tech Fund itself, funding open digital infrastructure work (https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/fund, weight 0.57).
- Sovereign Tech Standards, "a network [that] closes the gap between those who write open standards and those who implement them," ensuring "that the technologies comprising our digital infrastructure remain interoperable, open, and grounded in real-world practice" (https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/standards, weight 0.69). This is relevant where a project implements or maintains open standards rather than an application.

Applications run through a dedicated portal that requires reviewing "all information regarding the requirements, criteria, and the proposal process" before preparing an application (https://apply.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.55). Note that sovereign.tech is served at both https://www.sovereign.tech/ and https://www.sovereign.tech (the latter page scored 0.33 in this dig as a duplicate); use the primary domain.

## Fit assessment: why this is deferred, not rejected

For a project that is pre-launch and prototype-stage, the fit fails on the program's own terms:

1. The program funds maintenance of already-critical infrastructure (https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/document/funding-open-source-case-study-sovereign-tech-fund, weight 0.89). A prototype has no maintenance record to fund.
2. The agency's remit is infrastructure that serves Germany's and Europe's digital sovereignty (https://www.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.63). A project should be able to articulate its criticality to that infrastructure base, not merely its technical merit.
3. The application gate is deliberate: criteria and proposal process must be reviewed in full before applying (https://apply.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.55), which signals a considered proposal process rather than a light-touch one.

None of this is a governance or values conflict; it is a staging mismatch. The correct treatment is deferral: revisit once a stable, production-grade core exists with users and a maintenance burden to fund. At that point the fit inverts, because the program will pay for exactly the maintenance work the project will then actually have.

## Practical conclusion

Sovereign Tech Fund is a post-GA target, not a now target. Keep it in the backlog with a revisit condition attached (a stable, non-prototype deliverable and a maintenance workload to fund), and re-read the criteria and process pages (https://apply.sovereign.tech/, weight 0.55) immediately before any revisit, since application windows and criteria evolve. Do not apply prematurely: an application from a prototype-stage project would be rejected on the program's own maintenance-first thesis (https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/document/funding-open-source-case-study-sovereign-tech-fund, weight 0.89) and would spend application capacity better spent on near-fit programs.
