# NLnet Foundation and NGI Zero: R&D grants for the open internet

Scope: NLnet Foundation and NGI Zero funds: grant sizes, themed call calendar, eligibility for privacy and security research, and open-source requirements.

## What NLnet funds

NLnet Foundation has supported the open internet since 1997, funding "projects that help fix the internet through open hardware, open software, open standards, open science and open data" (https://nlnet.nl/, weight 0.52). Its funding page frames the remit: "We support projects that contribute to an open, resilient, trustworthy, sustainable and human-centered internet," and it financially supports "organisations and people who are developing open solutions that empower users on all layers of technology" (https://nlnet.nl/funding.html, weight 0.93). NLnet is a European institution with a long history: it was influential in spreading the internet through Europe in the 1980s and founded NLnet Labs in Amsterdam in 1999 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLnet, weight 0.61).

## Hard requirements for proposals

NLnet's application guidance states the requirements that shape any proposal:

- "Our grants are primarily for technical development."
- "Software, hardware and content must be released under open licences and apply (preferably open) standards."
- "We are not interested in AI-generated projects or proposals" (NLnet publishes a Generative AI policy).
- "Our EU-supported funds require a strong 'European dimension'; usually this means applicants should (mostly) be from Horizon Europe-eligible countries."

(All from https://nlnet.nl/propose/, weight 0.77.)

These are the binding eligibility gates: open licence by construction, genuine technical development, human-authored proposals, and, for the EU-supported NGI funds, European eligibility.

## NGI Zero and the call calendar

NGI Zero is "part of a key initiative by the European Commission centered around establishing a Next Generation Internet," aimed at addressing "the many security, privacy and operational issues of todays internet" (https://nlnet.nl/NGI0/, weight 0.76). The NGI Zero coalition site points applicants to NLnet as the coalition lead, hosting the grant programmes, the application form, and support services (https://ngi-0.eu/, weight 0.59).

NLnet runs multiple themed funds in parallel with its open call. Its current-call page advertises "Apply for funding before November 3rd 2026" and lists active theme areas including "Building a scalable open internet stack (Restack)" and "Strengthen software supply chain security with packaging metadata (CodeSupply)," adding: "In addition there are a number of smaller theme funds you could have a look at. Uncertain? You can always submit through the open call" (https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260903-call.html, weight 0.87). The supply-chain-security theme (CodeSupply) is directly relevant to a project whose work is supply-chain verification and provenance gating.

NLnet also maintains an areas-of-special-interest page, noting historical support for open protocol work and that themed funds are "intended to be complementary to the funding of R&D work" (https://nlnet.nl/themes/, weight 0.88).

## Fit assessment for a scoped security deliverable

The fit is strong on mission and structure:

1. Privacy and security research is squarely inside the remit ("secure... internet", https://nlnet.nl/NGI0/, weight 0.76).
2. Grants fund technical development of an existing project rather than entity-building or commercialization, which matches an open-source deliverable pattern (https://nlnet.nl/propose/, weight 0.77).
3. There is a live, dated call window (November 3rd, 2026 deadline, https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260903-call.html, weight 0.87), plus themed funds that change, so the correct pre-application step is to identify which fund is currently open.

The constraints that shape targeting:

- EU-supported funds require the European dimension / Horizon Europe-eligible applicant base (https://nlnet.nl/propose/, weight 0.77). A US-based solo project should target NLnet's own non-EU funds or the open call, and should confirm eligibility there first.
- The open-licence requirement is already satisfied by any open-source project, making this gate cheap to clear.

## Practical conclusion

NLnet is the best foundation-style target for a discrete technical deliverable with a security framing, provided the applicant verifies the currently open fund and the eligibility rule for its jurisdiction before drafting. The November 3rd, 2026 deadline (https://nlnet.nl/news/2026/20260903-call.html, weight 0.87) is the nearest dated call in this corpus; confirm on nlnet.nl whether it is still accepting submissions at application time, since calls close and reopen on their own calendar.
