# 01. Union basis construction: one coordinate system for two corpora

Scope: how the differential curve builds a shared 19-D primitive basis by concatenation with zero-padding, why mixing is forbidden, and why the union curve has zero dropped columns.

## The problem the union basis solves

The parent audit (`curve-guided-rsi`) and the offshoot audit (`curve-guided-rsi-self`) each fit their own 2-D learned curve. The parent works on 77 yubiOS skills scored on a 10-D security-primitive basis; the offshoot works on 131 self-doc items scored on a 9-D audit-trail basis. Each fit produces its own (u,v) plane, and the two planes are not comparable. The question "where do yubiOS skills and self-doc items live relative to each other?" has no answer until both item sets share one coordinate system ([source doc](https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/curve-guided-rsi-and-self-differential-2026-08-04.md), primary).

The union basis is the mechanism. It is a 19-D binary vector per item, built by concatenation: columns 0-9 carry the yubiOS primitives (attestation, trust_chain, least_privilege, declarative_policy, continuous_adaptive, immutability, audit_evidence, cryptographic_identity, segmentation, self_describing); columns 10-18 carry the self-doc primitives (has_purpose, has_source, has_evidence, has_correction, has_constraint, has_pushback, has_whole_self_note, has_test, has_cadence). A yubiOS skill keeps its native 10-D vector with columns 10-18 forced to 0. A self-doc item keeps its native 9-D vector with columns 0-9 forced to 0. Every item is then a 19-D binary vector whose "side" is implicitly encoded by which columns can be nonzero ([source doc](https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/curve-guided-rsi-and-self-differential-2026-08-04.md), primary).

## Concatenation, not mixing

The load-bearing constraint is that the union basis preserves each corpus's native primitive signal. The source doc lists "mixing primitive bases" as a new anti-pattern: assigning self-doc primitives to a yubiOS skill, or vice versa, destroys the audit. Zero-padding the other side is what prevents this. Each item's coverage pattern stays native; only the container is shared ([source doc](https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/curve-guided-rsi-and-self-differential-2026-08-04.md), primary).

External grounding supports the general design choice. A survey of multimodal alignment and fusion frames the core decision as whether to align representations into a shared space before or after encoding, with distinct tradeoffs for each path [w=0.87, https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11263-025-02667-1.pdf]. A unified framework for feature fusion in multimodal systems likewise treats the fusion strategy (early concatenation versus learned shared latents) as the central architectural decision [w=0.89, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-025-09622-1]. Feature-level fusion by concatenation is the standard "early fusion" baseline, and the comparative study of fusion strategies found that the alignment quality of the underlying representations determines which fusion strategy works [w=0.62, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01207]. Federated work on heterogeneous feature concatenation shows concatenated heterogeneous blocks remain trainable end to end, which is exactly the union-basis posture [w=0.79, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167739X24006381]. The shared-latent-space alternative (ShaLa) learns a joint space from heterogeneous modalities; it is the more powerful but heavier cousin of what the differential does with plain concatenation [w=0.83, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17376]. The yubiOS pipeline picks the lightest variant that preserves the audit: deterministic, no learned mixing, and reversible by slicing columns.

## Why zero dropped columns matters

Near-constant columns are normally dropped before a curve fit: in the parent fit, 3 of 10 yubiOS columns were dropped at a 0.90 coverage threshold, and in the offshoot fit, columns were dropped aggressively (per-file kept counts of 1 to 4 of 9). On the union basis, the pipeline used a slightly relaxed 0.92 threshold and dropped 0 of 19 columns. The reason is structural: each corpus saturates its own side's columns, and is zero on the other side, so every column has variance contributed by exactly one side. No column is near-constant across the union, so the full 19-D basis survives into the fit ([source doc](https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/curve-guided-rsi-and-self-differential-2026-08-04.md), primary).

## Result

Fitting the single curve on the combined 208-item, 19-column matrix produced PC1+PC2 explained variance of 0.6770 and holdout R-squared of +0.7013, both higher than either parent fit. A single 2-D learned surface can represent the union corpus because 67.7 percent of union-basis variance is captured by 2 dimensions ([source doc](https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/curve-guided-rsi-and-self-differential-2026-08-04.md), primary). The union fit did not displace the per-corpus fits: all three (u,v) planes are kept, and the differential plane is the overlay in which cross-corpus positions become meaningful.
