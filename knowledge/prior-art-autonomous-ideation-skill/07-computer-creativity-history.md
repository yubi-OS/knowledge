# 07 - Computer Creativity History

Scope: the pre-LLM computer creativity arc (Pygmalion, AARON, EMI, TALE-SPIN, JAPE, the 2005 NSF creativity support tools agenda) and the amplification versus autonomous creation split.

## AARON: the autonomous-creation pole

AARON is the strongest documented ancestor of autonomous creation. The Computer History Museum documents Harold Cohen as "a pioneer in computer art, in algorithmic art, and in generative art", who "was first and foremost a painter" and "an engineer whose work defined the first generation" of the field, in a 40-year collaboration with the AARON program (source: https://computerhistory.org/blog/harold-cohen-and-aaron-a-40-year-collaboration/, weight 0.71). A Leonard journal article documents Cohen's last phase, 2010 to 2016, as "an accelerated coevolution of Cohen's relationship with the artificial intelligence program AARON" (source: https://direct.mit.edu/leon/article/54/4/412/97258/Harold-Cohen-and-AARON-Collaborations-in-the-Last, weight 0.91, the highest weight in this corpus). Wikipedia's entry states AARON was "a computer program designed to produce paintings and drawings autonomously, which set it apart from previous programs" (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Cohen_(artist), weight 0.10, weak backing; cite the museum and journal sources). A Brooklyn Rail review situates the work historically as AI image tools went mainstream with text-to-image systems (source: https://brooklynrail.org/2024/04/artseen/Harold-Cohen-AARON/, weight 0.63). A practitioner blog notes AARON was "equipped with basic knowledge of physics and drawing" and "creates drawings from scratch", a different approach from modern models (source: https://undetectable.ai/blog/first-ai-generated-image/, weight 0.19, weak backing).

## The 2005 NSF creativity support tools agenda

The formal research agenda predates LLM agents by two decades. The NSF Workshop Report on Creativity Support Tools frames the topic as "high risk but potentially very high payoff" and states the goal of "develop[ing] improved software and user interface" tools for creative work (source: https://www.pamelajennings.org/uploads/2/7/0/8/27088277/policy_2005_nsf_creative_support_tools_workshop_final_, weight 0.62). The workshop site records the same objective and that it brought "leading academic and industrial researchers together to share experiences, identify opportunities" (source: http://creativeit.cs.colorado.edu/CreativeIT/59, weight 0.60). The June 13-14, 2005 workshop page planned a 25-30 person workshop of leading academic and industry researchers (source: http://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST, weight 0.58). A retrospective notes the NSF-sponsored workshop "brought together 25 research leaders and graduate students to share experiences, identify opportunities, and formulate research" directions (source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220302282_Creativity_Support_Tools_Report_From_a_US_National_Science_, weight 0.47, weak backing). The source doc's summary of the agenda's pillars, exploratory search, rich history-keeping, and rapid alternative generation, is consistent with these records; the workshop pages above are the citable primary sources.

## Amplification versus autonomous creation

The source doc frames the two foundational lineages as amplification (Pygmalion, 1975, extend the human) versus autonomous creation (AARON, replace the human). The dig corroborates the autonomous pole strongly (AARON "designed to produce paintings and drawings autonomously": source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Cohen_(artist), weight 0.10, weak backing, but the autonomy claim is corroborated at higher weight by the CHM and Leonard records above). The Pygmalion thesis PDF (source: https://web.media.mit.edu/~lieber/Publications/Pygmalion-Remixed.pdf, weight not established by this dig; listed for the lineage, treat as unweighted) is the citable primary for the amplification pole. The judgment that modern autonomous ideation sits closer to AARON than Pygmalion follows from the autonomy framing in the AARON sources (source: https://computerhistory.org/blog/harold-cohen-and-aaron-a-40-year-collaboration/, weight 0.71).

## Other historical anchors

The source doc anchors EMI (David Cope's music composition system analyzing and mimicking composer styles), TALE-SPIN (1977, narrative generation), JAPE (1994, pun generation), and The Combinator (combinational creativity linking unrelated ideas via simulation; source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/combinator-a-computerbased-tool-for-creative-idea-generation-based-on-a-simulation-approach/12C723397EB477F421699D02A025E724, weight not established by this dig, treat as unweighted). A personal site of a creativity researcher indexes related work but adds no independent claims (source: http://www.conceptlab.com/, weight 0.40, weak backing).

## Implications

The 50-year arc establishes that both poles are old: tools that extend a human's creative process, and systems that create autonomously. What the LLM-agent generation adds is not autonomy itself (AARON had it) but autonomous ideation over text domains with self-critique, and with it the evaluation questions in docs 05 and 06. The NSF agenda's "high risk but potentially very high payoff" framing remains an accurate description of the current autonomous ideation space (source: https://www.pamelajennings.org/uploads/2/7/0/8/27088277/policy_2005_nsf_creative_support_tools_workshop_final_, weight 0.62).
