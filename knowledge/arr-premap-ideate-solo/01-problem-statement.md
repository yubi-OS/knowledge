## Half one: separating arrangement from morphology

The instrument question is a separation question. The oracle reads two quantities per image, the box-counting dimension D and the walk dimension d_w. A pixel-level warp changes both the shape of each droplet and where droplets sit; an arrangement warp moves only droplet centres and re-renders identical disks. The record's three-arm design (clean, pixel warp, arrangement warp) exists so the response to arrangement alone can be read without the morphological change mixed in.

This is a calibration problem in the classic sense. Calibration is "the determination of the experimental relationship between the quantity being measured and the output of the measuring device" per [Instrument Calibration - an overview \| ScienceDirect Topics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/instrument-calibration) (clef weight: 0.3966) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5), and the ISA calibration chapter frames it as comparing measuring equipment against a standard, including setting zero and span, per [PDF CALIBRATION PRINCIPLES - International Society of Automation](https://www.isa.org/getmedia/a9356c34-5956-4952-9888-d6df8dd3e7ba/calabration-principles-chapter1.pdf) (clef weight: 0.3447) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). The LibreTexts treatment of external standards, where the standard is prepared separately from the sample, per [1.3: Methods of Calibration - Chemistry LibreTexts](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Duke_University/CHEM_401L%3A_Analytical_Chemistry_Lab/06%3A_Instrument_Facilities_for_CHEM401L/01%3A_Analytical_Equiptment_and_Methods_for_Calibration/1.03%3A_Methods_of_Calibration) (clef weight: 0.2339) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5), maps onto the record's clean arm: the clean gasket is the external standard against which both warped arms are read. ## Why affine pre-normalization matters here

The variations that touch registration (V4, V7) and the stress test of V3 all hinge on frame alignment: containment only works at the identity frame, and any displacement larger than the tolerance breaks it. A standard tool for removing a linear distortion before comparison is the whitening (sphering) transformation, which maps a vector with known covariance to one whose covariance is the identity, per [Whitening transformation - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitening_transformation) (clef weight: 0.2741) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). The Windsor notes write it as Z = W(X - mu), centring at the mean before the linear map, per [PDF Whitening transformation - University of Windsor](https://jlu.myweb.cs.uwindsor.ca/8380/Whitening_transformation.pdf) (clef weight: 0.1974) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5), and SciPy exposes the operation directly as `Covariance.whiten`, per [whiten - SciPy v1.18.0 Manual](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.Covariance.whiten.html) (clef weight: 0.2182) (weak backing, clef weight < 0.5). The ground source does not adopt whitening; it names registration for real panels as open and Phase 2. Whitening is cited here only as the obvious candidate for the affine part of that open gate, and every source behind it is weak backing.
