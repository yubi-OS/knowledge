# 06. Per-diagnostic admission criteria: rayleigh, axis, spectra, radius

Scope: per-diagnostic admission criteria: the Rayleigh uniformity trial, the axis-redundancy trial, the spectra Parseval-share trial (E0 through E3, even, odd), and the radius I(r) count trial, including the n_at_least_100 floor.

Program-specific statements derive from the instrument's trial record of 2026-09-19 (yubiOS refs/admission-trials-2026-09-19.md). External claims are cited inline with jev weight; weights below 0.5 are labeled weak backing.

## Trial 1: the Rayleigh uniformity trial

The Rayleigh test assesses the significance of the mean resultant length for circular data, against a uniformity null with a unimodal alternative of unknown mean direction (circular package documentation, rdrr.io, weight 0.825). An equivalent formulation is that the test evaluates uniformity of circular or directional data by the mean resultant length (tectonicr package, CRAN, weight 0.568). In the trial framework, rayleigh/1 tests whether the corpus point cloud's angular positions are indistinguishable from uniform; it was the first diagnostic to be computed rather than hard-coded (since PR #252) and is now delegated inside the unified call. Practitioner guides note the test remains reliable for discrete data, such as data produced by a measuring instrument with finite precision (Statistics How To, Rayleigh test of uniformity, weight 0.438, weak backing), which matters here because the corpus frames are finite point sets, not continuous angle samples.

## Trial 2: the axis-redundancy trial

The axis trial (axis-trial/1) asks whether per-axis verdicts are redundant or informative. Its admission criteria are: a second independent null seed, non-degenerate nulls, reproducible per-axis verdicts, certified margins, and N >= 100. The mechanism follows the standard permutation logic: a null distribution is generated by shuffling the association between variables, and verdicts come from comparing observed statistics against that distribution (Applied Biostatistics, bookdown, weight 0.785). The trial reports per-axis exclusions (for example, "excl 7/7" meaning 7 of 7 axes excluded) but admission additionally requires the per-axis verdict pattern to reproduce across seeds. The results table shows this is the criterion that most often fails: maps 431 and 78 lost admission to `verdicts_reproducible_all` despite large exclusions.

## Trial 3: the spectra shares trial

The spectra trial covers the S-squared Parseval shares E0, E1, E2, E3 and the even/odd energy split, as displayed on the spectra card of every map. Before this change the card carried a constant `admitted:false`; after it, the statistical admission of the shares is computed by the unified call, while the card's flag remains for homology and physical readings, which stay permanently not admitted. The trial checks each share against the margin-matched null distribution: the record notes that on refs/ 436 and skills/ 81 the shares are strongly excluded from the fixed-margin null, reproducibly, so the shares are admitted for reporting there, and that this is the first time the card's numbers carry a null. The observed values on 436 are recorded verbatim: E0 0.43457396894193234, E1 0.4663251610438744, E2 0.038721576679943956, E3 0.06037929333424935, even 0.4732955456218763, odd 0.5267044543781237.

## Trial 4: the radius I(r) counts trial

The radius trial covers the I(r) counts on the fixed grid: how many corpus points fall within each radius band. On 436 the grid counts are I(0.075)=34, I(0.085)=29, I(0.095)=23, I(0.105)=14, I(0.115)=7. The trial's operative radius stays 0.095 and the grid is never reselected, because the bounds and their validation flags describe caller-supplied perturbation assumptions, not data-derived quantities. The record observes that the I(r) counts are not-excluded on every refs/ and docs/ frame: the isolate profile of the refs/ point cloud is what a margin-matched random bit matrix also produces. Admission therefore tracks reproducibility, not the raw counts: admitted on 436, 431, and 326, refused on 78 and 81 where a single grid point flipped between seeds.

## The N >= 100 floor

Every trial carries an N >= 100 criterion, and it is designed to refuse everything below the floor: the docs/ round-11 baseline (map 296, N=21) is refused on all four diagnostics with `n_at_least_100` as the only why_not. Sample-size planning underpins this: statistical power is a critical step in the design of experiments, and for a given design the power can be calculated by simulation (SimEngine vignette, CRAN, weight 0.691). Simulation-based sample-size planning is recommended when standard formulas do not fit the design (a tailored tutorial on simulation-based sample-size planning for GLMMs, weight 0.640). Reliability research suggests concrete minimum sample sizes from simulation studies, which is why a floor rather than a formula is used (Sample size recommendations for studies on reliability and measurement error, Springer, weight 0.972). Clinical tooling likewise determines the minimum number of subjects for adequate study power (ClinCalc sample size calculator, weight 0.669). The general background is standard: sample size determination is the act of choosing the number of observations to include in a statistical sample (Wikipedia, weight 0.264, weak backing). The trial framework picks 100 as a fixed floor so the criterion is decidable without design-specific power analysis per frame.
