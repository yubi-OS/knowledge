# 01. Outline decomposition

Scope: How a free-form research request is decomposed into a doc outline: decomposition by domain joints (subsystems, lifecycle stages, comparison axes, key decisions), one-line scopes, and seed query authoring.

Corpus minting starts with a decomposition step, and the research literature on topic structure supports doing it deliberately rather than ad hoc. Academic work on outline generation treats it as topic segmentation: dividing a document into coherent topic sections and generating the corresponding subheadings, which surfaces the discourse topic structure explicitly. A paragraph-level hierarchical topic structure with three levels was proposed for exactly this purpose in Chinese text corpora (weight 0.90, https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.44/; the same work is on arXiv as 2305.14790, weight 0.84, https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14790v1). The lesson for corpus minting is that an outline is a hierarchy of topic representations, not a flat list of titles.

A second research thread supports decomposing by the domain's own joints. Work on topic decomposition and summarization for temporal-sequenced corpora explicitly frames the task as discovering different topic aspects, that is, sub-topics, and the incidents related to each sub-topic (weight 0.75, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-13657-3_47). Translated to a system-design topic, the joints are subsystems, lifecycle stages, comparison axes, and key decisions; the outline should be cut where the domain itself is cut, not where generic category labels suggest.

For long-form generation specifically, outline-based planning with hierarchical decomposition and entity tracking is the mechanism that keeps coherence across a long document (weight 0.73, https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/long-form-generation-outline-hierarchical-coherence-evaluation). In minting terms: each subtopic doc carries a one-line scope so that entity definitions stay consistent across docs, and the outline itself becomes the entity-tracking artifact for the corpus.

The practical guidance from research-writing pedagogy converges on the same move. Library guides teach topic narrowing through concept maps, where a broad topic breaks into subtopics with examples attached to each, for example accessibility, price, and health risks as subtopics of junk food (weight 0.48, weak backing, https://guides.laguardia.edu/c.php?g=845196&p=6043265). USC's writing guide covers preparing and organizing a research topic with practical narrowing strategies (weight 0.37, weak backing, https://libguides.usc.edu/writingguide/narrowtopic). Waterloo's guide makes the narrowing dynamic explicit: as you narrow into one subtopic, the number of candidate subtopics decreases and each remaining one becomes more specific (weight 0.35, weak backing, https://uwaterloo.ca/writing-and-communication-centre/develop-and-narrow-topic). The Waterloo observation is directly relevant to outline pruning: decomposition and validation are coupled, because a validated outline is one where the surviving subtopics have all become more specific than the parent topic.

One design decision in corpus minting is that each outline entry also carries seed queries. This couples the outline to the dig phase: the queries must be answerable by a metasearch engine, which pushes scope lines toward concrete nouns rather than abstract themes.

An honest observation about weighting from this dig: the highest-weighted result was a dictionary definition of "break" (weight 0.93, https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/break) and a kernel-community aggregation page scored 0.54 (https://planet.kernel.org/). Both are primary in the source-authority sense but content-thin for this subtopic. The practical rule that falls out is that a high weight certifies source authority, not topical fit, so the author still filters for relevance even among high-weight results.

## Sub-claims

1. Outline generation is hierarchical topic segmentation with subheadings (weight 0.90, https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.44/).
2. Decomposition should follow domain joints: aspects and sub-topics of the corpus subject (weight 0.75, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-13657-3_47).
3. Outline-based planning with hierarchical decomposition and entity tracking preserves coherence in long-form generation (weight 0.73, https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/long-form-generation-outline-hierarchical-coherence-evaluation).
4. Concept maps and narrowing strategies are the standard pedagogy for subtopic discovery (weight 0.48, weak backing, https://guides.laguardia.edu/c.php?g=845196&p=6043265; weight 0.35, weak backing, https://uwaterloo.ca/writing-and-communication-centre/develop-and-narrow-topic).
5. High weight certifies authority, not topical fit; relevance filtering remains the author's job (observed in this dig: weight 0.93 for https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/break, weight 0.54 for https://planet.kernel.org/).
