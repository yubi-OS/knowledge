# 09. Provenance and honesty discipline

Scope: Corpus minting as an honesty contract: never inventing facts, deleting unsourced claims instead of softening them, padding prohibition, cannot-author as a success signal, and audit trails that let a reviewer re-run verification.

A minted corpus makes an unusual promise: every factual claim in every doc carries a source URL and a recorded decision-model weight, and any claim that cannot be backed is deleted rather than softened. This is a provenance discipline, and the definitions behind it are established. Content provenance is information that helps identify where digital content came from, how it was created, and whether it was modified (weight 0.77, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/content-safety/provenance-disclosure). A mint's research DB is that definition made operational for a corpus: the provenance of each claim is the URL plus the weight plus the decision record that produced the weight.

Why the discipline matters is quantified in the FAQ of an AI-documentation provenance initiative: without standardized provenance, it becomes difficult to verify origin, accountability, and proper attribution, and making provenance explicit and referenceable across the AI lifecycle is the remedy (weight 0.64, https://hikma.hbku.edu.qa/aidoi/faq). A corpus whose claims are unverifiable is worse than no corpus, because it borrows the authority of documentation without the accountability.

The failure mode the discipline guards against is hallucination, and the research record treats grounding as the countermeasure. A 2026 peer-reviewed QA-systems paper states the requirement in system terms: question-answering systems must be both semantically nuanced and factually grounded, and addressing the limits of keyword-based retrieval and similar techniques requires explicit grounding mechanisms (weight 0.88, https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/3/541). On the practitioner side, a citation-first discipline has been productized as an explicit workflow: classify claims, verify before writing, signal confidence levels, and audit output for hallucinations (weight 0.32, weak backing, https://github.com/moonpiesheldon1337/grounded-research). The mint contract is a strict version of that workflow: verification happens at the weighting stage before any writing starts, confidence is signaled through the weight label in the text itself, and the audit is the post-push re-parse plus a reviewer's spot-check of claim-to-snippet support.

Two structural rules make the honesty contract enforceable rather than aspirational.

First, the padding prohibition and the skip-as-gap rule. If a subtopic's dig cannot support an honest doc after its allowed redos, the doc is skipped and the gap is recorded in the corpus README. A documented gap is a deliverable; padded prose is a defect. This inverts the usual completion pressure: the pipeline succeeds by honestly reporting what it could not do. A commercial knowledge-base product describes the same posture from the serving side, ensuring every AI response is verified against governed knowledge, no guessing, no fabrication (weight 0.15, weak backing, https://bloomfire.com/resources/hallucination-prevention/), and strategy roundups for agent reliability treat hallucination prevention as a production requirement rather than a nice-to-have (weight 0.12, weak backing, https://agentplace.io/blog/hallucination-prevention-strategies-for-reliable-agent-output).

Second, the audit trail that lets a reviewer re-run verification independently. The reviewer does not need to trust the mint agent: they need the preflight record to re-probe the endpoints, the jev log to recount requests and usage tokens, the archive to recompute the weight split, and the PR diff to confirm the research DB actually landed. Conversational approaches to grounding, such as controlling the flow of a conversation to reduce hallucination (weight 0.14, weak backing, https://xoltar.substack.com/p/grounding), are not available to an autonomous mint; the audit trail is what replaces conversational verification for an unattended pipeline.

One weighting-behavior caveat belongs on the record because it is the honest limit of automated provenance: a homepage of a well-known research organization scored 0.70 in this corpus's weighting pass while carrying almost no content relevant to this topic (weight 0.70, https://openai.com/). Automated authority weighting cannot substitute for human relevance judgment; it bounds the problem, it does not solve it. The author's delete rule, a claim with no source is deleted, remains the final enforcement layer.
