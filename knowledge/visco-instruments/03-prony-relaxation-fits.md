# 03 Prony relaxation fits

Scope: the `/visco/prony` route: fitting a Prony relaxation series over a runs history with a deterministic tau grid plus non-negative least squares, K <= 3 arms, time basis selection, and honest fit-quality reporting (r2).

## What the route does

`GET /visco/prony?metric=dbc&arms=2` fits a Prony relaxation series over the corpus-runs history. The fit is deterministic by construction: relaxation times are drawn from a fixed tau grid, arm weights come from non-negative least squares (NNLS), the arm count is capped at K <= 3, and the time basis defaults to `created_at` with a row-index fallback when the history lacks usable timestamps [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md]. The deterministic-fitter choice is one of the jev-qualified design decisions carried into v1, recorded at weight 0.82 [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md].

## The live fit is honestly bad, and that is the point

On the live runs history the route returned 35 points on the `created_at` time basis, a ke of -13.54, 2 arms with tau values 45.8 and 476.4, and a fit quality of r2 = 0.069 [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md]. An r2 of 0.069 is a poor fit, and the build record says so plainly: the mixed-history series does not follow a clean relaxation curve. The same record documents the companion fact that the recorded round-3 trajectory alone fits at r2 near 0 with a ke-only model, and that this is written into the fixtures [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md]. Reporting a bad fit honestly is a feature of the instrument: a relaxation fit over a history that is not relaxation-shaped should say so rather than manufacture a confident curve.

## Why a deterministic tau grid plus NNLS

The Prony series represents a relaxation modulus as a sum of exponential decays; it is a discretization of the generalized Maxwell model used for relaxation and damping behavior in finite element simulations [source: https://novasolver.jp/en/structural/nonlinear-material/viscoelastic-prony.html, jev weight 0.3291, weak backing]. Fitting one is an ill-posed inverse problem: both the relaxation times and the weights are unknown, and a naive simultaneous search is non-convex. The deterministic design sidesteps the non-convexity by fixing the tau grid (the search space becomes linear in the weights) and using NNLS for the weights.

NNLS is the constrained least-squares problem in which the coefficients are not allowed to become negative [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_least_squares, jev weight 0.6689]. The constraint is not cosmetic here: a negative arm weight would mean a modulus contribution that grows instead of decays, which is physically meaningless for a relaxation spectrum.

The literature supports constrained identification over free fitting. A study on recovering a non-negative relaxation spectrum model from discrete data develops a hierarchical identification scheme applicable to both relaxation time and frequency spectra, using finite-dimensional parametric model classes and least-squares identification with a smoothing constraint [source: https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/15/16/3464, jev weight 0.7197; same paper preprint: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457811/, jev weight 0.7595]. A related inverse problem, recovering a distribution of relaxation times from multi-echo T2 magnetic resonance data, is likewise treated as a constrained estimation problem with explicit quality assessment of the recovered spectrum [source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811921008557, jev weight 0.7554].

## Fitting quality in the literature, for contrast

An optimal discrete-time Prony series fitting method for viscoelastic materials was validated against experimental data from the temporomandibular joint disc, a curve with a short-time and high relaxation rate, and improved the fit significantly over prior approaches [source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11043-018-9394-z, jev weight 0.9112]. Commercial finite element practice treats the Prony parameters as standard inputs: relaxation moduli come from elasticity parameters, and the Prony relative moduli and relaxation times are entered through a Prony data table [source: https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/////////Views/Secured/corp/v242/en/ans_mat/evis.html, jev weight 0.7116]. Master-curve construction with Prony series and time-temperature shift factors is the standard characterization route [source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282265959_Viscoelastic_Relaxation_Modulus_Characterization_Using_Prony_Series, jev weight 0.5102].

## What the route is for in the corpus context

Applied to a runs history, the fitted tau values are timescales over which the corpus metric decays or recovers between rounds, and the arms count reports how many distinct timescales the history supports. The route caps K at 3 because a small history cannot identify more arms meaningfully. The combination of a fixed grid, non-negative weights, a capped arm count, an explicit time-basis choice, and a reported r2 makes the fit reproducible and self-criticizing: two callers fitting the same history get the same numbers, and the r2 tells them how much to trust them [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md].
