# 10 Viscoelastic foundations

Scope: the physics grounding the instrument names: linear viscoelasticity, creep and recovery, stress relaxation, Prony-series representation, and hysteresis loops, as the vocabulary the API surface borrows for corpus auditing.

## Linear viscoelasticity and superposition

Linear viscoelasticity describes materials whose response to load is time-dependent but superposable. Its basic foundation is Boltzmann's superposition principle, which states that every loading step makes an independent contribution to the final state [source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/linear-viscoelasticity, jev weight 0.5856]. Formally, the total response to a stress or strain history is equivalent to the sum of the responses to the individual steps of that history [source: https://appliedmath.brown.edu/sites/default/files/fractional/12%20EssentialsofLinearViscoelasticity.pdf, jev weight 0.8816]. A reference chapter reviews the Boltzmann superposition principle and the constitutive equations of linear viscoelasticity in simple shear as the theory's core [source: https://www.eolss.net/Sample-Chapters/C06/E6-197-05-00.pdf, jev weight 0.7715]. Experimental demonstrations of viscoelastic behavior include creep tests, relaxation tests, recovery tests, and dynamic mechanical analysis, and the linear viscoelastic functions are formulated on this basis [source: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-007-2546-1_5, jev weight 0.905].

## Creep, recovery, and relaxation

The three canonical experiments define the vocabulary. In a stress relaxation test a material is strained to a constant strain and held; the stress required to hold it decreases over time [source: https://pkel015.connect.amazon.auckland.ac.nz/SolidMechanicsBooks/Part_I/BookSM_Part_I/10_Viscoelasticity/10_Viscoelasticity_01_Intro.pdf, jev weight 0.9391]. In creep a constant stress produces a growing strain; the relation between creep and recovery can be derived for a linear viscoelastic material using the Boltzmann superposition principle, which the literature treats as a fundamental theorem of the field [source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020746214001425, jev weight 0.8961].

The corpus instrument set maps onto these directly, and the mapping was made explicit in the creep-recovery replay that motivated the build: the load leg applies the edits (analogous to creep under load), the unload leg reverts the text and re-scores (recovery), and the persistence leg re-grades the loaded state with an independent grader [source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/knowledge/main/knowledge/linear-viscoelasticity/08-creep-recovery-replay.md, internal replay record]. The replay also demonstrated the mapping's limits: with a deterministic scorer the revert leg returns the matrix exactly, so the measured recovery fraction R is 1.0 for every edit class, and the discriminating measurement turned out to be the persistence leg (all 9 applied flips persisted under the blind grader) [source: internal replay record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/knowledge/main/knowledge/linear-viscoelasticity/08-creep-recovery-replay.md].

## The Prony series

The Prony series is the standard representation of a relaxation modulus as a sum of exponential decays, and it is a discretization of the generalized Maxwell model used for material relaxation and damping analysis in finite element simulation [source: https://novasolver.jp/en/structural/nonlinear-material/viscoelastic-prony.html, jev weight 0.3291, weak backing]. Optimal discrete-time fitting of Prony series to viscoelastic data is an established problem with validated methods [source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11043-018-9394-z, jev weight 0.9112], and finite element practice takes the Prony relative moduli and relaxation times as standard material-model inputs via a Prony data table [source: https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/////////Views/Secured/corp/v242/en/ans_mat/evis.html, jev weight 0.7116]. The `/visco/prony` route applies this representation to a corpus runs history: the fitted relaxation times (tau 45.8 and 476.4 on the live history) are the timescales over which the corpus metric decays between rounds [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md].

## Hysteresis

When a viscoelastic material is loaded and unloaded, the two stress-strain curves do not coincide; the energy absorbed during one loading-unloading cycle equals the area within the hysteresis loop, and the loop shape depends on the loading and unloading rates [source: https://www.doitpoms.ac.uk/tlplib/bioelasticity/viscoelasticity-hysteresis.php, jev weight 0.6984]. In the stress-strain plot of a viscoelastic material the loop area shows the energy lost as heat per cycle [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscoelasticity, jev weight 0.2003, weak backing]. Hysteresis appears because the material needs finite time to reach the steady state corresponding to the applied field, and that time is governed by the material's relaxation times [source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00890, jev weight 0.922].

The corpus mapping is the hysteresis rollup: predicted deltas form one branch and realized deltas the other, and the sum of absolute predicted-minus-realized error per round is the loop area. The round-3 calibration case measured 102.9 dBc-units dissipated across 10 cycles (mean 10.29 per cycle), where the lens expected +9.79 to +11.50 per candidate against realized per-cycle deltas summing to +0.65 [source: internal replay record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/knowledge/main/knowledge/linear-viscoelasticity/08-creep-recovery-replay.md]. That gap between predicted and realized is the dissipation the instrument measures, and the relaxation times fitted by the prony route are the same timescales that govern how quickly the "material" (the corpus metric) equilibrates after a load is applied.

## Why borrowed physics names are load-bearing

Each instrument name encodes its measurement question in a vocabulary with 80 years of measurement theory behind it: persistence asks the test-retest question, the hysteresis rollup asks the loop-area question, the prony fit asks the relaxation-spectrum question, and snapback asks the reversal question. The build record notes the surface is fixture-parity-tested against a Python system of record so the borrowed mathematics are implemented once and verified everywhere [source: internal build record, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/main/refs/visco-instruments-2026-10-02.md]. The names are not decoration: they tell a caller which physics question the endpoint answers, and the fixtures pin the numbers.
