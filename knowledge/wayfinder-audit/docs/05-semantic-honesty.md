# Semantic honesty: what the geometry is allowed to claim

Scope: how the audit demoted interpretive overreach, keeping azimuthal sectors anonymous, the twelve NSS axes unvalidated, PCA angles non-probative, and pole-shift sign non-decisive.

## The defect

The pre-audit reporting read semantic content into geometric artifacts. Azimuthal sectors of the map were treated as if they carried meaning; the twelve negative-skill-space axes were treated as if they were a validated lens dictionary; a PCA angle was treated as evidence of a semantic defect; and the sign of a pole shift was treated as a verdict on whether an edit was good. Each step translated a number into a claim the number cannot carry.

## The repair

The audited instrument states what its numbers are:

1. Azimuthal sectors are anonymous numbers. Reporting 12 occupied sectors on the 159-document corpus is an inventory of occupancy, not a semantic classification.
2. The twelve NSS axes remain an unvalidated lens dictionary. They organize questions; they do not measure defects.
3. No PCA angle establishes a semantic defect. Geometry can flag distance and orientation; semantics need validation the geometry does not supply.
4. Pole-shift sign no longer decides whether an edit is good. A directional statistic is not a quality judgment.

## Why the restraint matches the statistics

PCA gives directions of maximal variance and coordinates of samples along them; reconstructing or interpreting a fitted model requires knowing both where the samples lie relative to the axes and where the axes sit relative to the original variables (https://bryanhanson.github.io/LearnPCA/articles/Vig_04_Scores_Loadings.html, weight 0.646). The reference treatment of PCA is careful about what its objects mean, including exactly when mean-centering matters and what a correlation-based variant changes (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_component_analysis, weight 0.803). An angle between two fitted axes is a function of the data and the projection; calling it a semantic verdict adds a label the mathematics never issued. Weaker sources in the dig agree on the failure mode: misreading loadings and components is a common PCA interpretation error (https://metricgate.com/blogs/common-mistakes-in-pca-interpretation/, weight 0.150, weak; https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-interpret-pca-results-loadings-scores-more/, weight 0.234, weak).

The gap between geometric perception and semantic understanding is an active research problem, not a solved mapping. Point-cloud work frames it directly as "the semantic gap between geometric perception and language understanding" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580526003584, weight 0.570). Label-aware topic modeling validates against human labels explicitly, measuring "topic coherence and the correlations to labels" on labeled corpora (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417425045737, weight 0.756), and manifold-alignment work calls for "cross-domain validation and adaptation of the framework" before its layerwise semantic organization claims extend to new domains (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20333v2, weight 0.835). Validation is the price of a semantic claim; the wayfinder has not paid it.

## The unvalidated NSS lens

The twelve NSS axes (audience, inputs, outputs, mode, assumption set, adjacent problems, failure modes, lifecycle, composition, knowledge sources, calibration, recursion) are a vocabulary for asking questions of a document corpus. The audit keeps them as a lens and only a lens: a corpus cell that scores low under the lens is a question to investigate, not a defect proven. Calling the dictionary unvalidated is the honest description; no experiment has shown that the lens's axes correspond to independently measurable corpus properties.

## What the demotion changes in practice

Three reporting behaviors change. First, sector occupancy appears in reports as counts, without semantic adjectives. Second, any statement that a document "has a defect" is replaced by a statement that geometry flags it plus what the flag was, with the semantic claim left to a validated process that does not yet exist. Third, edit quality is judged by task outcomes and content inspection, not by the sign of a pole movement. This is the same discipline the evidence-standard doc applies to rungs versus trials: statistics describe the instrument; only validated prediction licenses claims about usefulness.

## What remains unproven

The audit does not claim the opposite of the old overreach either. It does not prove the sectors are meaningless; it stops claiming they mean anything. A future semantic layer could be validated the same way the spectral coordinates are gated (margin-invariance analysis, a non-degenerate matched null, selection-aware controls, and a pre-registered task test, per the audit record). Until that happens, anonymous numbers stay anonymous.
