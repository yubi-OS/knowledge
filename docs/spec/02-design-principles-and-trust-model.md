# Design principles and trust model

**Scope line:** the 6 design principles of section 2 and the two complementary roots of trust of section 3.1 to 3.2: a YubiKey 5 answering "is the authorized human present?" and an fTPM answering "is this exact firmware + OS stack what we signed?".

Grounding spine: `yubi-OS/yubiOS docs/SPEC.md` (source doc). External mechanisms cited by the source doc carry their own URL and jev weight below.

## The 6 principles

The source doc states 6 design principles, each anchored to an ADR (source doc):

1. **Owner-held trust anchors.** Every key gating the machine MUST be generated by and held by the owner, either on the YubiKey or as a printed offline recovery key. No OEM, vendor, or distribution key may be a mandatory trust anchor (ADR-001).
2. **Verify before execute.** Every component from bootloader to userspace MUST be signed, measured, or hash-verified before it runs or is read (ADR-002/007/013).
3. **Immutability.** The OS ships as a read-only, dm-verity-protected image; the running system MUST NOT be able to modify /usr (ADR-007).
4. **Update-survivability.** OS updates MUST NOT invalidate disk-unlock enrollments. The spec explains that this is exactly why LUKS2 binds to FIDO2 hmac-secret rather than TPM PCR hashes (ADR-011).
5. **Physical presence.** Unlocking secrets MUST require YubiKey touch and PIN where supported; no silent decryption (ADR-003).
6. **Auditable supply chain.** Every build input MUST be digest-pinned per PINNED.md and pass the OPA/Rego build policy yubiOS.rego before any layer executes; published images MUST carry SLSA provenance and SBOM attestations (ADR-014/015).

The principles interlock: principle 4 exists because principle 2's measurement-based binding (PCR hashes) would break on every update, so the identity binding moved from measurement to physical possession. Principle 5 makes possession a human-in-the-loop event, not just a hardware property.

## Two roots of trust, two questions

Section 3.1 tabulates the roots of trust along the question each one answers (source doc):

| Root | Hardware | Question answered | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| YubiKey 5 (primary) | External token, owner-held | "Is the authorized human present?" | Secure Boot signing, LUKS2 unlock, homed, SSH, PAM, TOTP |
| TPM / fTPM | On-device | "Is this exact firmware + OS stack what we signed?" | PCR measurement, attestation, ConditionSecurity=measured-os gating |

The spec's strongest structural statement follows the table: the two roots are "complementary, never interchangeable", and the fTPM MUST NOT become a sole disk-unlock gate (ADR-018) (source doc). The fTPM is optional on x86-64 and is a yubiOS-owned fTPM on ARM64 post-launch (source doc). The reasoning is visible in the table: the fTPM answers a software-integrity question and cannot answer the human-presence question, so making it the sole disk gate would collapse two distinct trust questions into one device.

## The YubiKey interface map

Section 3.2 maps 1 token to 5 trust boundaries (source doc):

1. **PIV slot 9c (CCID)** for Secure Boot / UKI signing via `systemd-sbsign --key pkcs11:...`, requiring `pcscd` (ADR-002/008).
2. **FIDO2 hmac-secret (hidraw)** for LUKS2 root plus swap unlock via `systemd-cryptenroll --fido2-device=auto --fido2-with-client-pin=yes` (ADR-003).
3. **FIDO2 hmac-secret (hidraw)** for per-user LUKS2 homes via `homectl create --fido2-device=auto` (ADR-009).
4. **FIDO2 ed25519-sk resident keys (hidraw)** for SSH with `-O resident -O verify-required` (ADR-004).
5. **FIDO2 U2F (hidraw)** for sudo and login via `pam_u2f.so` with `auth required`, on pam-u2f at least 1.3.1 (ADR-005).

Three mechanisms cover these 5 boundaries: PIV for signing, FIDO2 hmac-secret for secret derivation into disk unlock, and FIDO2/U2F protocols for authentication. On the PIV side, Yubico documents that a PIV-enabled YubiKey 4 and 5 hold 24 certificate slots and that each slot serves distinct purposes (https://developers.yubico.com/PIV/Introduction/Certificate_slots.html, jev weight 0.59; https://docs.yubico.com/yesdk/users-manual/application-piv/slots.html, jev weight 0.59). Slot 9c is the PIV digital-signature key slot, and the PIV attestation key in slot F9 can produce an X.509 attestation statement for keys in slots including 9a, 9c, and 9d (https://docs.yubico.com/yesdk/users-manual/application-piv/slots.html, jev weight 0.59), which is the property that lets the owner export attestation evidence without exporting the signing key.

On the hmac-secret side, the systemd-cryptenroll documentation states it enrolls FIDO2 tokens implementing the hmac-secret extension, "most FIDO2 keys, including YubiKeys", into a LUKS2 volume (https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/253/systemd-cryptenroll.html, jev weight 0.12, weak). Poettering's systemd 248 writeup describes the same FIDO2 hmac-secret unlock path for LUKS2 volumes (https://0pointer.net/blog/unlocking-luks2-volumes-with-tpm2-fido2-pkcs11-security-hardware-on-systemd-248.html, jev weight 0.15, weak).

On the signing side, the yubiOS project's own validation record documents signing with systemd-sbsign against YubiKey PIV slot 9c through PKCS#11 and verifying the signed UKI with osslsigncode (https://github.com/yubi-OS/yubiOS/blob/main/refs/sbsign-pkcs11-validate-2026-07-23.md, jev weight 0.51).

## What the model excludes

The trust model makes the fTPM an attestation device, not an access device. It measures boot stages, holds PCRs, and gates services through ConditionSecurity=measured-os, but the disk keys themselves derive from a token the owner can touch, see, and carry. The 5-interface map is the concrete expression of principle 1: every boundary, from boot signing to sudo, resolves to a key that lives in one piece of owner-held hardware.
