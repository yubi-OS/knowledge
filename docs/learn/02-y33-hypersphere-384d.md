## The notation

The notation Y carrying a degree subscript and an order superscript indexes spherical harmonics by degree and order; that is the standard convention ([Wikipedia, Spherical harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonics), weight 0.22, weak). Read that way, Y_3^3 is the degree 3, order 3 spherical harmonic. The source doc never states this reading; the corpus records it as the conventional interpretation under weak external backing, not as a doc claim.

Spherical harmonics are basis functions for irreducible representations of SO(3), the 3D rotation group, and play a central role in group theoretic descriptions of systems ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonics), weight 0.22, weak). In machine learning they serve as expansion bases: atomic environment descriptors are obtained by expansion in multipolar spherical harmonic bases ([arXiv 2503.09618](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09618v2), weight 0.21, weak; [APS version](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/g49f-b1x5), weight 0.09, weak), and a real basis of hyperspherical harmonics underpins linear time kernel matrix approximation ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358458540_Linear_Time_Kernel_Matrix_Approximation_via_Hyperspherical_Harmonics), weight 0.13, weak; [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05110), weight 0.26, weak).
